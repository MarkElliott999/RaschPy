# Changelog

All notable changes to RaschPy are documented in this file.

## [2.0.0] - 2026-11-24

### Added
- **`per_rater_model_selection(delta_measure=, delta_max=)`** (`'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'`, `'cml'`): how a departure is judged where a step adds a full set of item (or threshold) effects. `'overall'`: the RMS departure against `delta`, which accumulates a departure spread over many items or thresholds but dilutes one at a single item or threshold (a shift of s logits at one of K thresholds has RMS departure s·√(K − 1)/K). `'largest'`: the largest single deviation against `delta_max`: each item's (threshold's) effect against the others', divided by its standard error from the joint fit's sandwich covariance, purified (the most deviant flagged and left out of the reference, the rest re-examined, at Holm's levels). `'either'` (default with `test='LR'`): the larger representation is kept if either test rejects, each at `alpha`, and the step's p is the smaller of the two. A departure within both tolerances cannot be near both at once (it cannot be both spread over many items and concentrated on one), so testing both at `alpha` adds few false rejections: in simulations (12 x 5 and 20 x 5 instruments, 1,500 persons each marked by 2 of 8 raters, 150 datasets each; raters with 1, 3 or 5 items shifted, up to the tolerances), the share of raters wrongly given separate item effects rose by at most 0.013 over the RMS test alone, and the largest-deviation test's own share was at most 0.027. The table adds `p overall`, `p largest`, `Max deviation` and `Flagged` (the items or thresholds flagged) for those steps.
- **`excluded_models=` for `MFRM.model_selection()` and `per_rater_model_selection()`:** the representations to leave out, as an alternative to listing the rest in `candidate_models=` (e.g. `excluded_models=['matrix']`). Passing both is an error unless they give the same set.
- **Per-item close-fit test in `RSM` / `PCM` `model_selection(test='LR', delta=...)`:** new `delta_measure` (`'either'` default, `'overall'`, `'largest'`). Besides the existing test on the departure from RSM over all items (`'overall'`), each item's departure (its PCM thresholds minus RSM's, centred on the item's own mean, as an information-weighted root mean square) is tested against `delta`, Bonferroni over items; with `'either'`, PCM is preferred if either rejects. The overall measure averages a single deviant item away: in simulations (`'cp-full-rcl'`; 10 or 20 items, maximum score 3 or 5; 300 / 1,000 / 5,000 persons; `delta=0.1`), one item departing by 0.5 widths was found in 0.20 / 0.44 / 0.62 of datasets by `'overall'` and 0.74 / 1.00 / 1.00 by `'either'`, which chose PCM for at most 0.01 of RSM datasets. A test on single category widths was also tried and was less powerful (single widths are estimated less precisely). New attributes `model_comparison_rsm_pcm_close_fit_p_item` and `model_comparison_rsm_pcm_item_close_fit` (each item's departure and p). The docstrings and manual now document `delta`, `delta_units`, `sampling`, `power_threshold` and `n_subsamples` (`sampling` was wrongly listed as deprecated).
- **Default tolerances for `per_rater_model_selection()`** (`'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'`, `'cml'`; every test): `delta=0.1` and `delta_max=0.2` median category widths, `stretch_delta=0.1` and `delta_measure='either'` (previously 0, 0, 0 and `'overall'`: the plain likelihood ratio tests). With `test='AIC'` or `'BIC'` they apply at each step of the ladder (see the next entry); with `'pair-cpat'` they stay off. Where the instrument has no usable category width (maximum score 1, or disordered thresholds), the default `delta` and `delta_max` fall back to 0 with a warning (a `delta` or `delta_max` the user sets still raises there; use `delta_units='logits'`). The docstring recommends keeping `delta_max` at about twice `delta`: one of m items shifted by s gives an RMS departure of about s/√m, so the RMS test alone already counts a single shift beyond about `delta`·√m as beyond tolerance, and the largest-deviation test adds sensitivity only below that (at 2:1, from m = 4). In simulations (1,500 persons each marked by 2 of 8 raters; 6 instruments from 6 x 3 to 20 x 5; 12 datasets each), raters with no departure were given a full set of item or threshold effects at a rate of 0.009, and the choice between `delta_max` 0.2 and 0.25 changed 0.8% of the selections. `model_selection(sampling='power')`'s default `delta` (`RSM`, `PCM`) is the same 0.1 widths. For AIC and BIC, see the next entry.
- **`per_rater_model_selection(test='AIC')` and `'BIC'` build each facet element's representation up from `'global'`** (`'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'`, `'cml'`), on the same ladder as `test='LR'`: a step keeps the larger representation only if the criterion prefers it (AIC with `aic_relative_likelihood`: the smaller's relative likelihood at most `alpha`) and, with tolerances, the close-fit test rejects as with `test='LR'`. The table's `Kept` columns give each step's decision. Previously the lowest criterion was taken over all representations, and `delta` and `stretch_delta` raised an error with AIC and BIC. A rule taking the lowest criterion and then stepping down while the estimated departure is within the tolerances was also tried. In simulations (1,500 persons each marked by 2 of 8 raters, 3 of them anchors; 6 instruments from 6 x 3 to 20 x 5; 216 datasets; each other rater with one item or threshold shifted by 0–0.5 widths, and small departures everywhere; default tolerances), raters with no shift were given a representation beyond `'global'` at 0.08 with AIC and 0.07 with BIC (0.29 and 0.26 with the step-down rule), and raters shifted by 0.35–0.5 widths at 0.89 and 0.86 (0.98 and 0.90). `'pair-cpat'` keeps the lowest criterion.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()` with AIC or BIC builds one form for every facet element up from `'global'`, with a tolerance: `delta=None`, `delta_units='width'`, `stretch_delta=None`** (`'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'`, `'cml'`). The ladder is `per_rater_model_selection()`'s: a step keeps the larger form only if the criterion prefers it (AIC with `aic_relative_likelihood`: the smaller's relative likelihood at most `alpha`, from the composite test's tail probability) and the close-fit test rejects the null hypothesis that the departure is at most the tolerance. The departure is the information-weighted root mean square, over every facet element × item × threshold, of the larger form's fitted locations minus the smaller's, each cell centred over facet elements (for steps adding stretches, the RMS change in the stretch factors). Defaults: `delta` 0.2 median category widths and `stretch_delta` 0.2, higher than `per_rater_model_selection()`'s since the choice is the form that best describes the facet elements as a group and some natural differences between them are expected. `'mixed'`, if a candidate, is compared with the ladder's choice by the criterion alone. With `test='LR'` the pairwise table adds `Departure` and `p-value (close fit)`, which then decides `Preferred`. With `'cml'` the statistic is the conditional likelihood ratio, whose reference at the tolerance is a noncentral chi-square (the local approximation, as for the composite fits, with weights of 1). In simulations (6 raters, each person marked by 2; 6 x 3 and 12 x 5 instruments, thresholds 1 logit apart; N = 600 and 2000; 100 datasets per setting; `'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cml'` on each dataset), data with global effects only stayed on `'global'` in every dataset with both; item effects shared by every rater moved the set off `'global'` in 17–66% of datasets with `'cp-full-rcl'` (21–74% with `'cml'`) at an RMS of 0.2 widths and 96–100% (97–100%) at 0.25; shared threshold effects in 6–18% (5–14%) at 0.2 and 69–100% (73–100%) at 0.25; one rater of six with item effects of RMS 0.3 in none, and of 0.5 in 1–9% (2–9%). The two estimations chose the same form in 80–100% of datasets per setting. At a departure equal to the tolerance the test rejects more often than `alpha` (5–74% at 0.2), so the tolerance works as a size threshold rather than a test at level `alpha`. With `'pair-cpat'`, or `'cml'` with `unused_categories='null'`, there is no close-fit test, and passing `delta` raises. New attributes `model_comparison_mfrm_aic_steps` / `_bic_steps` (each step: criterion difference, relative likelihood and its tail probability, `Departure`, `p-value (close fit)`, `Kept`); `model_comparison_mfrm_info` records the tolerances. Removed attributes, which described the previous rule: `model_comparison_mfrm_aic_baseline`, `_aic_gate`, `_aic_relative_likelihood`, `_aic_p` and `_aic_level`.
- **Progress line for the slow methods: `progress=`** (all four classes): CML fits, bootstraps, Monte Carlo p-values and model-selection fits show one line, updated in place (done / total, elapsed, estimated time left). `None` (default) shows it when the run is expected to take more than 10 seconds, `True` always, `False` never; a run expected to take more than 10 minutes gives one `UserWarning`.
- **`MFRM.calibrate(model='mixed', rater_models=..., anchors=...)` is a joint fit** with each facet element (e.g. rater) on its own representation, sharing the item locations and thresholds (`estimation='cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` or `'cml'`; with `'cpat-rcl'` each facet element pair's smoothing constant is the larger of the two representations'; with `'pair-cpat'` each facet element's effects are derived from the `'matrix'` fit, as before). The fit is in the frame of the anchors (none: the average facet element): their global effects average zero, and in each part (item effects, threshold effects, interactions) the anchors whose representation lacks that part (e.g. those on `'global'`) fix the shared item locations and thresholds, so an anchor with that part is free in it (where every anchor has the part, the anchors' average deviation is zero there). Each facet element's representation is read relative to the anchors, so the same data can fit differently in different frames. Each facet element's own parameters are stored in its representation's layout (`facet_effects_mixed_{model}`, `rater_params_mixed`) as well as in the matrix layout (`facet_effects_mixed`). With every facet element on one representation it reproduces that representation's fit (in simulation, to 2.4e-7 logits; `'cpat-rcl'`: 3.6e-8). `std_errors(model='mixed')` gives sandwich standard errors for every estimate, native and matrix layout (in simulation, 200 replicates of all eight representations at N 1000: median ratio of the SE to the sampling SD 0.93–1.13 by kind of parameter, 95% coverage 0.92–0.99). Without `rater_models`, `calibrate(model='mixed')` first selects each facet element's representation (`selection=` passes `per_rater_model_selection()`'s arguments).
- **`per_rater_model_selection(test='LR', delta=, delta_units=)` with `'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` and `'cml'`:** a likelihood ratio ladder, previously `'pair-cpat'` only, on joint `'mixed'` fits. It builds each facet element's representation up from `'global'`. Each side (items, thresholds) is at one of three levels: nothing, a stretch (`'pseudo_halo'`'s item stretch, `'centrality'`'s threshold stretch) or a full set of effects (`'items'`, `'thresholds'`). (1) Each side is set with the other at nothing: above nothing only if its stretch or its full set beats `'global'`, then the full set if that beats the stretch, otherwise the stretch. (2) If both sides stay at nothing, `'bistretch'` is tested against `'global'`; if it wins and neither stretch is needed given the other, the facet element takes `'bistretch'`. (3) Each side is re-examined with the other at its current level until neither changes. A full set on one side beside a stretch on the other is fitted for these tests only, and is reported, fitted and used from then on as `'bivector'`. (4) `'matrix'` is tested against `'bivector'`. An anchor that alone among the anchors lacks a part (item, threshold or interaction) defines that part's reference, so its test of that part is empty; it is tested against the anchors' average instead, in every pass, and the table's `Note` says so; with `test='AIC'` or `'BIC'` (which climb the same ladder) the criterion and the tolerance test of that part are both against the anchors' average. Testing such an anchor this way only once the other choices settled let the passes cycle (each anchor left alone without a part fell back to the smaller model), so they did not settle and the test never ran: in simulations (6 items, maximum score 3, 8 raters, 600 or 1,500 persons each marked by 2; 3 anchors, 25 datasets per setting) with every anchor's item effects different, 80–84% of datasets hit `max_passes`, against none now; the other raters given their generating representation rose from 0.47–0.52 to 0.64–0.74, and a rater with a global effect only was given a more detailed representation in 4–12% of datasets instead of 56–76%. With anchors that agree the choices were the same; with one anchor's item effects different from the other two's, they differed in one dataset of 50 under the likelihood ratio test (600 persons), where the two agreeing anchors were given a stretch and 'matrix'. In simulations (6 items, maximum score 3, 8 raters, 1,500 persons each marked by 2 raters; 11 generating representations, 25 or 50 replicates each), the share of raters given their generating representation, averaged over the 11, was 0.865 (top-down from `'matrix'`: 0.860) with effects of RMS 0.4 logits or stretch factors of 1.3, and 0.616 (0.582) at half that size; the lowest over the 11 was 0.72 (0.52) at full size. With `'pair-cpat'` the ladder stays top-down, since its restricted representations are derived from the matrix fit. Each rung's null is a weighted sum of chi-square(1) variables (Kent, 1982) for the composite likelihoods and chi-square for CML. `delta` is a close-fit tolerance: a rung keeps the larger representation only if the facet element's departure, the root mean square over its item x threshold cells of the difference between its effects under the two, is significantly larger than `delta`. `delta_units='width'` states it as a share of the instrument's median category width, so one value means the same on wide- and narrow-category instruments. The table gives each rung's p and its bias-corrected departure estimate (`Delta-hat`). With large samples the plain tests keep departures too small to matter (on 846 persons x 15 raters every rater went to `'matrix'`). `stretch_delta` is the tolerance of the tests that add a stretch: the departure there is the change in the stretch factor (0.1: the facet element stretches or shrinks its item locations or thresholds by up to 10%); a dict `{'items': x, 'thresholds': y}` sets the two sides separately, since the same proportion can mean a different size of effect on each.
- **Anchors are selected like the other facet elements** in `per_rater_model_selection()` (and `calibrate(model='mixed')` without `rater_models`) with `estimation='cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` or `'cml'`, starting on `'global'`, so an anchor whose departure is patterned is fitted on the representation that captures it instead of showing it as misfit. A sole anchor stays on `'global'`; with several, a part that every anchor takes is fixed by the anchors' average (as in 1.4.0), so the result does not depend on the order the anchors are visited; anchors with cells cut are not selected (see the anchor sets entry). `force_global_anchors=True` holds the anchors on `'global'`, except anchors that the anchor set marks to anchor through their global effects only. In simulation (5 anchors, two of them generated on items and thresholds, N 800), the selection recovers all five anchors' generating representations with each estimation. Previously an anchor given a representation with a part no other anchor had (e.g. `rater_models` putting one anchor on `'matrix'`) had that part held at zero by the anchors' average, fitting as `'global'` without a warning.
- **`'mixed'` downstream:** person estimates, fit statistics (person-based and conditional), the statistics tables, `icc`, the Wright map (each facet element's global effect), `global_fit` (the composite Andersen test), `dif_test`, `drf_test` (see below), bootstrap standard errors, `recovery_stats` (one comparison per representation) and `model_selection` (`'mixed'` as a candidate once fitted) work on a joint `'mixed'` fit; with every facet element on one representation each gives that representation's results.
- **`_mixed` per-model aliases on MFRM:** `calibrate_mixed()`, `std_errors_mixed()`, `person_estimates_mixed()`, `rater_stats_df_mixed()`, `icc_mixed()` and the rest of the per-model alias families (44 in all; not `anchor_std_errors`, as a `'mixed'` fit's frame is fixed when it is fitted), each forwarding to its method with `model='mixed'`.
- **`MFRM.model_selection(estimation=...)`:** the estimation every candidate is compared under. Candidates not yet calibrated, or calibrated with another estimation, are calibrated with it (`'mixed'` with its `rater_models` and anchors), replacing the earlier fit. `None` (default) uses the estimation of the latest calibration, and a `UserWarning` names any candidate recalibrated. Previously candidates calibrated with different estimations raised a `ValueError`.
- **`MFRM.model_selection(sampling=n or 'dynamic', n_subsamples=1)`** (`'LR'`, AIC and BIC; every estimation): every candidate is refitted on n persons drawn at random (each with all their ratings, with the candidates' calibration settings), and the comparison is made on each of `n_subsamples` subsamples. With `'LR'` a pair's larger representation is preferred if more than half the subsamples reject the smaller; with AIC or BIC the representation chosen most often is preferred (ties go to the one with fewer free parameters); `min_effect` is then applied on all the data. `'dynamic'` keeps v1.4.0's size rule, n = min(20R(I − 1)(K − 1), 1500) (K − 1 taken as 1 for dichotomous data); with n at least the number of persons the comparison uses all the data. The default is `sampling=None` (all the data); `'auto'`, v1.4.0's default, is accepted and means `None`. Not combined with the close-fit tolerances (`delta`, `stretch_delta`), whose defaults are then 0. `model_comparison_mfrm_sampling` records n, the seed (drawn and recorded if not given, so the draws can be repeated) and each subsample's choice or p-values; subsamples whose fits fail are left out, counted and named in one warning. The AIC (BIC) summary gains `Subsample share` and the LR summary `Share rejecting`. In simulations (6 raters, each person marked by 2; 6 items x 3 thresholds; N = 2,000; n = 300 and 600, one subsample; 200 datasets per setting), on data with global effects only, the LR tests rejected at 0.035–0.062 per pair on average (at most 0.075 for any one pair; `'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cml'` and `'pair-cpat'`), and AIC and BIC left `global` in at most 0.035 of datasets. With item effects of RMS 0.25 median category widths shared by every rater, global v items was rejected in every dataset; AIC chose items in 0.96–0.99 of datasets, BIC in 0.14–0.27 at n = 300 and 0.94–0.97 at n = 600.
- **`se_correction=` for `MFRM.model_selection()`, `per_rater_model_selection()` and `check_anchor_homogeneity()`** (`None`, the default, or `'kauermann-carroll'`): `'kauermann-carroll'` applies the Kauermann-Carroll small-sample correction, as in `std_errors()`, to the tests built on the sandwich (the composite likelihood ratio and close-fit tests with `'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'`; the anchor check's tests, its pattern step and refits through `check.cut()`). It may help with very small sample sizes, where its time cost is also small. With many persons and parameters it dominates the run time, since it takes an eigendecomposition per person the size of the free parameters: in the Mixed MFRM example notebook (10 raters, 12 items, maximum score 4, 400 persons, each marked by every rater), `per_rater_model_selection(anchors=...)` took 147 seconds with it and 27 without. In simulations of `per_rater_model_selection()` (default `test='AIC'`; 4 or 8 raters, 8 items, maximum score 3; 50, 100 or 200 persons; fully crossed or 50% missing; all raters on `global` or a mix of representations; 20 datasets per setting, 480 in all), the choices with and without it agreed for 99.1% of facet elements, and each chose the generating representation for 78% of them.
- **`calibrate(model='mixed')` accepts data with an unused score category** (previously a `ValueError`), and treats it as a single representation's fit does. With `'cp-full-rcl'` or `'cpat-rcl'`, the category widths next to an unused middle category are set, as for a single representation, by RSM's CPAT threshold estimator (`threshold_method='cpat-cl'`) on all facet elements' responses together, which smooths them heavily because no response pattern informs them, and the response patterns involving those widths are left out of the fit; those widths, and the thresholds next to them, get NaN standard errors. With `'cml'`, the penalty on the widths next to the unused category holds them (`unused_categories='ridge'`, the default), or `unused_categories='null'` (Wilson and Masters, 1993; needs `rater_models=`) fits the model with that category's probability zero, and the thresholds and effects are reported as for a single representation's `'null'` fit. An unused bottom or top category, and a category that one facet element never used, are held by the existing ridges; `std_errors(model='mixed', no_data_se=...)` treats the facet element effects next to such a category as it does for a single representation, and `no_data_parameters['mixed']` lists them. Check: with every facet element on the same representation, the mixed fit gives the single fit's item locations and thresholds within 1e-5 logits (8 representations, 3 estimations, the bottom, a middle or the top category unused), and the same sandwich SEs. Under `'null'` (8 representations, each with one middle category unused, two adjacent categories unused, two non-adjacent ones, the top one and the bottom one), each facet element's thresholds on each item and their SEs agree within 1e-6 logits, except `'bivector'` and `'matrix'` with the bottom or top category unused, which agree within 0.001 logits: there the ridge penalty also falls on combinations of parameters that leave the conditional likelihood unchanged, and the single and mixed fits parameterise those combinations differently. `drf_test`: the thresholds next to a category that no facet element used in either group are left out of every facet element's test, and the threshold test uses the other thresholds (previously, with the default `min_category_count`, such facet elements were not tested at all, global effect included). In simulation (5 raters on `'items'`, `'thresholds'`, `'bivector'` and `'global'`, 6 items, maximum score 4, responses drawn with category 2's probability set to zero, 100 datasets at each of 150 and 400 persons), CML recovered the `'items'` and `'bivector'` raters' item effects as well as from data using every category (median RMSE 0.142 / 0.138 against 0.145 / 0.151 at 150 persons); under `'cp-full-rcl'` the RMSE about doubled (0.283 / 0.359 against 0.147 / 0.160), as it does for a single representation, because every width touches category 2 when the maximum score is 4, so only the patterns comparing the same threshold on two items, or for two facet elements, remain.
- **`drf_test` rebuilt (all representations and `'mixed'`):** each group is fitted jointly (`calibrate(model='mixed')`, every facet element on `model`, which reproduces that representation's own fit), and each facet element is tested in its representation's own terms. This replaces the per-element tests (one test per facet element × item or threshold, purified per element, bootstrap SEs), which are removed: they tested shape and global effect together and, for the stretch representations, flagged unchanged facet elements 0.10–0.31 of the time when one facet element's stretch changed (head-to-head simulation, 6 facet elements, groups of 150 and 400). `drf_test` now needs a fit with `estimation='cp-full-rcl'` (the default), `'cpat-rcl'` or `'cml'`. Removed arguments: `selection_method`, `corr_tol`, `sd_ratio_tol`, `welch`, `constant`, `method`, `matrix_power`, `robust`, `log_lik_tol`, `warm_corr`, `tolerance`, `max_iters`, `ext_score_adjustment`; removed attribute: `drf_tc`. By default (`p_method='monte-carlo'`) the p-values are Monte Carlo ones (`no_of_samples`, default 500; `seed`): both groups' effects are drawn from their sandwich covariances about a version of the data with no DRF for the tested facet element and its pool, and the pool's trimming is repeated on each draw, so that choosing the pool from the same data is part of the reference distribution (with the pool held fixed, the trimming added about 0.01 to the no-DRF rates), and each draw's statistic is multiplied by nu / chi2(nu − df + 1), an independent draw for the noise in estimating the covariance (see the F reference below), so that with the pool held fixed the draws follow that F reference (Hotelling's T² is nu chi2(df) / chi2(nu − df + 1); Anderson, 2003, Section 5.2; the same device as the parametric bootstrap of Krishnamoorthy and Lu, 2010); `p_method='f'` gives the F p-values with the pool held fixed (faster: about 45% less time in a 9-facet-element example). `drf_anchor_selection` now gives each facet element's global effect in both groups and whether it is in the pool trimmed over all facet elements, and `plot=True` draws it. The tests: global effect (`drf_table`, 1 df); item effects (`drf_item_table`, I − 1 df, or 1 df for the change in `omega`/`omega_items` of `pseudo_halo`/`bistretch`); threshold shape (`drf_threshold_table`, K − 1 df, or 1 df for the change in `omega`/`omega_thresholds` of `centrality`/`bistretch`); the interaction (`drf_interaction_table`, `matrix` only, (I − 1)(K − 1) df less the cells left out); and an overall test of all of these (`drf_overall_table`) for facet elements with more than one. The overall and component tests answer different questions and either one can flag a rater without the other doing so: the overall test pools the evidence, so it can flag diffuse DRF spread across components with no component flagging on its own, while a component test focuses on one kind of DRF, so can flag a localised change that the overall test does not flag due to its higher degrees of freedom. An omnibus test (`drf_omnibus_table`, run by default for every representation) tests all facet elements' components together: one Wald statistic T, df = the rank of its covariance, with the multivariate Welch F reference below (columns `T`, `df`, `F`, `Denominator df`, `p`, `Flagged`). With many degrees of freedom the chi-squared reference is liberal: with no DRF on 9 facet elements on 8 representations (37 df), over two simulation studies it rejected 0.103 at alpha = 0.05 with groups of 400 (620 datasets) where F rejected 0.061, and 0.070 with groups of 1000 (500 datasets) where F rejected 0.058; with 6 `global` facet elements (5 df, groups of 400) both 0.0050–0.055. The likelihood-ratio omnibus for `global`, and with it the arguments `warm_corr`, `tolerance`, `max_iters` and `ext_score_adjustment`, are removed (with 6 `global` facet elements it agreed with the Wald omnibus on 95–97% of datasets). Each facet element's effects are summarised as the main effects of an additive fit (level + item effects + threshold effects) and, for `matrix`, the remaining interaction; each test compares its focal-minus-reference difference with the average over the other facet elements (leave-one-out, the pool trimmed at `wald_alpha`), a Wald test T with each group's sandwich covariance, on df = that covariance's rank. As the covariance is estimated from the persons, T (in every table, the trimming and the omnibus) is referred to the multivariate Welch F reference of `global_fit()` (Nel and van der Merwe, 1986): F = T (nu − df + 1) / (df nu) on (df, nu − df + 1) degrees of freedom (new column `Denominator df`), with nu = [tr(V²) + tr(V)²] / sum over the groups of [tr(V_g²) + tr(V_g)²] / (n_g − 1), V_g each group's part of the covariance and n_g its number of persons (with equal covariances and group sizes, nu = persons in both groups − 2, the omnibus's previous Hotelling reference). Reported per item: the item's own difference (level plus item effect); per bounded category 1 to K − 1: the change in its width, as in DCF; each with a Welch t-test (each group's variance counted with its own number of persons), Holm-corrected within the facet element. In simulations with no DRF (6 facet elements, 5 items, maximum score 3, fully crossed; `'global'` and `'thresholds'` with 25–200 persons per group, `'matrix'` with 50–200; 200 datasets per design, 2,200 in all), with chi-squared references and Monte Carlo draws without the covariance noise the rejection rates at alpha = 0.05 were up to 0.065 for the global effect tests, 0.068 for the item and threshold tests and 0.077 for the overall tests (0.096 with the pool held fixed); with the F reference and the draws above, 0.034–0.062 for the global effect tests and 0.039–0.061 for the item, threshold, interaction and overall tests (up to 0.073 with `p_method='f'`); the omnibus, 0.040–0.095 either way (200 datasets per design; its reference changed little, as the groups were of equal size). Against one facet element shifted by 0.3 logits in global effect or by (+0.5, 0, −0.5) in its thresholds (50 and 100 persons per group, 200 datasets per design), power at the same no-DRF rejection rate was unchanged (within ±0.04 in every table); the raw rejection rates of the overall tests fell by up to 0.10, as the facet elements with no DRF were flagged 0.043–0.057 of the time instead of 0.059–0.095. New `min_category_count` (default 5): cells whose categories have fewer ratings than this in either group are left out of a matrix facet element's fit (thresholds/bivector: a threshold rarely used over all items; items/bivector: an item rarely rated), with one `UserWarning`; a facet element whose remaining cells cannot support the fit (too few to estimate its item and threshold effects) has its global effect tested as the mean of its effects over the remaining cells, and its other tests are not run; it stays out of the other facet elements' pools and the omnibus test (simulation with no DRF, 5 facet elements, 5 items, maximum score 3, 300 persons per group, 400 datasets, a `'matrix'` facet element giving 1 on every item but one: its global-effect test gave p ≤ 0.05 in 0.042 of datasets with `p_method='f'` and 0.038 with the default Monte Carlo p-values). A facet element that gave fewer than `min_category_count` ratings in a score category in either group is still tested for global effect (and, where its representation has them, item effects), on its remaining ratings; its threshold test is not run, and its overall test leaves the thresholds out, with a note in the `Note` columns and one `UserWarning` (previously such a facet element was not tested at all). In simulation with no DRF (6 facet elements, 5 items, maximum score 3, 500 datasets at each of 300 and 800 persons per group, one `'thresholds'` facet element whose top category was rare enough to fall below 5 ratings in a group in 84 and 87 of the datasets), that facet element's global-effect test gave p ≤ 0.05 in 0.036 and 0.034 of those datasets, and the other facet elements' in 0.055 and 0.046. In simulation (9 facet elements on 8 representations, 5–10 items, K 3–5, groups of 400 and 1000, with a matrix facet element whose least-used category had about 1–13 ratings per 1000 persons; about 15,000 datasets), a matrix facet element's rate of p ≤ 0.05 with no DRF was up to 0.250 without the guard (10 items, groups of 400, item test) and mostly 0.015–0.070 with it (1000 datasets at the most lenient setting: global effect 0.064, item 0.052, threshold 0.058), the other facet elements 0.038–0.062, and power for 0.4–0.5-logit shifts was kept except where a category was rarely used on most items (global effect 0.75 → 0.50 at groups of 1000). With 25 persons per group, most matrix facet elements could not be tested.
- **`MFRM.per_rater_model_selection()` compares fitted candidates** (`estimation='cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` or `'cml'`): each candidate representation of a facet element is a joint `'mixed'` fit, in the anchors' frame, with the others held at the scheme's representations (`scheme='iterate'`, the default: update one facet element at a time until nothing changes; `'one-pass'`: the others on the most general candidate), compared by AIC (with the significance test against the simplest candidate) or BIC. The frame used is recorded in `per_rater_model_selection_info`. Previously each facet element's simpler representations were derived from one `'matrix'` fit (still the method with `'pair-cpat'`).
- **Mixed-representation MFRM simulation: `MFRM_Sim_Mixed(no_of_items, no_of_persons, max_score, no_of_facet_elements, rater_models=...)`**, or `MFRM_Sim(model='mixed', ...)`, each facet element (e.g. rater) on its own representation (any of the eight, including the stretch models). `rater_models` takes a list (one representation per facet element, values drawn at random), a dict `{facet_element: model}` or `{facet_element: (model, params)}` (parameters in each representation's own form; facet elements left out drawn at random), or None (every representation drawn, from `candidate_models=`, weighted by `proportions=`). Each facet element's random values are drawn on their own: global effect across all facet elements as for `model='global'` (`facet_range`), item and threshold patterns per facet element (`item_facet_range`, `threshold_facet_range`), matrix interactions (`interaction_range`) and stretch multipliers between `stretch_range=(lowest, highest)` (any real numbers; `item_stretch_range=` / `threshold_stretch_range=` for one axis). The data come from each facet element's parameters relative to the shared item locations and thresholds (`rater_params`, `native_items`, `native_thresholds`, `native_persons`, one `facet_effects_mixed_{model}` table per representation); `facet_effects` gives all of them in the matrix layout. `MFRM_Sim_Mixed`, like the eight single-representation classes, takes `max_score` before `no_of_facet_elements`, and calls the spread of the global effects `global_range` (as `MFRM_Sim_Centrality`, `MFRM_Sim_PseudoHalo` and `MFRM_Sim_Bistretch` do; `facet_range` in `MFRM_Sim`).
- **`anchors=` and `neutral_anchors=` for the MFRM simulation classes.** `anchors` (a list of facet elements) reports the true values in the frame `calibrate_anchor()` gives the estimates, so they compare directly with anchored estimates; the data are unchanged. With `neutral_anchors=True` the anchor facet elements are generated with no effect (unless their values are passed) and the others without centring, so the anchored frame is the generating frame; it does nothing without `anchors`. The stretch simulations also take `stretch_range=(lowest, highest)`, and `MFRM_Sim_Bistretch` `item_stretch_range=` / `threshold_stretch_range=`; a single number keeps its meaning, so seeded results are unchanged.
- **Conditional maximum likelihood (CML) for `SLM`, `PCM` and `RSM`: `calibrate(estimation='cml')`** (the default is `'cp-full-rcl'` for all four classes; see Changed). Item locations (`SLM`), uncentred thresholds (`PCM`), or item locations and shared thresholds (`RSM`) maximise the conditional likelihood given each person's total score over the items they responded to, starting from the default estimates, with the same centring; by default the chord method (`solver=None` or `'chord'`: one exact Hessian at the start, then Newton steps with it held fixed and the exact gradient; `'newton'` and `'lbfgs'` are alternatives, and all reach the same estimates). In timing tests from 30 to 1,000 items, complete and missing data, chord was the fastest or within about 10% of the fastest: 1.3–1.7× faster than Newton-Raphson, and 1.35–1.9× faster than L-BFGS on 1,000-item polytomous banks. CML bootstrap resamples reuse the observed Hessian as their fixed matrix (2.2–9.2× faster per resample in those tests, standard errors unchanged). Everything in the conditional likelihood that depends only on the data (the persons who contribute, observed category counts, missingness groups and their totals, batches) is computed once per fit rather than at every step (`_esf.prepare`; 1.0–1.6× faster per fit, identical results). On the benchmark datasets RaschPy's CML (estimates and standard errors) was 9–22× faster than `psychotools` on data with 20% missing responses and up to 31× faster on complete data, and slower only on the smallest `SLM` designs (10 items, or 30 items with 500 persons: 7–25 ms per fit, 0.54–0.98× `psychotools`' speed). The conditional likelihood and its exact gradient and Hessian are computed in log space with no subtractions, for any pattern of missing responses (persons grouped by the items they responded to). `std_errors()` defaults to `se_method='sandwich'` for CML estimates (the sandwich of the conditional likelihood; see below), with `'hessian'` (the inverse information matrix) as an option; `'bootstrap'` refits with CML. `self.estimation` and `self.convergence` record the method and the fit. Items whose CML estimates do not exist (an item not compared with the others in both directions, or, for `SLM`/`PCM`, a category unused by the persons with a non-extreme total) are removed from `responses` to `cml_dropped_responses`, with a `UserWarning`, and listed with the reason in `convergence['dropped_items']`; for `RSM`, a category unused on every item raises a `ValueError`. Refits (`std_errors` bootstrap, `dif_test`, `global_fit`) use CML when the object was calibrated with it; `global_fit` then uses the CML covariance of the object's `se_method`. On simulated datasets (complete, missing, booklet), estimates agreed with `psychotools` to within 2e-5 logits, SEs to within 1e-6 and the conditional log-likelihood to 1e-6, the differences being within `psychotools`' optimiser tolerance; on `PCM` and `RSM` booklet designs where `psychotools::pcmodel`/`rsmodel` returned invalid fits (positive log-likelihoods, reported as converged), RaschPy's agreed with brute-force enumeration. For `RSM`, `psychotools`' threshold standard errors were about twice the sampling SD in a replicate simulation for all but the last threshold; RaschPy's matched it.
- **Andersen's conditional likelihood ratio test: `global_fit(method='andersen')`** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; estimates from `calibrate(estimation='cml')`), previously reserved and raising `NotImplementedError`. LR = 2 (sum of the groups' ll − ll_pooled), each the conditional log-likelihood at its own CML maximum, with df = (groups − 1) × the model's free parameters; the pooled model is fitted to the persons of the groups. With `split='exogenous'` there is one group per value of `covariate`, which may have more than two (the Wald test still needs exactly two). `split='score_groups'` splits into two groups of whole score groups, cut where the persons with non-extreme scores divide most evenly, as iarm's `clr_tests` does (the default `'score'` split cuts at the median mean score, so persons with the same score may be separated). On iarm's AMTS data (complete cases) the overall test (`'score_groups'`), and the tests by sex and by the three age groups, reproduce `clr_tests` exactly (24.122, df 9; 18.753, df 9; 18.558, df 18). Items without CML estimates in every group are left out of every fit, with a `UserWarning` (`global_fit_excluded`). With estimates from the default estimation it raises a `ValueError` naming `calibrate(estimation='cml')`. In null simulations (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; I = 10; N = 500 and 2,000; score, random, exogenous and person-location splits; 300 datasets each), the rejection rate at alpha = 0.05 was 0.023–0.070 and the mean LR/df 0.94–1.04; `PCM`'s score split left out 3.8 of 10 items on average at N = 500 (unused top or bottom categories in one group). Power was similar to the Wald test's (e.g. `SLM`, DIF of 0.5 logits on 2 of 10 items, N = 1,000: 0.83 against 0.82). Under CML, the Wald test uses the CML covariance of the object's `se_method` (the sandwich by default, or `'hessian'`), with no small-sample correction (`global_fit_summary` reports it as `covariance`, with `se_correction=None`).
- **Martin-Löf's test of unidimensionality: `global_fit(method='martin-lof')`** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; estimates from `calibrate(estimation='cml')`), for two item subsets (`split='median'`, the default, or a list of the items in the first subset). LR = 2 (ll_A + ll_B − ll_all + Σ n_rs log(n_rs/N) − Σ n_t log(n_t/N)), with the subtotal and total distributions taken within each missingness pattern. On complete data the statistic equals `eRm::MLoef`'s for all three models and the df for `SLM` and `PCM`; for `RSM` the df is K − 1 larger than `eRm`'s, because each subset is fitted with its own category widths (`eRm`'s df formula counts score cells only, so its `RSM` test is liberal: 0.060–0.107 at alpha = 0.05 in null simulations where this df gave 0.033–0.040). The chi-square reference is conservative when the subtotal table is sparse: in null simulations it held its level only when at most about a quarter of the table's cells had fewer than 5 persons, and its rejection rate fell to 0 as that share grew; a `UserWarning` is then raised, naming `p_method='monte-carlo'`. That option simulates `no_of_samples` datasets from the joint CML fit given each person's total and observed items (the conditional distribution does not involve the persons), refits each starting from the observed estimates, and recomputes the statistic, each refit using the observed fit's Hessian as a fixed Newton matrix (the chord method: the same maximum, with gradient evaluations only; 1.0–3.2× faster than Newton steps per sample in timing tests, 2.4× with 20% missing responses); `mc_exceedances=` stops early once that many simulated statistics reach the observed one (Besag and Clifford's sequential p-value). In the sparsest null designs (`PCM` and `RSM`, I = 10, K = 3, N = 500 and 2,000, complete and booklet; 150–300 datasets each, 200 samples), the Monte Carlo rejection rate was 0.027–0.077 at alpha = 0.05, against 0.000–0.013 for the chi-square p-value.
- **Conditional item and person fit: `fit_statistics(conditional=True)`** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`). The expected scores, variances and fourth central moments behind the residuals and the item and person infit/outfit statistics are their conditional values given each person's total over the items they responded to (Christensen & Kreiner), which do not involve the person locations; the output is otherwise the same as before, with ZSTD from the exact conditional moments. Outputs that need person locations (point-measure correlations, discrimination, CSEM/RSEM, separation, reliability) are unchanged. Also: `item_restscore_table` (Kreiner's item-restscore test: Goodman-Kruskal gamma of item score against restscore, observed against expected under the model, with BH-adjusted p-values) and `person_conditional_p` (each person's probability, given their total, of a response pattern no more likely than theirs; Monte Carlo from exact conditional draws, `conditional_samples=`). Works with any estimation method (the theory assumes CML). Conditional infit/outfit mean squares and their SEs agreed with `iarm` to within 2e-5 and 5e-6, and item-restscore with `iarm`'s printed values. In null simulations (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; I = 10 and 20; N = 500 and 2,000, complete and 20% missing; 150 datasets each; CML and default estimation), the rejection rate at alpha = 0.05 was 0.016–0.044 for item infit |ZSTD| > 1.96, 0.029–0.046 for outfit, 0.016–0.041 for item-restscore and 0.043–0.050 for `person_conditional_p`, with mean infit/outfit mean squares 0.994–1.004; the person-based ZSTD on the same data flagged 6–99% of items, more with larger N.
- **Conditional fit for `MFRM`: `fit_statistics(model=..., conditional=True)`**, under every representation (including the stretch models and `mixed`). Each facet element x item is treated as a virtual item whose thresholds are that cell of the model's location grid, and each person is conditioned on their total over all the ratings they received, so the expected scores, variances and fourth moments behind the residuals and the item, facet element and person infit/outfit do not involve the person locations. Threshold fit, defined through the person locations, is unchanged. In null simulations (each of the five simulated representations fitted with itself, and the three stretch models fitted to `global` data; 6 items x 6 facet elements, N = 300, 50 datasets each), item infit |ZSTD| > 1.96 occurred in 0.023–0.047 of items against 0.083–0.093 person-based, with mean item infit 1.001–1.002 against 0.972–0.974; facet element infit was conservative for the representations with many parameters per facet element (0.000–0.010 for `matrix`, `bivector`, `thresholds` and the stretch models). With `conditional=True`, `MFRM` also gives item-restscore tests pooled over facet elements and over items (`item_restscore_table_{model}`, `rater_restscore_table_{model}`), `person_conditional_p_{model}`, Q3\* from `item_res_corr_analysis` (`item_q3_star_{model}`), and, with `bootstrap_samples=`, the parametric bootstrap p-values (marginal and Westfall-Young) and bands for item and facet element infit/outfit, restscore and Q3, and the first residual contrast's bootstrap mean, 95% cutoff and p-value (`residual_contrast_bootstrap_{model}`), each simulated dataset recalibrated with the object's settings. In null simulations (6 items x 6 facet elements, N = 300, 200 samples), the Westfall-Young familywise false-positive rates were 0.055 (item infit), 0.055 (item outfit), 0.070 (facet element infit), 0.055 (facet element outfit) and 0.065 (Q3) (`global`, 200 datasets), and the first contrast's rate at 0.05 was 0.050 (`global`, 300 datasets) and 0.06 (`items`, `matrix`; 100 datasets each); with half the items on a second dimension (correlation 0.6 or 0.8; 30 datasets each) the first contrast was significant in every dataset, though it never exceeded 2 (mean 1.40 and 1.72, against a bootstrap 95% cutoff of 1.13). With one facet element giving random ratings, the others' infit falls below 1 and some are flagged for overfit (with 5%, 10% and 20% random ratings, 0.6, 1.7 and 4.5 of the other 5 on average): recalibrate without the misfitting facet element before interpreting overfit in the rest.
- **Bootstrap reference distributions for conditional item fit: `fit_statistics(conditional=True, bootstrap_samples=...)`** (Johansson, 2025, 2026). The given number of datasets (at least 400 recommended; 1,000–2,000 for a final analysis) are simulated from the fitted model given each person's total over the items they responded to, so the score distribution is the observed one and no person estimates are needed; each is recalibrated with the object's `calibrate()` settings (CML refits reuse the observed Hessian) and its conditional infit and outfit computed. `item_fit_bootstrap` gives each item's observed mean square, a 95% highest-density band, a studentised marginal p-value and a Westfall-Young step-down p-value (familywise error control across items, the recommended decision rule, on infit); `item_fit_bootstrap_info` records the samples used, those skipped (an item without an estimate in a simulated dataset) and the seed. In null simulations (`SLM` I = 10 and 20, `PCM` and `RSM` I = 10; N = 250 and 500; 100 datasets each, 400 samples) the familywise false-positive rate on infit was 0.02–0.08; with one item on a second dimension (correlation 0.7, N = 500) the Westfall-Young rule detected it in 100% (`PCM`, `RSM`) and 82% (`SLM`) of datasets with 0.7–4.2% false positives on the fitting items, against 4.6–15% for the conditional ZSTD. The same simulated datasets give bootstrap p-values (marginal and Westfall-Young) for item-restscore (added to `item_restscore_table`), for Yen's Q3 on every item pair (`q3_bootstrap`, upper one-sided, with the bootstrap mean and 95th percentile as a sample-specific cutoff) and for the first residual contrast (`residual_contrast_bootstrap`, the largest eigenvalue of the standardised conditional residuals' correlations, with its simulation-based 95% cutoff; Chou & Wang, 2010). In null simulations (`SLM` and `PCM`, I = 10, N = 500, 100 datasets, 200 samples) their familywise rates were 0.06–0.07 (restscore), 0.05–0.06 (Q3) and 0.04–0.05 (first contrast); with local dependence on one item pair the Q3 rule flagged that pair in 62–70% of datasets (other pairs 2–6%), and with half the items on a second dimension (correlation 0.6) the first contrast was significant in every dataset.
- **Anchoring with the anchors fixed under CML: `calibrate_anchor(fix_anchors=...)`** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`). With CML estimates (the default, `fix_anchors=None`, resolves to `True` for `estimation='cml'`), the anchor items that keep their supplied values under `overwrite_anchors` are held at those values in a CML fit and every other parameter is estimated given them (for `PCM`, each anchor item's location, the mean of its uncentred thresholds, is fixed and its threshold shape estimated; for `RSM`, the shared thresholds are estimated), so the other items take up the spacing of the supplied anchors, not only their mean. Anchor selection (`selection_method`) runs first, on the CML estimates without anchors. `anchor_items` holds the fitted locations and `anchor_cml` the fit (fixed items, item locations with Hessian SEs, thresholds, log-likelihood, convergence); `fix_anchors=False` gives the translation as before, and `fix_anchors=True` with other estimation methods raises a `ValueError`. When the supplied anchors equal the CML estimates plus a constant, the fit reproduces that shift exactly (to 1e-8). In simulations (I = 20, N = 500, 6 anchors at their generating values; 100 datasets per model), the SD of (estimate − generating value) / SE over the non-anchor items was 0.96–0.99, with RMSE 0.120 (`SLM`), 0.096 (`PCM`) and 0.076 (`RSM`), against 0.120, 0.097 and 0.077 for the translation.
- **`RSM`/`PCM` `model_selection()` tests RSM within PCM with the estimation the object was calibrated with** (see Changed). The other model is fitted with the same estimation, and the test follows it. `'cml'`: the LR test of the two fits' conditional log-likelihoods (unpenalised, with `PCM`'s default ridge), df = (I − 1)(K − 1). `'cp-full-rcl'`: a composite likelihood ratio test on the pairwise conditional likelihood of every item pair's response patterns, (a, b) against (a − 1, b + 1), which both models' estimates maximise; persons appear in many pairs, so the null distribution is a weighted sum of chi-square(1) variables (Kent, 1982) from the likelihood's sensitivity and its variability over persons, and the p-value is that sum's exact tail probability, and AIC and BIC count tr(J H⁻¹) in place of the parameters (Varin and Vidoni, 2005). PAIR with `method='log-lik'`: the same test on PAIR's own Bradley-Terry likelihood of those comparisons, with RSM as that likelihood's maximum under the rating scale restriction, so PAIR objects are not refitted with another estimator. `'cpat-rcl'`: the same composite test on CPAT's patterns only, with `PCM` fitted by its new `'cpat-rcl'` estimator (see below). PAIR with `method='cos'`, `'ls'` or `'evm'`: no likelihood fits these, so the p-value is from a conditional parametric bootstrap (`no_of_samples` datasets, default 199, simulated given each person's total and items under the fitted RSM, PCM refitted to each with the same method; the statistic is the sum of squared item-specific threshold deviations of the PCM fit; `seed=`); only `test='LR'` is available there. `model_comparison_rsm_pcm_info` records the test, the estimation and, for the composite test, the range of its weights. In null simulations (RSM data, alpha = 0.05; 10 or 20 items, maximum score 3 or 4, complete or 20% missing; 4,800 datasets), the composite test rejected RSM in 0.052 / 0.044 / 0.059 of datasets at 200 / 500 / 1,000 persons under `'cp-full-rcl'` (0.035–0.095 by design) and 0.056 / 0.045 / 0.060 under PAIR, against 0.059 / 0.052 / 0.057 for the CML LR test without a ridge, with 98% of that test's power (4,800 PCM datasets, item-specific threshold deviations of SD 0.2 and 0.4); CL-AIC chose PCM in 0.002–0.006 of the RSM datasets (CML's AIC 0.001–0.003) and in 0.118 / 0.650 / 0.961 of the PCM datasets with SD 0.2 (CML's AIC 0.129 / 0.708 / 0.979). With 10 items, 100 or 300 persons, maximum score 3 or 5, and complete, 50% missing or booklet designs (2,400 datasets) it rejected 0.048 (0.010–0.075 by design). The CML test with `PCM`'s default ridge rejected 0.036 / 0.041 / 0.046 at 100 / 300 / 1,000 persons (10 or 20 items, maximum score 3 or 4, complete or 20% missing; 2,374 datasets; 0.010–0.072 by design), against 0.046 / 0.041 / 0.046 without the ridge, with power 0.30 / 0.77 / 0.99 (SD 0.2) against 0.34 / 0.77 / 0.99; referring it instead to the ridge fit's effective degrees of freedom rejected 0.132 at 100 persons. The bootstrap test rejected 0.038–0.052 by method (10 items, maximum score 3, 100 or 300 persons, complete or 20% missing; 400 datasets per method), with power 0.20–0.40 at 100 persons and 0.72–0.92 at 300 (SD 0.3). When a middle score category is rarely used or unused, no version of the test holds its level: in null simulations (10 items, 100 or 300 persons, maximum score 3 or 5), the composite test rejected RSM in 0.100 of datasets with a rarely used middle category and 0.999 with an unused one, a conditional bootstrap of it 0.001 and 0.987, and CML had no estimates; one `UserWarning` names the categories, or the thresholds around them.
- **`dif_test`'s omnibus test under CML is a conditional likelihood ratio** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; estimates from `calibrate(estimation='cml')`): the pooled CML fit to the combined reference and focal data against the same fit plus a focal-group location shift on each common item, threshold shapes kept common (df = common items − 1; `PCM`'s `omnibus_scope='full'` frees all the common items' thresholds, df = their number − 1). It involves no person locations; with every item common, the `SLM` statistic is Andersen's (`global_fit(method='andersen')`). With the person-location omnibus used for the other estimation methods, CML estimates gave rejection rates of 0.057–0.107 at alpha = 0.05 in null simulations (I = 10, N = 300 per group; all, 8 or 6 of 10 items answered by each group; 300 datasets per design and model); the conditional test gave 0.030–0.067 (mean LR/df 0.97–1.10), and 0.043–0.060 for `PCM`'s `'full'` scope. Per-item DIF, DCF and `global_fit` under CML were at nominal in the same simulations, with Hessian (0.034–0.059, 0.038–0.050, 0.030–0.067) and sandwich standard errors (see the sandwich entry).
- **Andersen's test for `'cp-full-rcl'` estimates: `global_fit(method='andersen')`** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; previously CML only, so it now runs with the default estimation). The groups and their pooled persons are refitted with `'cp-full-rcl'` (`PCM`'s groups at the pooled fit's ridge weight), and the composite likelihood ratio is twice the difference of the pairwise conditional log-likelihoods at the group fits and at the pooled fit, unpenalised. `p_method=None` resolves to `'monte-carlo'`: the adjusted composite LR (Pace, Salvan and Sartori, 2011: the LR multiplied by the ratio of the sandwich Wald statistic of the group differences to the Wald statistic from the likelihood's own curvature) against `no_of_samples` datasets (default 500) simulated from the pooled fit given each person's total and items, every fit repeated, stopping once 10 simulated statistics reach the observed one. `p_method='chi2'` refers the unadjusted ratio to a weighted sum of chi-square(1) variables (Kent, 1982; the p-value is that sum's exact tail probability); for `PCM` a `UserWarning` says it is liberal and recommends `p_method='monte-carlo'`. `global_fit_summary` reports both statistics, the Satterthwaite scale and df and the chi-square p-value; `global_fit_obsexp` is set as for CML and `global_fit_item_table` is `None`. `global_stats_df(run_all_tests=True)` now runs it for `'cp-full-rcl'` objects. In null simulations (SLM, and PCM and RSM with maximum score 3 or 4; 10 or 20 items; 200, 500 and 2,000 persons; complete and booklet designs; score, score-group, random and exogenous splits; 1,200 datasets per model and maximum score, each split four ways; alpha = 0.05) the Monte Carlo test rejected 0.047 (SLM), 0.044 / 0.043 (PCM, maximum score 3 / 4) and 0.051 / 0.054 (RSM) of datasets (0.00–0.12 by cell, no more spread than chance), against 0.052, 0.044 / 0.054 and 0.050 / 0.056 for CML's Andersen test; with three exogenous groups (600 and 2,000 persons; 1,000 datasets) 0.050 against 0.054; with sparse top categories (maximum score 4, persons centred 1.5 logits below the items, 300 and 1,000 persons; 300 datasets per model, each split two ways) 0.020–0.040 for PCM and 0.033–0.073 for RSM. The chi-square p-value rejected 0.057, 0.045 / 0.070 and 0.051 / 0.053 (PCM with maximum score 4: 0.082 and 0.086 on the score and score-group splits). With 0.5 logits of DIF on two items (exogenous split; 600 datasets per model and maximum score) the Monte Carlo test's power was below CML's with 200 and 500 persons (PCM 0.10–0.17 lower, RSM 0.02–0.14, SLM 0.04–0.06), the same with 2,000; with 200 and 500 persons it was 0.03–0.24 above the chi-square p-value's for PCM and RSM. - **Partial gamma tests of local dependence: `local_dependence_gamma()`** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; Kreiner & Christensen, 2004). - **CML with unused score categories: `calibrate(estimation='cml', unused_categories='null')`** (`PCM`, `RSM`. `RSM`'s default is `'drop'`, unchanged, which raises a `ValueError`; `PCM`'s is `'ridge'` with its CML ridge on (next entry) and `'drop'`, removing the item, with `ridge=0`). A score category that no person with a non-extreme total used is kept with probability zero and the scores unchanged (Wilson & Masters, 1993; psychotools' `pcmodel(nullcats='keep')`): the data identify only the sum of the two thresholds either side of it, so the item (`RSM`: the model) has one parameter fewer for each such category. In `thresholds_uncentred`/`thresholds` such a category's threshold is `inf` and the next threshold holds that sum; thresholds below the lowest used category are `-inf`, and those above the highest used one `inf`; `cml_unused_categories` lists them. Every method then uses each item's lowest and highest used category for the possible totals (extreme scores, person estimates, score lookup tables, targeting, ability groups), and the category widths and SEs that touch an unused category are NaN. With an unused top or bottom category (and `PCM`'s `ridge=0`) the model is the same as the item with its score range narrowed (scores shifted down for a bottom one): on DESC-II with such categories (`PCM` and `RSM`), every output compared (log-likelihood, item locations and SEs, person estimates, infit/outfit and their conditional versions, item-restscore, Q1, R1c, targeting, reliabilities, score lookup, Andersen, Martin-Löf, partial gamma, fixed-anchor CML) matched the narrowed-range fit within 1e-7. With an unused interior category, the log-likelihood and parameter count match psychotools exactly, and the conditional infit and outfit match a direct computation of each response's conditional distribution given the total (within 1e-14; iarm cannot be compared here, as its fit statistics return NA for items with an unused category).
- **Ridge-penalised CML for `PCM`, on by default: `calibrate(estimation='cml', ridge='minimise_bias')`** (`'minimize_bias'` also accepted; a number sets the weight directly, and `ridge=0` gives plain CML as before). The conditional log-likelihood is maximised minus a penalty on each item's centred thresholds (its threshold pattern), pulling them towards the mean pattern of the items with the same maximum score, plus a weak penalty (weight 0.3) pulling each such group's mean category widths towards the mean width over all groups, which keeps the estimates finite when a category is unused by every item in a group. The fit starts from the PAIR estimates. `'minimise_bias'` sets the weight from the data: 2.418 × S^−0.607 × K^−0.385, S the mean over items of the highest minus the lowest PAIR threshold and K the mean maximum score (clipped to 0.003–4), chosen so that the item locations are neither pulled in nor spread out on average. `self.ridge` and `convergence` report the weight used. With the ridge on, an item with a category that no person with a non-extreme total used is kept and its thresholds estimated (`unused_categories='ridge'`, the default then; `'null'` and `'drop'` remain available, and with `ridge=0` the default stays `'drop'`). The group and subset refits of `global_fit()` and `dif_test()`, and the fixed-anchor fit of `calibrate_anchor()`, use the full-data fit's weight; bootstrap resamples apply the rule afresh. Standard errors include the penalty in the information (`'sandwich'` and `'hessian'`). The weight rule was fitted on 2,000 simulated datasets (200 random designs: 6–30 items, equal or mixed maximum scores 2–10, 100–1,000 persons, seven patterns of category widths including position-dependent, disordered and underused categories) and checked on 3,000 fresh datasets from 150 other random designs: mean over designs of the item-location slope (estimated on true) 0.991 (5th–95th percentile 0.905–1.045), item-location RMSE 0.218, threshold slope 0.981 and RMSE 0.709, person-estimate RMSE 0.106 (Warm estimates from estimated minus true thresholds, same responses); PAIR on the same data gave 0.765 (0.216–1.015), 0.384, 0.834, 1.732 and 0.187. A fixed weight of 0.3 gave 0.989 (0.888–1.041), 0.218, 0.980, 0.714 and 0.107. Lowering the weight spreads item and threshold estimates out and increases their error; raising it, up to about twice the default, lowers the item-location error slightly while pulling the estimates towards the common pattern, and beyond that the error increases. `SLM` and `RSM` CML have no ridge (in simulations it only pulled their item locations in). With the ridge, `global_fit(method='andersen')` defaults to Monte Carlo p-values (`p_method=None`, stopping once 10 simulated statistics reach the observed one, Besag and Clifford's sequential p-value), because each group's fit is pulled towards the common pattern more than the pooled fit (fewer persons, same weight), which shrinks the likelihood ratio: in null simulations (10 items, maximum score 3, 5 or 8, N = 100–500, 100 datasets per design, the score and random splits) the chi-square p-value rejected 0% and 2% of the time at alpha = 0.05 (mean LR/df 0.31–0.78 with the score split), Monte Carlo 3.6% and 3.7% (1–7% per design). With `ridge=0` the default stays chi-square.
- **Full-pattern pairwise conditional likelihood for `PCM`: `calibrate(estimation='cp-full-rcl')`**, the `PCM` counterpart of `RSM`'s and `MFRM`'s estimator of that name. Every item pair and every pair of response patterns with the same pair total, (a, b) against (a − 1, b + 1), is a binomial comparison of two thresholds; the composite log-likelihood of all of them is maximised by Newton-Raphson from the PAIR estimates, with the same ridge as CML (`ridge=`, `unused_categories='ridge'` or `'drop'`; `'null'` is CML only). Its composite likelihood counts each response in many pairs, so its weights are on a larger scale: `'minimise_bias'` is 17.14 × S^−0.808 × K^−0.051 (clipped to 0.03–40) and the group-width weight 3. Standard errors are the sandwich of the composite likelihood. The rule was fitted on the same 2,000 simulated datasets as CML's and checked on the same 3,000: mean over designs of the item-location slope 0.992 (5th–95th percentile 0.900–1.054), item-location RMSE 0.225, threshold slope 0.980 and RMSE 0.739, person-estimate RMSE 0.110, against 0.991 (0.905–1.045), 0.218, 0.981, 0.709 and 0.106 for ridge CML, at a median 0.009 s per fit against 0.060 s. Ridge CML for `PCM` now starts from these estimates (the same maximum; 2.6× less time in total on 60 of those designs), except with `ridge=0` or `unused_categories='null'`, which start from PAIR.
- **Standard errors of parameters the data do not inform: `std_errors(no_data_se='posterior')`** (`PCM` CML and `'cp-full-rcl'` with the ridge, `SLM` CML with `ridge > 0`, `RSM`, and `MFRM`'s `'items'`, `'thresholds'`, `'bivector'` and `'matrix'`; `'nan'` is the alternative; `no_data_parameters` lists them). `RSM`: an empty score category's thresholds and the widths touching them, set by the empty-category rule, and with `'pair-cpat'` a distance with no comparison patterns, set to the mean of the others, have no likelihood behind them, so their SE is NaN under either option; under `'cp-full-rcl'`/`'cpat-rcl'` a distance with no comparison patterns is set by the width ridge and follows the option. `MFRM`: a facet element × item cell with no responses, and a facet element's thresholds next to a score category it never used, are set by the ridge (`'pair-cpat'`: the smoothing, NaN); their sandwich SE was near zero (0.000 for an unrated cell in a test design). In every `MFRM` sub-model, as for `RSM`, a score category no response used makes the shared thresholds either side of it and the widths touching it rule-set (NaN under either option; all thresholds when every width is rule-set): with the sandwich the thresholds away from it had shown SE 0.000, and the bootstrap about 0.06 for those next to it. With the ridge such parameters get estimates, set by the ridge alone: for `PCM`, the thresholds either side of a score category no person with a non-extreme total used and the widths touching them, and the item location when its lowest or highest category is unused (with only interior categories unused the location is identified, as the data fix the sum of the thresholds either side); for `SLM`, an item answered correctly, or incorrectly, by every such person, or not compared with the others in both directions. `no_data_parameters` lists them. Their sandwich or bootstrap SE would show almost no uncertainty, since the ridge fixes them; `'posterior'` gives the SE from the information including the ridge (as `se_method='hessian'`), and `'nan'` gives NaN. One `UserWarning` names them and each option's blind spot: with `'posterior'` tests on them are conservative and their estimates lean towards the common pattern (`SLM`: towards 0), so DIF or disorder in them may be missed; with `'nan'` they are left out of confidence intervals and tests, so DIF in them cannot be found. Every other parameter keeps its usual SE.
- **`SLM`: `calibrate(estimation='cml', ridge=)`** (default 0, plain CML as before): a penalty ridge × the sum of the squared centred item locations / 2. With `ridge > 0` every item has a finite estimate, so none is removed (an item answered correctly, or incorrectly, by every person with a non-extreme total, or not compared with the others in both directions, is kept, its location set by the ridge). In simulations of sparse and extreme designs (10–40 items, N = 30–300, 0–90% missing at random, persons centred on the items or 2 logits above them; 1,920 datasets) no weight improved on PAIR in both item-location slope and RMSE (PAIR 0.734 and 0.537; ridge 0.3: 0.825 and 0.559; ridge 1: 0.647 and 0.502, means over designs), while plain CML kept 81% of the items (63% with 80–90% missing), hence the default.
- **`SLM`: `calibrate(estimation='cp-full-rcl')`** accepted, for the same options as `PCM`, `RSM` and `MFRM`: for dichotomous items the full-pattern pairwise conditional likelihood has one comparison per item pair, which `method='log-lik'` maximises, so it gives PAIR's estimates (with the smoothing constant and no ridge; other `method=` values raise a `ValueError`).
- **Andersen's test with categories unused in a group: `global_fit(method='andersen', unused_categories='null')`** (`PCM`, `RSM`; default `None`, following the object's `calibrate(unused_categories=...)`). An item with a category that no person with a non-extreme total used, in one group or the pooled data, is kept, with that category's probability zero and the scores unchanged (Wilson & Masters, 1993; psychotools' `pcmodel(nullcats='keep')`): the data identify only the sum of the two thresholds either side of it, so that fit has one parameter fewer for each such category, and df = the groups' free parameters minus the pooled fit's. On iarm's DESC-II data the log-likelihoods and parameter counts match psychotools' `pcmodel` and `rsmodel` exactly (score-group split: LR 45.454, df 25, where `'drop'` leaves no items and iarm's `clr_tests` refers the same LR to df 39; the four clinical groups: 162.015, df 112, where `'drop'` leaves out 3 items); for RSM with a category unused by every item, which psychotools does not fit, it matches a direct maximisation of the conditional likelihood (within 1e-9). With sparse categories the chi-square p-value is liberal (a `UserWarning` says so): in null simulations (PCM, 10 items scored 0–4, persons centred 1.5 logits below the items, N = 300 and 1,000, score-group and random splits, 300 datasets each) it rejected 7.0–25.3% of the time at alpha = 0.05.
- **Monte Carlo p-value for Andersen's test: `global_fit(method='andersen', p_method='monte-carlo')`**, optional (default `'chi2'`, except with `PCM`'s CML ridge; see above): `no_of_samples` datasets simulated from the pooled CML fit given each person's total and the items they responded to, each refitted by group and pooled with the same settings (`seed=`, `mc_exceedances=` as for Martin-Löf). On the null design above with `unused_categories='null'` and 199 samples, it rejected 3.7–4.3% of the time in three of the four PCM designs and 8.7% in the fourth (N = 300, random split; Monte Carlo SE of a 5% rate 1.3 percentage points); for RSM, 5.0% and 6.0%. About 0.04 s per sample on DESC-II (799 persons, 10 items).

- **Loaders: `missing=`, `sheet_name=` and `sep=`** (`loadup_slm`, `loadup_pcm`, `loadup_rsm`, `loadup_mfrm_single`; `missing=` was in `loadup_mfrm_xlsx_tabs` and `loadup_mfrm_multiple` only; `sheet_name=` and `sep=` also in `loadup_exogenous`, `sep=` in `loadup_mfrm_multiple`). Missing-value codes match however a number is written (`99` matches `99`, `99.0` and `'99'`); `sheet_name=` chooses the Excel sheet; `sep=` sets the separator of a text file (`None` detects it; `.tsv` files are read with tabs). MFRM loaders take `facet_name=` (default `'Rater'`) for the first index level.

- **Checking an `MFRM` anchor set: `check_anchor_homogeneity(anchors=)` on joint fits** (the default with `estimation='cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` or `'cml'`; `rater_homogeneity_test='screen'`), using the anchors' responses only. One fit gives every anchor its own location at every item-threshold cell, and the check takes two steps. (1) Global effect: an anchor's global effect is its mean location over the cells; every subset of the anchors (up to 12; with more, a multi-start search) is tried, largest first, and a subset agrees when each member's global effect minus the mean of the other members' is within `delta` (close-fit test, corrected across the members, `correction='bh'` by default). The largest agreeing set is suggested and the anchors left out dropped; tied sets are ranked by the shift of the zero point from the average of all the anchors given (then the range of their global effects, then their number of responses), listed in a warning, kept in `check.ties` and chosen with `apply_suggested(choice=k)`. (2) Pattern, within that set: `per_rater_model_selection(scheme='one-pass')` is run on the set's responses only, with the set as the anchors, so each member is tested by the selection's steps with the other members on `'global'` as the reference, at the check's `delta` (`delta_max` twice it); the check and `per_rater_model_selection(anchors=check.suggested)` therefore judge an anchor's pattern by the same tests. An anchor with cells cut stays on `'global'` in the selection, so it is tested instead by a close-fit test of the root mean square of its departures over its cells not cut. For each member, `check.pattern` reports the representation selected and the size of its departure: its departures from the other members' mean at each cell, less their weighted mean over the cells, as a root mean square with each cell weighted by the inverse sampling variance of the anchor's location there (as `per_rater_model_selection()` sizes a departure). An anchor selected off `'global'` anchors through its global effect only: it still helps fix the zero point, but its pattern is estimated freely (`AnchorSet.pattern_free`; in `calibrate(model='mixed', anchors=)` give it a representation with a pattern, which `per_rater_model_selection(anchors=)` selects, and `calibrate(model='mixed')` warns if it is on `'global'`). `delta` defaults to 0.1 median category widths (`delta_units='width'`, or `'logits'`); `delta=0` gives plain tests. The widths are those of the joint fit of every rater's responses with the anchors on `'global'` and the others on `'matrix'` (`per_rater_model_selection()`'s starting fit), anchored on the anchors given for the global effects and on the suggested set for the pattern, so the check and `per_rater_model_selection(anchors=check.suggested)` measure departures in the same unit. An overall test is also reported (all anchors on `'global'` against all on `'items'` and on `'thresholds'`, composite likelihood ratio with Kent's weights or conditional likelihood ratio for `'cml'`, and a Wald test of equal global effects, Holm across the three). Nothing is changed: the check object (also kept as `anchor_check`) reports `global_effects`, `pattern` and `suggestions`, and its `cut()`, `drop()`, `restore()`, `free_pattern()`, `hold_pattern()` and `apply_suggested()` return a new check on the adjusted set, logging each change; `action='apply'` returns the check on the suggested set; `report='full'` adds `profile` (the suggestion at `delta` 0.1, 0.15, 0.2 and 0.3 widths) and `subsets`. To anchor on the anchors' mean with no check, as in v1.4.0, pass a list of anchors to `calibrate_anchor()` or `calibrate(anchors=)`. In simulations (`'cp-full-rcl'`; 5 anchors and one other rater, every rater marking every person, 8 items, maximum score 3; thresholds 3 or 0.6 logits apart; N = 300 and 800; 20 datasets with no departure and 10 per departure scenario at each instrument and N), with no departure no anchor was dropped and 4 of 400 anchors were given a free pattern (14 of 1199 anchors with no departure over all the scenarios); an anchor 0.3 widths off in global effect was dropped in every dataset but one (0.6-logit thresholds, N = 300); with 3-logit thresholds, an anchor with one item shifted 0.5 widths, thresholds shifted by −0.25, 0 and +0.25 widths, or one middle-threshold cell shifted 1 width, was given a free pattern in every dataset, and one with a top-threshold cell shifted 1 width in 2 of 10 at N = 300 and 0 of 10 at N = 800 (few responses fall at an extreme threshold); with 0.6-logit thresholds these departures were found in 1–3 of 10 datasets at N = 300 and 3–7 of 10 at N = 800. For 99.5% of the anchors in the suggested sets, whether the check gave a free pattern matched whether `per_rater_model_selection(anchors=)` on every rater's responses selected the anchor off `'global'`. The Wald tests take Hotelling-type references on persons − 1 degrees of freedom (F for joint tests, t for single contrasts), because with few persons the sandwich covariance is poorly estimated and chi-squared references reject too often. Design notes: `ANCHOR_AGREEMENT_DESIGN.md`.
- **Anchor sets with cut cells: `calibrate(model='mixed', anchors=)`** (cells cut with `AnchorSet.cut()` or `check.cut()`) and `per_rater_model_selection(anchors=check.anchors)`. An anchor cut at an item, a threshold or an item-threshold pair is not an anchor there: it gets one free shift (`anchor_cuts_mixed`), its uncut cells stay at its global effect and fix the instrument, and its global effect is defined by them (the form of Jin and Eckes's, 2026, category-specific global effect shift). It is reported under the representation its cuts imply, in `rater_models`, the `facet_effects_mixed_{model}` tables and `rater_params_mixed`: threshold cuts only `'thresholds'`, item cuts only `'items'`, both `'bivector'`, any item-threshold pair `'matrix'`; `rater_models` may give that representation or `'global'` for it. A cut whose shift no remaining anchor can fix, given the representations, raises a `ValueError` naming the cells. Available with `'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` and `'cml'`, not with `unused_categories='null'`.
- **The stretch models' divergence test (`rater_stats_df(divergence_test=...)` for `'centrality'`, `'pseudo_halo'` and `'bistretch'`) takes its standard errors from the stacked sandwich** with `estimation='cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` or `'cml'` and no anchors: the free model (`'thresholds'`, `'items'` or `'bivector'`) and the stretch model are each fitted once to the same responses, each person's influence on both fits' effects is computed, and the standard error of each free-minus-stretch difference is the root of the summed squared differences in influence, which allows for the two fits' correlation as the paired bootstrap did. The paired bootstrap remains with anchors, `'pair-cpat'`, or where the sandwich does not apply; `divergence_se_method` records which was used. With the sandwich, `divergence_test='t'` uses the number of persons minus 1 as degrees of freedom. Simulations with the stretch model true (5 raters, 8 items, maximum score 3, N = 400; 150 datasets per model and estimation, 30 of them also with a 200-resample paired bootstrap): on the same datasets, the median ratio of sandwich to bootstrap standard error was 0.987–1.007 across the three models and three estimations (5th–95th percentiles 0.89–1.09, the bootstrap's own noise); each element's difference was flagged at alpha = 0.05 (uncorrected) in 0.044–0.066 of datasets with the sandwich and 0.033–0.069 with the bootstrap. The test now takes 0.01–0.08 s instead of 6–47 s per fit; in the example notebooks under CML, the stretch models' divergence cells took 1,345 s, 1,549 s and over 30 minutes before, and the whole notebooks now run in 31–61 s.

### Fixed
- **Recalibrating `SLM`, `PCM` or `RSM` with other settings now refreshes the results derived from the earlier fit** (v1.4.0 known issue). Standard errors, person estimates, fit statistics and their tables, and anchored, DIF and model selection results were computed when missing and otherwise reused, so after, for example, `calibrate()`, `item_stats_df()`, `calibrate(constant=2.0)`, `item_stats_df()`, the second table showed the new estimates with the earlier standard errors and fit statistics. `calibrate()` now removes what the earlier fit left, and these are recomputed on demand from the new fit; the data and the constructor's settings are kept. `MFRM` already did this per representation. Regression tests added.
- **Results and plotting methods calibrate an uncalibrated `SLM`, `PCM` or `RSM` object first** (v1.4.0 known issue), as `fit_statistics()` and the stats tables did: `person`, `person_estimates`, `csem`, `score_lookup`, `score_lookup_table`, `icc`, `crcs`, `threshold_ccs`, `iic`, `tcc`, `test_info`, `test_csem` and `wright_map` previously raised an `AttributeError`. The class-interval methods also estimate persons, and `std_residuals_plot()` also computes the fit statistics, when these are missing. Regression tests added.
- **`per_rater_model_selection(test='AIC', estimation='cpat-rcl')`: the relative likelihood check did not allow for the composite likelihood.** With `aic_relative_likelihood=True` (the default), ΔAIC is divided by the composite likelihood's mean weight (the larger representation's effective number of parameters minus the smaller's, divided by the difference in free parameters) before the relative likelihood exp(−ΔAIC/2) is compared with `alpha`; this was done for `'cp-full-rcl'` only, so under `'cpat-rcl'`, where the mean weight was about 3.9 in the measured case, the check overstated the evidence for the larger representation about fourfold. The close-fit test that follows each step limited the effect: in 24 simulated datasets (10 raters on eight representations, 12 items, maximum score 4, N = 200 and 400), 2 of 240 selections changed.
- **`per_rater_model_selection()` with `estimation='cpat-rcl'` raised a `KeyError`** when its ladder (`test='LR'`, and now `'AIC'` / `'BIC'`) fitted a full set of effects on one side beside a stretch on the other: the smoothing constant's spread over cells had no entry for those two representations. They now take `bivector`'s.
- **Close-fit tests no longer hang** (`per_rater_model_selection(test='LR')` with `delta` or `stretch_delta` above 0, and the RSM-within-PCM close-fit test): the search for the true departure of the tolerance's size widened its bracket without limit, refitting at every step, and on some data never stopped (one simulated dataset: over 6 hours). The bracket is now capped; where it fails, that test uses the noncentral approximation, as where the exact distribution is not available.
- **`MFRM.anchor_std_errors(model='mixed')` on a `calibrate(model='mixed')` fit** raised `KeyError: 'sm'` (it sent the joint fit down the single-representation sandwich route). The fit is already in its anchors' frame, so it now stores the fit's `std_errors(model='mixed')` results under the `anchor_` names, running `std_errors` first if needed; other anchors raise a `ValueError` asking for a refit, as for `calibrate_anchor`.

- **Conditional item chi-square (Q1) with fewer than about 60 persons** (`fit_statistics(conditional=True)`) was not computed, without a warning: the default grouping merged groups of fewer than 30 persons until one group was left, so every item had Q1 ≈ 0 with df = 0 and p = NaN, and `item_q1_overall` reported p = 1.0 with 1 group. Merging now stops at 2 groups, so small samples get 2 groups (of fewer than 30 persons) and Q1 is computed, however unreliable at such sizes. With fewer than 2 groups (supplied through `q1_groups`, or every person location tied), Q1 and its p-values, per item and overall, are NaN, with a `UserWarning`.
- **`MFRM` `'centrality'`, `'pseudo_halo'` and `'bistretch'` with a single item** failed with `LinAlgError: SVD did not converge` (and LAPACK "DLASCL" messages): the `'pair-cpat'` fit the estimation starts from gives no item location for one item (NaN). The start now uses 0, the centred location of a single item, and the fit runs, with the existing warning that one item reduces the model to RSM; the item stretch of `'pseudo_halo'`/`'bistretch'` then has no data and stays at 1.
- **Compatibility with the declared minimum and the latest dependency versions:** the full test suite was run with NumPy 1.24, SciPy 1.9, pandas 2.0, matplotlib 3.6 and scikit-learn 1.1 (Python 3.10), and with NumPy 2.5, SciPy 1.18, pandas 3.1, matplotlib 3.11 and scikit-learn 1.9. Fixed: a legend helper used `Legend.legend_handles`, which matplotlib added in 3.7; under pandas 3 the anchor check's `suggestions` `Cell` column (and `log_table()`'s `cell` column) showed NaN instead of None for a dropped anchor.
- **`rater_stats_df`, `item_stats_df`, `threshold_stats_df` and `category_stats_df` with `model='mixed'` and `anchors=`** raised `KeyError: 'sm'` for a joint mixed fit (the anchored standard errors were computed as for a single representation). A joint fit is already in its anchors' frame, so its own anchors now give the fit as it is, and other anchors raise a `ValueError` asking for a refit.
- **Selection tests stopped with `LinAlgError: SVD did not converge`** when LAPACK's SVD-based least-squares solver failed on an ill-conditioned (but finite) Jacobian, in `per_rater_model_selection()`, `model_selection()`'s composite likelihood ratio test and `check_anchor_homogeneity()`'s overall test. The solution is then taken from the complete orthogonal factorisation, which gives the same minimum-norm solution.
- **`per_rater_model_selection(min_effect=...)`** was silently ignored with `estimation='cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` or `'cml'`; it now gives a `UserWarning` saying it applies to `'pair-cpat'` only.
- **`MFRM.recovery_stats(model='mixed')` failed** in two cases: when a representation's raters all had the same generating value (e.g. anchors simulated with `neutral_anchors=True`, all at 0), fitting the plot's regression line failed (`LinAlgError`); fit lines are now drawn only when the generating values vary (all classes). When the fitted representations differ from the generating ones (e.g. after `per_rater_model_selection()`), the representations' tables did not line up (`ValueError`); such a fit is now compared on every rater's item × threshold effects, as a fit of another representation already was.
- **`MFRM.threshold_stats_df(model='mixed')`** showed no standard errors (their index, 1 to `max_score`, did not match the table's), and with an interval no confidence bounds; both are now shown.
- **`MFRM.targeting()`, `local_dependence_gamma()` and `global_stats_df()`** failed with an internal `AttributeError` (they are for `SLM`, `PCM` and `RSM`); they now raise a `NotImplementedError` saying so.
- **CML with unused categories (`MFRM`, `RSM`, `PCM`): two more problems found in simulation.** With categories 1 and 2 unused and maximum score 4 or 5, `unused_categories='null'` did not converge in 13 of 400 datasets: for those data the CML estimates of the steps between the used categories do not exist, because the conditional likelihood is flat along some combination of the steps where the fit stops (its smallest curvature along them was 4e-9 or less, against 9.5 or more in the fits that converged). The warning now says that the estimates do not exist and recommends `unused_categories='ridge'`, in place of "did not converge" (`MFRM`, `RSM`, `PCM`); it flagged all 13 and none of the other 387, and in the same settings for `PCM` (10 items, item threshold deviations SD 0.3) the 1 non-converged fit of 400 and none of the other 399; all 400 `RSM` fits converged. With maximum score 3 and categories 1 and 2 unused (only scores 0 and 3 used), `'ridge'` reported non-convergence in all 200 datasets, although its item locations were accurate: every width is next to an unused category, the likelihood pushes them all down, and the ridge, which pulled them towards each other, left their common size free. With only two categories used it now pulls each width towards 0 (`MFRM`, `RSM`; for `PCM`, whose ridge pulls each group of items' mean widths towards the mean over all groups, a group whose items use only two categories has its mean widths pulled towards 0); all 200 fits converge in each class (median item RMSE 0.038 `MFRM`, 0.105 / 0.061 `PCM` at 150 / 500 persons), where `PCM`'s had reported non-convergence in all 200.
- **CML with `unused_categories='null'` (`MFRM`): two fit failures fixed.** Two adjacent unused categories (e.g. maximum score 4, categories 1 and 2 never used): the fits of `'global'`, `'items'`, `'thresholds'`, `'bivector'` and `'matrix'` did not converge, ending far below the maximum of the conditional likelihood. The CML search starts from the `'cp-full-rcl'` estimates. In one example these thresholds were −8.86, −2.95, 2.95 and 8.86 logits. With categories 1 and 2 unused, the model's probability of each score depends on two sums of them: −8.86 for the step from 0 to 3 (the first three thresholds) and 8.86 for the step from 3 to 4. Putting these into the model's category probabilities, a person located at the item's location (rater effects aside) scores 0, 3 or 4 with probabilities 0.0001, 0.9997 and 0.0001, whereas 25%, 64% and 11% of the data's responses were 0, 3 and 4. With the probabilities this close to 0 and 1, moving those two sums in opposite directions barely changes the likelihood, so Newton-Raphson's step in that direction was enormous (about 10^8 logits), and the step-halving accepted the worse values it led to. Now a step that does not increase the penalised likelihood is not taken: L-BFGS, with the exact gradient, continues from the current values and Newton-Raphson finishes (`convergence['solver']` is then `'lbfgs+newton'`; this applies to every CML fit). Two non-adjacent unused categories (e.g. 1 and 3): the `'centrality'`, `'pseudo_halo'` and `'bistretch'` fits stayed at the `'global'` estimates while reporting convergence. No two adjacent categories were both used, so `'cp-full-rcl'` had no comparisons to use, and its estimates, the starting values, had every item location 0 and thresholds cancelling in pairs; at those values the stretch parameters do not change the likelihood, and the fit removed them as unidentified. Which parameters are identified is now decided both at the starting values and at evenly spaced item locations and thresholds.

- **Refitting an `MFRM` model kept the previous fit's results:** methods compute derived results (fit matrices, fit statistics, CSEM, reliability, the stats tables, standard-error tables) when they are missing and reuse them when present, so after `calibrate()` was called again for the same model (other settings, or `'mixed'` on other representations, as `per_rater_model_selection` does) `fit_statistics()` and the stats tables kept reporting the previous fit, and a `'mixed'` refit could make `recovery_stats()` fail on a missing standard-error table. A refit now clears what was derived from the model's previous fit (and DIF/DRF results run on it), and these are recomputed on demand; in every one of the 8 representations and `'mixed'`, a refitted object now matches a fresh object fitted the same way. `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM` recomputed these already; their CML notes (`cml_dropped_responses`, `cml_unused_categories`) are now dropped by a refit with another estimation.
- **`MFRM_Sim(manual_raters=...)` read labelled values by position or failed on them**: under `model='items'` a pandas Series of item effects was read in its own order rather than by item name, and under `model='thresholds'` a dict keyed by threshold failed. Labelled values (dict, Series, DataFrame) are now aligned by label, with an error naming any missing or unknown label, and unlabelled values must have exactly the right shape.
- **The Kauermann-Carroll small-sample correction was sometimes left out** (`global_fit(method='wald')`, whose default is `se_correction='kauermann-carroll'`; `SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`). Each person's score is corrected by (I − H)^(−1/2), H the person's leverage, and this was computed from an eigendecomposition of a non-symmetric matrix. When a person's contribution to the information has low rank, its eigenvector matrix can be singular, and the correction was then silently skipped for the whole block of 500 persons containing that person. For example, with each person answering 3 of 10 `RSM` items (N = 400, 5 datasets), 800 of 2,000 person scores were left uncorrected and the Wald statistic changed by up to 0.51. It is now computed in an equivalent symmetric form, which applies to every person; results are unchanged where the old computation worked.
- **`MFRM` objects could not be pickled** (or passed to a `multiprocessing` worker): the facet-named method aliases (`rater_stats_df_{model}`, `rater_res_corr_analysis_{model}`, or the names for the object's `facet`) were lambdas defined in `__init__`. They are now `functools.partial` objects; calling them is unchanged.
- **Westfall–Young familywise p-values of the conditional fit bootstrap were liberal** (`fit_statistics(conditional=True, bootstrap_samples=...)`, all four classes: infit, outfit, item-restscore, Q1, Q3). Each statistic was centred and scaled by the mean and SD of its simulated values only. A simulated value then contributed to its own scale and the observed one did not, which made the maximum over items too large. In simulations from the true thresholds, any item reached familywise p ≤ 0.05 in 0.059–0.076 of datasets at the 0.05 level, where it should be at most 0.05. The observed value is now included (`_esf.studentise`); on exchangeable data the rate is 0.051, and on the same null simulations (`SLM`, `PCM` with `'pair'` and `'cml'`, `RSM` with `'pair-cpat'` and `'cpat-rcl'`; 10 items, N 200 and 500, 200 datasets each, 99 samples) 0.043–0.048 with the fitted thresholds and 0.044–0.056 with the true ones. Two-sided per-item bootstrap p-values change slightly for the same reason; one-sided ones (Q3, Q1) are unchanged.
- **`MFRM` stretch models fitted with `estimation='pair-cpat'` replaced their parent model's results.** `centrality`, `pseudo_halo` and `bistretch` re-estimate their parent model (`thresholds`, `items`, `bivector`) with their own settings. That re-estimate overwrote the parent's stored results (`facet_effects_thresholds`, `facet_effects_items`, or `facet_effects_bivector` with its item and threshold parts, `facet_effects_matrix` and the marginal facet effects), and `calibrate_anchor()` on a stretch model overwrote the parent's anchored results the same way. The parent's results now stay those of its own calibration. The stretch model keeps its re-estimate for its own anchoring and for `divergence_test=`, so stretch model results are unchanged.
  - Effect on `model_selection(test='LR')` with `estimation='pair-cpat'`: the stretch models are fitted after their parents, so the parametric bootstrap compared the parents' refits with grids their own settings do not produce. In simulations, this made the bootstrap reject a true smaller model too often on crossed designs: 0.092 at the 0.05 level (data from the global, items, thresholds and bivector models; crossed design, I 6, R 5, K 3; N 200 and 500). On the same datasets it is now 0.067 (0.058 for pairs without a stretch model), with power unchanged.
- **Loaders changed data without saying so** (`loadup_slm`, `loadup_pcm`, `loadup_rsm`, the three MFRM loaders):
  - Values that are not valid scores (text, negative or non-whole numbers, scores above the maximum) are still set to missing, but now with one `UserWarning` naming the values and the items they occur in.
  - In long format, a response given more than once still keeps the lowest score, now with a `UserWarning`.
  - `loadup_mfrm_xlsx_tabs` and `loadup_mfrm_multiple` rounded non-whole scores down (2.5 became 2); they are now set to missing and reported, as `loadup_mfrm_single` already did.
  - `loadup_pcm` and `loadup_rsm` inferred the maximum score from every number in the data, so a missing code such as 99 became a score of 99; the maximum now comes from whole-number scores of 0 or more, and missing codes can be passed as `missing=`.
- **JSON files in the loaders:** `person_names` and `item_names` were ignored, so in a list of records (one object per person) the ID field was read as an item. A list of records, an object of columns (`DataFrame.to_json()`'s default) and pandas' `'split'` layout are now all read as the CSV equivalent.
- **Loaders name the person index `'Person'`** (`loadup_slm`, `loadup_pcm`, `loadup_rsm`, `loadup_exogenous`); it was named after the first column's header (e.g. `'ID'`), or not named with `person_names=False`.
- **`global_fit(method='andersen')` failed with a NumPy error** (`need at least one array to concatenate`) when dropping items without CML estimates in a group left fewer than two items in common; it now raises the intended `ValueError` (fewer than two items have CML estimates in both groups).
- **Confidence bounds in the statistics tables were wrong, missing or raised errors once standard errors had been computed** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`, `MFRM`). Asked for an `interval` at another width, `item_stats_df`, `threshold_stats_df`, `category_stats_df` and `MFRM.rater_stats_df` (anchored or not) returned the bounds already stored, at the earlier width, under the new labels: `interval=0.5` after `interval=0.95` labelled the 95% bounds `25.0%` and `75.0%`. Asked for bounds when the stored standard errors had none, `PCM`/`RSM` `threshold_stats_df` raised a `TypeError`/`AttributeError`, `PCM`/`RSM` `category_stats_df` left the bound columns out, and `SLM.item_stats_df(full=True)` raised an `AttributeError`. `std_errors()` now records the width of the bounds it stores, and each table runs it again when the width asked for differs. Regression tests added.
- **`dif_test` when the reference and focal groups share only one item** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; e.g. under CML, after a common item without estimates in one group is removed): it raised an error about anchor selection; it now says that DIF needs at least two common items and names any items removed from the group fits, with the reason.
- **`calibrate_anchor(calibrate=True)` recalibrates with the estimation method and solver of the latest calibration.** It used the default estimation (`SLM`, `PCM`: PAIR, discarding CML estimates; `RSM`, with `estimation=None`: `'cp-full-rcl'`, whatever the object had used); `RSM`'s `estimation=` argument still overrides.
- **`MFRM` residual analyses (`item_res_corr_analysis`, `facet_res_corr_analysis`) had the same eigenvalue error as `res_corr_analysis()`:** the PCA treated the correlation matrix as a data matrix; eigenvalues, eigenvectors, variance explained and loadings now come from its eigendecomposition. `item_residual_correlations_{model}` now correlates the raw residuals (Yen's Q3), as the facet element correlations and the other classes do; `item_res_corr_analysis(std_residual_df)` still accepts the standardised residuals alone, with an optional `residual_df=`.
- **`res_corr_analysis()` (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`) reported eigenvalues that were not those of the standardised residuals' correlation matrix:** it ran a PCA treating that matrix as a data matrix, so `eigenvalues` were the variances of its columns' principal components (e.g. 0.14 where the first contrast was 1.42), which a conventional first-contrast rule (e.g. > 2) could never meet; `eigenvectors`, `variance_explained` and `loadings` were affected likewise. They now come from the eigendecomposition of the correlation matrix (eigenvalues in descending order, the first being the first contrast; variance explained = eigenvalue / number of items; loadings = eigenvector × √eigenvalue, so that the loadings reproduce the correlation matrix). Shapes and labels are unchanged; values change.
- **`SLM.res_corr_analysis()`'s `residual_correlations` now correlates the raw residuals (observed minus expected), as `PCM` and `RSM` already did;** it correlated the standardised residuals. The PCA, which uses the standardised residuals in all three classes, is unchanged.
- **`MFRM.dif_test` when the groups were scored by different facet elements:** under every model except `'global'`, each group's item locations and thresholds are averages of the facet-element-specific ones over the facet elements that scored that group, so when the reference and focal groups were scored by different (partly overlapping) facet elements, `dif_test` compared averages over different facet elements, which produced apparent DIF and DCF with none present (in null simulations, per-item Type I error up to 0.66 and DCF Type I error up to 0.73 at nominal 0.05). Each group is now anchored on the facet elements shared by both groups (`calibrate_anchor()`, re-estimated in each bootstrap resample) before comparison, and in the omnibus test each group's item locations and thresholds are adjusted by the pooled model's mean facet element effects over the facet elements that scored the group. After the fix, per-item DIF and DCF were at or near nominal under all three estimation methods (`'cp-full-rcl'`: at most 0.08; `'cpat-rcl'` and `'pair-cpat'`: mostly 0.03-0.08). Results are unchanged when both groups were scored by the same facet elements. If the groups share no facet elements, `dif_test` raises a `ValueError` for every model except `'global'`.
- **`dif_test` (all four classes), `global_fit` (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`) and `MFRM.drf_test` when the groups responded to different items:** each group was calibrated on every item, including items it never responded to, which have no estimate in that group: `SLM.dif_test` raised an error, `RSM` gave differences of about 1e14, `PCM` and `MFRM` reported items one group never responded to, and `MFRM.drf_test` raised an error or gave meaningless results. Each group is now calibrated on the items its persons responded to, and only items shared by both groups are compared (`global_fit` matches parameters by name). The omnibus tests hold the items only one group responded to at their pooled values and anchor the person scale on the shared items. In null simulations with partly overlapping item sets, per-item DIF, DCF and `global_fit` were at or near nominal (`SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`: 0.025-0.07).
- **`MFRM.drf_test` frame of reference:** each group's stretch parameters were relative to the facet elements that scored it and its shift `lambda` to item locations centred over the items its persons responded to, and `'matrix'`'s threshold marginal was averaged over the items its persons responded to, so groups scored by different facet elements or responding to different items showed apparent DRF (in null simulations, `lambda` up to 0.49, `omega` up to 0.50, and per-item tests under `'bivector'`/`'matrix'` up to 0.40). Both groups are now put on the same reference before comparison: for `'centrality'`/`'pseudo_halo'`/`'bistretch'` the tested facet elements are the reference (mean shift 0, mean stretches 1) and item locations are centred over the shared items; `'matrix'`'s threshold marginal is averaged over the shared items. After the fix the tests were at or near nominal under all three estimation methods (mostly 0.03-0.08).
- **`MFRM.calibrate_anchor` for `matrix` changed the fitted model:** it subtracted the anchors' mean facet effect for each item-threshold cell from every facet element, but moved only its item and threshold margins into the item locations and thresholds, so the anchors' item-by-threshold remainder was removed from every facet element's threshold locations (in one simulated dataset, the log-likelihood fell from -9638.5 to -9660.9). Only the item and threshold margins are now subtracted, and the remainder stays in the facet effects, so anchoring leaves every threshold location unchanged apart from a common shift taken up by the persons, as for the other representations. Anchored item locations and thresholds, and the anchored marginal facet effects, are unchanged; anchored `matrix` facet effects, person estimates and fit statistics, and `mixed` results with anchors, change.
- **Standard errors for `method='cos'`, `'ls'` and `'evm'`:** the analytic (sandwich) standard errors are the covariance of the Bradley-Terry estimating equations, which only `method='log-lik'` solves, but were used for all four methods (in null simulations, `PCM.dif_test` under these methods was mildly liberal: 0.06-0.08). `std_errors(se_method=None)` (now the default for `SLM`) uses the sandwich for `'log-lik'` and the bootstrap for the other methods (`SLM`, `PCM`, and `RSM` with `estimation='pair-cpat'`); an explicit `se_method='sandwich'` with them raises a `ValueError`. `global_fit` then uses bootstrap covariances (`no_of_samples=`, default 500). With bootstrap standard errors, `PCM.dif_test` under these methods was at nominal (0.045-0.062).
- **`dif_test()`'s anchor selection (`selection_method='wald'`, the default) ignored the reference group's error** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`, `MFRM`): each candidate anchor's deviation from the translation constant was divided by the focal group's SE only, so the test was too liberal (with equal groups, an SE too small by about a factor of √2) and the selected anchors depended on which group was the reference. The deviation's SE now counts both groups' covariance matrices (analytic, else from the bootstrap resamples, else the squared SEs) and removes the item's own share of the translation constant; anchors are weighted by 1 / (reference variance + focal variance); and the statistic is referred to a t-distribution with Welch-Satterthwaite degrees of freedom from the two groups' person counts. The resampled re-selection behind the DIF standard errors (`_anchor_tc_draws`) uses the same rule. In null simulations (`SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`, 12 items, group sizes 50/50 to 900/300, 300 datasets each), the share of DIF-free items dropped from the anchor set at `wald_alpha=0.05` fell from 8.3-17.6% to 4.4-6.5%; the final DIF tests, already valid, moved from 3.7-6.0% to 4.4-6.3% rejection of DIF-free items, and power for an item with 0.5 logits of DIF rose by up to 5.3 points. `anchor_table` (and `calibrate_anchor`'s `anchor_selection`) report `SE (deviation)`, `t` and `df` in place of `z`. In `calibrate_anchor(selection_method='wald')` the anchor values stay fixed (focal error only), with the item's share of the translation constant removed and a t reference on persons - 1.
- **`PCM.global_fit()` ignored `se_correction` with `'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'` estimates** (the default estimation): the composite-likelihood sandwich had no Kauermann-Carroll path, so the Wald test used the uncorrected covariance while its summary reported `'kauermann-carroll'`. The correction is now applied, from each person's contribution to the information (`_rcl.pcm_person_bread`; the contributions sum to the stored information to rounding error). In null simulations (12 items, maximum score 3) PCM's rejection rate at alpha = 0.05 with the correction was 4.6-7.3%, against 5.6-9.7% without it.

### Changed
- **`MFRM.rater_stats_df(model='mixed')` no longer warns by default.** `marginal` now defaults to `None`, which gives `'matrix'` its marginal table as before; `'mixed'`, which has no marginal table and always returns the full (item, threshold) table, warns only when `marginal=True` is passed.
- **AIC's relative-likelihood check now steps down** (`per_rater_model_selection(test='AIC')` with `'pair-cpat'`, `aic_relative_likelihood=True`; the other estimations, and `MFRM.model_selection()`, now build up instead: see Added): if the lowest-AIC representation fails the check against the baseline (the candidate with the fewest free parameters, `'global'` when it is a candidate), the one with the next lowest AIC is checked, and so on; previously the baseline was kept as soon as the lowest-AIC representation failed. With a likelihood this changes nothing, since the lowest-AIC representation has the lowest relative likelihood of the baseline.
- **`per_rater_model_selection(test='LR')` table:** tests involving the representations fitted for the likelihood ratio ladder only (a full set of effects on one side beside a stretch on the other) are not shown.
- **`PCM`: with mixed maximum scores, `calibrate(constant=None)` now sets `self.constant` to `None`.** The per-cell constants actually applied are in `self.cell_constants`; the single value previously stored (the rule evaluated at the mean maximum score) was never applied and suggested a constant that did not exist. With one maximum score, or a supplied `constant`, nothing changes.
- **The warning for a score category with no responses (`RSM`, `MFRM`) is rewritten.** It names the thresholds either side of the category (τ₂ and τ₃, with Greek letters and subscripts) and says that only their sum can be estimated from the data (none of them when only the lowest and highest categories are used). With `'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` or `'pair-cpat'` it says that the widths next to the category are set by a smoothing rule, which in simulations also distorted other thresholds, and recommends `estimation='cml'` with its default `unused_categories='ridge'` unless the category is not a genuine response option or the scores are miscoded; with `'cml'` it says how `'ridge'` and `'null'` treat those thresholds. When no two adjacent categories are both used, it adds that the item locations cannot be estimated either. It now also covers an unused bottom or top score category (previously only the vague "max_score exceeds the maximum observed score" warning, and nothing for an unused bottom category): the thresholds beyond the used range (τ₁ for an unused category 0, τ_K for an unused category K) cannot be estimated at all, since the data are consistent with them being infinitely low or high; their reported values are set by the smoothing or penalty, and, as the thresholds are centred, they move the other thresholds and the item locations' reference point too. It says how to rescore (subtract the lowest used score from every response; lower `max_score`). The separate "no information on the distance between thresholds" warning is no longer given for distances next to an unused category, which this warning already describes.
- **`RSM`: `threshold_method='cpat-cml'` is renamed `'cpat-cl'`** (`calibrate()` with `estimation='pair-cpat'`, and `threshold_set()`). The estimator is unchanged: for each distance between adjacent thresholds it maximises the composite likelihood of CPAT's threshold patterns over item pairs (a product of binomials, one per pair: persons scoring `(k, k)` against `(k-1, k+1)` on two items), given the item locations. The old name suggested the conditional maximum likelihood of `estimation='cml'`, which it is not. `'cpat-cml'` is still accepted, with a `FutureWarning`, and will be removed; objects saved with it still load and refit.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()` bootstrap test (`'pair-cpat'`) now compares the two fitted representations directly.** The statistic is the sum, over facet element × item × threshold, of the squared difference between the larger and smaller representations' fitted locations, each relative to its own mean. Both representations are refitted on each simulated dataset. Previously the smaller representation's fit to the data was kept on every simulated dataset, and the statistic left out the directions the smaller representation could move in and weighted each location by its precision. In null simulations (4 generating representations, crossed 6 items x 5 facet elements and double-marking 6 items x 6 facet elements, maximum score 3, N 200 and 500, 60 datasets each, all 13 nested pairs, 99 samples), the rate of rejecting the smaller representation at 0.05 was 0.048 at N 200 and 0.073 at N 500 (with the previous statistic, 0.063–0.089 over `'pair-cpat'` and `'cpat-rcl'`); power was unchanged. On 600 new datasets at N 500 (same designs, null-true pairs only) the rate was 0.054.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()` defaults to `test=None`**, which resolves to `'LR'` (the bootstrap test, the only test available) when the candidates were calibrated with `estimation='pair-cpat'`, and to `'AIC'` for `'cml'`, `'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'`. Previously the default `test='AIC'` raised a `ValueError` for `'pair-cpat'`.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()` compares `'cpat-rcl'` candidates on CPAT's composite likelihood**, as `'cp-full-rcl'` candidates are compared on the full pattern set's: AIC and BIC with the effective number of parameters tr(J H⁻¹) (Varin and Vidoni, 2005), and the composite likelihood-ratio test with Kent's weights, each on CPAT's patterns (the full pattern set's comparisons of adjacent scores), unsmoothed, at each fit. Previously `'cpat-rcl'` had only the bootstrap test. A `'mixed'` candidate fitted with `'cpat-rcl'` is now compared with the others (previously no comparison was returned for it), on the same composite log-likelihood that `per_rater_model_selection()` uses. In simulation (generating representations global, items, thresholds and bivector; 6 items, maximum score 3; crossed with 5 facet elements at N 30, 50, 100, 200 and 500, and double marking with 6 facet elements at N 100, 300 and 900, about N/3 scripts per facet element; 100 datasets per cell, the same data for both estimations), `'cpat-rcl'` matched `'cp-full-rcl'`: AIC chose the generating representation at rates within 0.05 of `'cp-full-rcl'`'s in every cell (worst over the generating representations, `'cpat-rcl'` against `'cp-full-rcl'`: 0.01 and 0.00 at crossed N 30, 0.70 and 0.73 at N 200, 0.94 and 0.95 at N 500, 0.83 and 0.85 at double marking N 900); the composite likelihood-ratio test rejected null-true pairs at 0.05 at rates of 0.033–0.072 (`'cp-full-rcl'`: 0.032–0.066), the highest for both at double marking N 300.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()` and `MFRM.per_rater_model_selection()`: `models=` renamed `candidate_models=`.** `models=` still works, with a `FutureWarning`; passing both raises a `ValueError`.
- **`RSM.model_selection()` / `PCM.model_selection()` bootstrap test (PAIR's `'cos'`, `'ls'` and `'evm'` methods) now compares the two fitted models.** The statistic is the sum, over items and thresholds, of the squared difference between PCM's and RSM's fitted thresholds, each item's thresholds taken relative to its central location. Both models are refitted on each simulated dataset. Previously it measured PCM's fit against the RSM-form thresholds closest to it, which neither model had fitted.
- **Conditional simulation given each person's total (the parametric bootstraps of `model_selection()`, conditional fit p-values and Monte Carlo test p-values) is faster with missing responses.** The suffix ESFs of all missingness patterns are now built together, and each item's score is drawn for all persons at once. The ESFs are reused between resamples when the thresholds and missingness patterns are unchanged, up to about 80 MB. The conditional probabilities are the same as before, but with missing responses the random numbers are used in a different order, so a given seed gives different resamples; with complete data the resamples are unchanged. Per resample: 1,000 persons, 20 items, maximum score 4, 20% missing went from 504 to 4.3 ms; 200 persons, 10 items, 20% missing from 29 to 0.7 ms; 5,000 persons, 50 items, 30% missing (too large to reuse) from 6.6 to 1.8 s.
- **`RSM.model_selection()` and `PCM.model_selection()`: PCM is preferred only when RSM is also rejected by the close-fit test.** The default is `test=None`, which resolves to `'AIC'` with `aic_relative_likelihood=True` (as v1.4.0's `aic_sig_test=True`), or to `'LR'` with PAIR's `'cos'`, `'ls'` and `'evm'` (whose only test is the bootstrap test) or when `sampling=` is given; `model_comparison_rsm_pcm_test` records the test used. `delta` now applies with every test: `delta=None` means 0.1 median category widths (0 with `sampling=n` or `'dynamic'`, the bootstrap test, or when the median width cannot be used, with a warning), and AIC, BIC and the LR test prefer PCM only when the close-fit test of RSM within PCM (each item's departure as well as the overall one, with the default `delta_measure='either'`) rejects at `alpha`. Without a tolerance, every test chooses PCM at large N for RSM data that are only slightly wrong. In simulations (`'cp-full-rcl'`; 10 items with maximum score 3, or 20 with maximum score 5; 100 datasets each), the data were RSM data disturbed in a way neither model represents: item discriminations varying (log-SD 0.1 or 0.2), noise added to each response's category log-odds (SD 0.1 or 0.2), or two dimensions correlated 0.9 or 0.8. Over these, the largest proportion of datasets for which PCM was chosen at 300 / 1,000 / 5,000 persons was 0.18 / 0.88 / 1.00 for the plain LR test, 0.03 / 0.04 / 1.00 for AIC, 0.02 / 0.01 / 1.00 for AIC with the relative-likelihood check, and 0.01 / 0.01 / 0.60 with the tolerance added; BIC never chose PCM. The 0.60 is a design (log-SD 0.2, 20 items, 5,000 persons) where the most departing item's estimated departure had a median of 0.17 widths, beyond the tolerance. Pass `delta=0` for the previous decisions.
- **`model_selection(test='AIC')`'s `aic_sig_test` is renamed `aic_relative_likelihood`, and it reports a relative likelihood, not a p-value, and the level of its test** (`RSM`, `PCM`, `MFRM`, and `MFRM.per_rater_model_selection()`). Decisions are unchanged. The quantity compared with `alpha`, exp(−ΔAIC / 2), is the relative likelihood of the simpler model; it is now 1 when the simpler model has the lower AIC (previously exp(−|ΔAIC| / 2), the other model's relative likelihood). The rule is a likelihood ratio test at level P(χ²(d) > 2d + 2 ln(1 / `alpha`)), d the difference in free parameters: below `alpha` and falling as d grows (at `alpha` = 0.05, 0.0063 at d = 6, 0.00095 at d = 19, 2.2 × 10⁻⁶ at d = 57). The stricter level suits a likelihood calculated unconditionally (jointly with person estimates, as in v1.4.0), whose likelihood ratio is inflated; with the conditional and composite likelihoods used now, whose likelihood ratio tests hold their level, it is a more conservative test than `test='LR'` that favours the simpler model. Renamed outputs: the `RSM`/`PCM` AIC summary row `p-value` is now `Relative likelihood`, with a new `Test level` row; the `per_rater_model_selection_table` column `p` is now `Relative likelihood`, with a new `Test level` column; with `estimation='pair-cpat'` and anchors, the column `p_vs_baseline` is now `relative_likelihood_vs_baseline`, with a new `test_level` column. New attributes `model_comparison_rsm_pcm_aic_relative_likelihood`, `model_comparison_rsm_pcm_aic_level`, `model_comparison_mfrm_aic_relative_likelihood` and `model_comparison_mfrm_aic_level`; the v1.4.0 attributes `model_comparison_rsm_pcm_aic_p` and `model_comparison_mfrm_aic_p` still hold the relative likelihood. `aic_sig_test=` still works, with a FutureWarning, and will be removed.
- **Tests reject, and tables flag, when p ≤ `alpha`** (previously p < `alpha`), in every class: `Flagged` columns, `reject` in `global_fit()`, the counts in `global_stats_df()`, and the choices in `model_selection()`. This only changes results for p-values that take a limited set of values: Monte Carlo, bootstrap and permutation p-values, p = (1 + e) / (B + 1) with e the simulated statistics at least as large as the observed one out of B. With p < `alpha`, a test with 0.05 × (B + 1) a whole number rejected at most 0.05 − 1 / (B + 1) of the time under the null (0.045 for `model_selection()`'s default of 199 bootstrap samples, 0.040 at 99); with p ≤ `alpha` the rate is at most `alpha` for any B, and exactly `alpha` for such B. Results from continuous p-values are unchanged.
- **`'cp-full-rcl'` is the default estimation for `SLM` and `PCM`**, as for `RSM` and `MFRM`, so every class fits the same pairwise conditional likelihood by default (`calibrate(estimation=None)`, which resolves to `'cp-full-rcl'`, or to `'pair'` when `method` is `'cos'`, `'ls'` or `'evm'`). `SLM`: the estimates are unchanged (for dichotomous items that likelihood is the Bradley-Terry likelihood, which PAIR's `method='log-lik'` maximises), and `self.estimation` now reads `'cp-full-rcl'`. `PCM`: estimates change from PAIR to the full-pattern pairwise conditional likelihood with its ridge, and `unused_categories` defaults to `'ridge'`; pass `estimation='pair'` for the previous estimates. In simulations (24 designs: 200–1,000 persons, 10 or 20 items, equal or mixed maximum scores, 0% or 20% missing; 200 datasets each) its sandwich standard errors matched the spread of the estimates (SD / SE 0.97–1.06, median per design), with 95% coverage 0.87–0.95 on average, lower for the most extreme thresholds, which the ridge pulls in. Its DIF tests held their level as under PAIR (per-item 0.035–0.048 at alpha = 0.05, DCF 0.049–0.053) with 0.01–0.045 more power, and `global_fit` rejected in 0.058–0.075 of null datasets (PAIR 0.050–0.068).
- **`icc()` and `iic()`: the first argument is now `items`** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`, `MFRM`, and MFRM's per-representation `icc_*`/`iic_*`), since it takes one item name or a list. Positional calls are unchanged; `item=` still works but raises a `FutureWarning` and will be removed in a future release; passing both raises a `TypeError`.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()` no longer uses person estimates or the joint likelihood**: the comparison follows the candidates' estimation (see Added), so selections can differ from earlier versions. `likelihood=`, `warm_corr=`, `tolerance=`, `max_iters=` and `ext_score_adjustment=` have no effect and raise a `FutureWarning`; they will be removed in a future release. `sampling=` now refits every candidate on each subsample (see Added), and its default is `None` (all the data). `'auto'`, the previous default, is accepted and means `None` (it subsampled only under the joint likelihood); previously `'dynamic'` and n evaluated the log-likelihoods of the full-data fits on a subsample of persons without refitting.
- **`RSM`/`PCM` `model_selection()` no longer uses person estimates or the joint likelihood**: the test follows the object's estimation (see Added), so selections can differ from earlier versions. `likelihood=`, `warm_corr=`, `tolerance=`, `max_iters=` and `ext_score_adjustment=` have no effect and raise a `FutureWarning`; they will be removed in a future release. `sampling=` now refits both models on each subsample: `sampling=n` (with `n_subsamples`; PCM is preferred if more than half the subsamples reject RSM), `'dynamic'` (the same with v1.4.0's size rule, n = min(20(I − 1)(m − 1), 1500)) or `'power'`; its default is `None` (all the data). `'auto'`, the previous default, is accepted and means `None` (it subsampled only under the joint likelihood). Previously `'dynamic'` and n evaluated the log-likelihoods of the full-data fits on a subsample of persons without refitting. With PAIR's `method='cos'`, `'ls'` or `'evm'`, `test='AIC'` or `'BIC'` raises a `ValueError` (the bootstrap test has no likelihood); use `test='LR'`. Under CML, a `ValueError` is raised if either model's CML fit removes an item, naming `estimation='cp-full-rcl'`, which keeps them.
- **Refits use the object's calibration settings:** `std_errors`' bootstrap resamples, the group models of `dif_test` (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`) and `global_fit` (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`) now use the settings of the object's last `calibrate()` call (`constant`, `method`, `matrix_power`, `log_lik_tol`, and for `RSM` `threshold_constant` and `estimation`), unless passed explicitly; previously they used their own defaults (`method='log-lik'`), and `global_fit` always used `calibrate()`'s defaults. `constant=None` is re-resolved for each refit's data. Results change for objects calibrated with non-default settings.
- **`MFRM.dif_test`'s joint omnibus test (`df_omnibus_table`) is withheld (now `None`):** its df did not count the facet effect parameters, and in null simulations it rejected in most datasets. It is to be revisited with conditional maximum likelihood.
- **Focal groups that cannot be compared with the reference group:** with more than two groups, the data can be connected through other groups while a focal group shares no items (or, for `MFRM`, too few facet elements) with the reference group. `dif_test` and `drf_test` now leave such groups out, with one `UserWarning`, record them in `dif_excluded_groups` / `drf_excluded_groups` with the reason, and give them NaN rows in the omnibus tables; a `ValueError` is raised if no focal group can be compared.
- **New `MFRM.dif_test` warnings for designs where a test was liberal in null simulations**, each naming the test to use instead: `model='matrix'` with groups scored by different facet elements (per-item DIF up to 0.16 with 600 persons per group; use `model='bivector'`, 0.06, if it fits), and `dif_omnibus_table` when the groups responded to different items (up to 0.23) or, with `estimation='pair-cpat'`, were scored by different facet elements (up to 0.87; use the per-item Wald tests in `dif_table`).
- **`MFRM.dif_test`'s known issue #17 warning (`estimation='pair-cpat'`) is now raised only when the groups were scored by different facet elements, and gives measured rates and the re-run:** in null simulations with strong facet element effects (twice the simulation defaults), `'pair-cpat'`'s compression produced per-item DIF up to 0.15 and DCF up to 0.13 at alpha = 0.05, against 0.04-0.08 with `estimation='cp-full-rcl'`; with fewer facet elements, more items or larger samples the two methods were similar. `'matrix'` is covered by a separate warning instead, since `'cp-full-rcl'` does not remove its bias.
- **Purification warnings are reported once:** `dif_test` and `drf_test` run the anchor selection once per tested item or facet element (leaving it out) and once for the whole set, and each run could raise the same warning, so with few shared items one message was repeated for every item. Each distinct warning is now raised once, with the number of leave-one-out runs it occurred in, and not at all when the whole-set selection gives the same warning.
- **Documented null-simulation rates** for the omnibus tests (`RSM`: 0.09-0.13 with 2 of 10 items shared, otherwise at or below nominal), `PCM.global_fit` under `method='cos'`/`'ls'`/`'evm'` (0.08-0.10; use `method='log-lik'`, 0.05-0.07), and `'matrix'`'s DRF threshold marginal (0.09-0.10 with 150 persons per group, nominal by 600).
- **Code formatting and lint clean-up, no functional changes:** the package is formatted with `black`, unused imports and variables are removed, and the CI lint job (`flake8` + `black --check`, pinned versions) now blocks. `E741` is ignored, so `I` can denote the number of items.
- **`check_anchor_homogeneity()`'s default is the joint-fit check above** with `estimation='cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` or `'cml'`. The earlier test, Cochran's Q on each anchor's mean effect with bootstrap standard errors, is `rater_homogeneity_test='cochran'` (or `'robust'`) and stays the default with `'pair-cpat'`; its arguments are unchanged, but `model=` and `anchors=` are now keyword arguments with defaults, and calls with Cochran-only arguments (e.g. `no_of_samples=`) under a joint-fit estimation must pass `rater_homogeneity_test='cochran'`. The Cochran test compares mean effects only: in simulations where anchors differed only in their pattern over items, it flagged the deviant anchor in 0–6% of datasets, and with no disagreement it rejected 13–14 of 100 datasets at alpha = 0.05.
- **`global_fit()`'s Wald test refers W to a multivariate Welch F distribution** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; Nel & van der Merwe, 1986): F = W (nu - df + 1) / (df nu) on (df, nu - df + 1), with nu = [tr(V^2) + tr(V)^2] / sum over the groups of [tr(V_g^2) + tr(V_g)^2] / (n_g - 1), so each group's covariance counts with its own number of persons. The previous reference (`welch=True`, the default) was W/df on F(df, n_s - df), n_s the smaller group's size; with `welch=False`, chi-square. In null simulations (`SLM`, `RSM` and `PCM`; 12 items, maximum score 3 for `RSM` and `PCM`; N = 200, 600 and 2,000; random and score splits and an exogenous split with groups of 30% and 70% of the persons; 500 datasets per design, 1,000 for `PCM`), the rejection rate at alpha = 0.05 was 2.4-7.3%. On the exogenous split, `PCM` at N = 200 rejected 4.6%, against 16.9% with the pooled two-sample Hotelling reference (df from n_1 + n_2) and 47.5% with chi-square; the previous reference rejected 10.5% for `PCM` on the random split at N = 200 and 600 (200 datasets each). The Krishnamoorthy & Yu (2004) degrees of freedom were also tried and rejected 7.4% there. The F reference allows for estimating the covariances from the groups' persons; it is an approximation, not an exact F test, since d is a difference of estimates and V a sandwich (or bootstrap) covariance. The summary's `denominator df` is nu - df + 1.
- **`global_fit(welch=...)` is deprecated** (`FutureWarning`; to be removed in a future version). `welch=True` still gives the previous reference; `welch=False` now gives the F reference above, since chi-square is no longer offered.
- **`dif_test()`'s Wald tests use Welch's t by default** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; item locations, `PCM`/`RSM` threshold structure and the DIF category column): the statistic diff / combined SE referred to a t-distribution with Welch-Satterthwaite degrees of freedom (the one-parameter case of `global_fit`'s reference), in a `df` column of `dif_table` and `threshold_dif_table`; the statistic's column is renamed from `z` to `t` (still `z` with `welch=False`). Previously the default was a normal reference (z). In null simulations (`SLM`, `RSM` and `PCM`; 12 items; reference/focal sizes from 50/50 to 900/300, including 3:1; 300 datasets per design), the rejection rate of DIF-free items at alpha = 0.05 was 3.8-6.2% with t and 3.8-6.6% with z, and power against a 0.5-logit shift fell by at most 1.6 percentage points; with at least 50 persons per group the degrees of freedom were about 80 or more, so t and z differ little. `dif_test(welch=...)` is deprecated (`FutureWarning`; to be removed in a future version); `welch=False` still gives z until then. `MFRM.dif_test()` is unchanged.
- **`calibrate_anchor(anchor_thresholds=...)`'s threshold-structure tests are Wald tests of the category widths** (`RSM`'s `anchor_threshold_test`, `PCM`'s per-item `anchor_threshold_test` and `anchor_threshold_test_aggregate`): W = d' V^+ d, d the differences between this dataset's widths and the given ones, V their covariance from `std_errors()` (the Kauermann-Carroll corrected sandwich by default, see below; the Hessian covariance with `se_method='hessian'`; the bootstrap covariance with bootstrap SEs), referred to Hotelling's F on persons - 1. They replace likelihood-ratio tests that plugged the PAIR thresholds and the given ones into a log-likelihood with persons re-estimated; their rejection rates rose with the sample size (null simulations at 0.05: `RSM` 4.4-8.4% at N 30-50 up to 24.8-27.6% at N 1,000; `PCM` per-item 5.3% at N 50 to 7.6% at N 1,000, aggregate 1.3% to 6.7%). The Wald tests reject 4.2-6.4% (`RSM`, max score 3 and 4, N 30-1,000, 500 datasets per cell) and, for `PCM` (12 items, max score 3, 300 datasets per N), 4.6-5.8% per item and 5.0-8.3% in aggregate at N 100-1,000; at N 50, 7.4% per item and 9.3% in aggregate (24 widths from 50 persons). The tables now report `W`, `df`, `F`, `denominator df` and `p`. `RSM`'s per-category `anchor_category_width_test` uses the same covariance and refers difference / SE to a t-distribution on persons - 1 (columns `t` and `df` in place of `z`): 3.7-6.8% at 0.05 (normal reference, uncorrected SEs: 4.5-8.0%).
- **`calibrate_anchor(warm_corr=, tolerance=, max_iters=, ext_score_adjustment=)` are deprecated** (`RSM`, `PCM`; `FutureWarning` if given; to be removed in a future version): they set the person estimation of the replaced likelihood-ratio tests and have no effect.
- **The item-restscore test of `fit_statistics(conditional=True)` is a conditional Monte Carlo test** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; `MFRM`'s item and facet element restscore tables): each observed gamma is referred to its distribution in `conditional_samples` (default 1000) datasets drawn from the model given each person's total over the items they responded to (exact and free of the person locations, at the thresholds the conditional bootstrap uses; no refit). New argument `restscore_p_method`: `'studentised'` (default; z = (observed - mean of the draws) / their SD, normal p-value) or `'monte-carlo'` ((1 + draws at least as far from their mean) / (1 + draws)). The tables add `Simulated mean`, `Simulated SD` and `z`; `Expected gamma` and `SE` (the observed gamma's asymptotic SE, as iarm's) stay as descriptive columns. The previous test (observed minus expected-table gamma over the asymptotic SE, normal p) was liberal at small N and conservative at large N: in null simulations (12 items, max score 3, 300 datasets per cell) it rejected 6.6-7.4% at 0.05 at N = 50 and 2.4-3.1% at N = 300 and 1,000, because the expected-table gamma is not the expected gamma at small N and the asymptotic SE ignores the conditioning on the totals (SD of z 0.86-0.88 at N = 1,000). On the same datasets the Monte Carlo test rejected 3.6-5.7% (studentised 3.8-5.5%; SD of z 0.97-1.03) at N = 50-1,000. A t reference would not have helped (it changed the rates by at most 0.5 points).
- **`std_errors()` reports sandwich standard errors with the Kauermann-Carroll small-sample correction by default: `std_errors(se_correction='kauermann-carroll')`** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`; every estimation with `se_method='sandwich'`, including CML's default sandwich of the conditional likelihood). The sandwich builds the variance from each person's contribution to the estimating equations at the estimates, which are chosen to make those contributions sum to zero, so in small samples it understates the variance, as a standard deviation with divisor N understates the population SD; the correction scales each person's contribution up by their leverage (Kauermann & Carroll, 2001), which for a mean is exactly the N − 1 divisor, and makes little difference in large samples. `se_correction=None` gives the uncorrected sandwich; `self.se_correction` records the correction applied (None for `se_method='hessian'` and the bootstrap). `item_se`, `threshold_se`, `cat_width_se`, `se_covariance` and everything that uses them (`dif_test()`, anchor selection, `calibrate_anchor()`'s threshold tests, `recovery_stats()`, `category_stats_df()`) follow. Confidence intervals from analytic standard errors are now estimate ± t × SE, t the quantile of the t-distribution on persons − 1 (previously the normal quantile). For CML the correction uses each person's conditional information, which depends only on their total and the items they responded to. The `RSM` correction under `'cp-full-rcl'` is also faster (N = 10,000, 20 items, maximum score 4: 35 s to 1 s, the same results to 2e-15). `global_fit()`'s `se_correction` now also applies to CML's sandwich. In null simulations (item locations from 10 or 20 items, 300 datasets per design, rejection at 0.05 of error / SE on t with persons − 1 degrees of freedom), the share flagged with 50 persons fell from 7.6% to 5.8–6.2% for `PCM` under `'cp-full-rcl'` (thresholds −1, 0, 0, 1) and from 5.7–6.0% to 5.1–5.4% under CML; `RSM` and `SLM` (50 to 1,000 persons) were 4.0–5.7% with either SE. `MFRM` has the correction too: `std_errors(se_correction='kauermann-carroll')` (the default) for every representation and `'mixed'`, under `'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'` and `'cml'`, with `self.se_correction` and `self.se_correction_{model}` recording it, and its intervals are estimate ± t × SE on persons − 1 as well. Each person's contribution to the information counts all the comparisons their ratings enter, between items scored by one facet element and between facet elements scoring one item (for the stretch models and `'mixed'`, its Gauss-Newton part); it has rank at most the number of those comparisons, so the correction is computed from a small eigendecomposition per distinct response pattern. The tests built on the sandwich use it as well -- `per_rater_model_selection()`'s and `model_selection()`'s composite tests, `drf_test()`, `dif_test()`, `global_fit(method='andersen')` for composite fits, `check_anchor_homogeneity()`, `divergence_test()` and the z of `global_fit_obsexp`, `global_fit_obsexp_facets` and `global_fit_obsexp_cells` -- unless the last `std_errors()` call gave `se_correction=None`; the AIC and BIC penalties, which estimate a trace rather than a variance, are left uncorrected. In null simulations (5 items, maximum score 3; 5 facet elements fully crossed with 30 or 100 persons, or 10 facet elements each scoring 30% of 100 or 300 persons; `'global'`, `'thresholds'`, `'matrix'` and `'centrality'` under the three estimations; 300 datasets per design, `recovery_stats()` at alpha = 0.05), the correction lowered or kept every share flagged, by up to 2.6 percentage points: `'matrix'` facet element effects from 9.7-10.4% to 8.2-8.6% with 30 crossed persons (`'cp-full-rcl'`, `'cpat-rcl'`) and from 12.1-13.1% to 9.8-10.5% in the sparse design with 100 persons; item locations under `'matrix'` from 6.7-7.2% to 5.2-5.7% in those designs. With 100 crossed persons the changes were 0.7 points or less. Excess flagging that remains with the correction -- `'matrix'`'s shared thresholds in the sparse designs (5.7-15.7%) and the `'centrality'` thresholds under RCL in the sparse designs (12.1-26.3%, from the 127 and 197 of 300 datasets where the sandwich applied) -- has other causes, not yet investigated.
- **`recovery_stats()` tests item-side parameters with t on persons − 1, and `category_stats_df()`'s `Prop disordered` with analytic standard errors is a t probability** (`SLM`, `PCM`, `RSM`, `MFRM`). For items, thresholds, category widths and (`MFRM`) facet elements, error / SE is referred to a t-distribution on persons − 1 degrees of freedom (columns `t` and `df` in place of `z`); person rows keep `z` against the normal distribution. The SEs are those of `std_errors()` (now the Kauermann-Carroll corrected sandwich by default, see below). With analytic standard errors, `Prop disordered` is P(width < 0) from that t-distribution centred on the estimate, with the width's SE (previously the normal probability); with bootstrap standard errors it is still the share of resamples with a negative width. In null simulations (`PCM` and `RSM` with `'cp-full-rcl'`, thresholds −1, 0, 0, 1; 10 or 20 items; 300 datasets per design), the share of item locations flagged at alpha = 0.05 with 50 persons fell from 8.2–8.3% to 5.8–6.2% for `PCM` and from 5.9–6.4% to 5.0–5.3% for `RSM` (4.8–5.5% before and 4.5–5.3% after with 100 to 1,000 persons), and of `PCM` category widths from 8.1–8.2% to 5.9%. At the category whose true width is 0, `PCM`'s `Prop disordered` was below 0.05 in 7.8–8.4% of datasets with 50 persons before and 6.7% after, and above 0.95 in 5.7% before and 4.2–4.8% after (5% expected for each).
- **The Kauermann-Carroll correction is computed with batched matrix products** instead of `numpy.einsum` (`_kc_scores`, used by `global_fit()`'s default `se_correction`): the same results to 1e-14, and much faster (`PCM`, N = 2,000: 0.3 s per covariance).
- **`MFRM`'s Kauermann-Carroll correction builds each person's information through the grid cells the person's comparisons touch** when the person has more comparisons than cells (e.g. fully crossed designs), instead of from every comparison: the same result to rounding, and about a fifth less time for the correction in the Mixed MFRM example notebook.

## [1.4.0] - 2026-10-15

### Added
- **RCL-CPAT (regularised composite likelihood CPAT) estimation for `RSM` and `MFRM`** (`calibrate(estimation='cpat-rcl')`; the default `'cp-full-rcl'` extends it to the full pattern set, below). CPAT conditions on response patterns with equal totals: odd totals give the item comparisons within each facet element (and, for `MFRM`, the facet element comparisons within each item), even totals the threshold patterns ((k, k) against (k-1, k+1) on two items). RCL-CPAT fits all of them jointly, as one composite likelihood, by Newton-Raphson, with additive smoothing constants on the pattern counts and, for `MFRM`, ridge penalties on the facet element parameters. `RSM` is the single-facet-element case of the same estimator. `estimation='pair-cpat'` is the method used before v1.4.0 (Elliott & Buttery, 2022b); it reproduces v1.3.1's estimates exactly for `RSM`, and for `MFRM` apart from the threshold-stage fix below; the choice persists across refits of the same object and is carried into bootstrap, DIF, DRF and global-fit refits. `method=`, `matrix_power=`, `log_lik_tol=`, `threshold_method=` (`RSM`) and `matrix_marginals=`/`robust=` (`MFRM`) apply to `'pair-cpat'` only; other values passed with `'cpat-rcl'` are ignored, with a `UserWarning`. In simulation (several thousand datasets across crossed and incomplete rating designs), RCL-CPAT's median RMSE for item locations, thresholds and facet element parameters was equal to or lower than `'pair-cpat'`'s in every sub-model and design studied, and it removed the compression of item locations and thresholds that `'pair-cpat'` showed under the `items` sub-model (median item slope 0.88); person estimates were mostly unchanged. It is slower (milliseconds to a second for typical designs; several seconds for the `matrix` sub-model with many facet elements, items and categories).
- **Full-pattern RCL (`estimation='cp-full-rcl'`), now the default for `RSM` and `MFRM`.** For two items (scored by the same facet element), or two facet elements (scoring the same item), every pair of response patterns (k, l) and (k−1, l+1) with the same total is a binomial comparison in which the person cancels. CPAT's patterns are the subset with a difference of one between the two responses; the other patterns are most informative for item pairs far apart in difficulty, where CPAT's patterns are lopsided. All are fitted jointly by the same penalised composite likelihood as RCL-CPAT. Smoothing constants go on the item, facet element and CPAT threshold pattern counts only; the other pattern counts are left unsmoothed (smoothing them pulled thresholds together, most at high K). Patterns involving an unused category are left out. `estimation='cpat-rcl'` keeps the CPAT-only version (its estimates are unchanged). Sandwich standard errors cover both. In simulation: for `RSM` across 194 random designs (I 5–40, N 60–3,000, K 2–8, up to 60% missing), median threshold RMSE 9.5% below RCL-CPAT's (median paired ratio 0.905), at parity with CML (psychotools; 1.016) and with the lightest tail of the three (99th percentile 0.57 logits, against 0.88 for RCL-CPAT and 1.25 for CML); for `MFRM` `global` across 155 random designs, thresholds 12% below RCL-CPAT (0.880) and below TAM's MML (0.873), facet element effects 20% below RCL-CPAT (0.800) and at parity with TAM (1.005); the extended sub-models gained 3–10% on the full location grid, apart from `matrix` (no change). Sandwich SE/SD 0.94–1.13 and 95% coverage 0.93–0.97 across the 8 sub-models. `model_selection()` classification was unchanged or slightly better (worst-case correct-choice rate over generating sub-models 0.825 against 0.804). Fitting takes longer: about 10% in `model_selection()` at typical sizes, but 2–5 times longer at very large ones, mostly because facet element pairs now contribute all their patterns (e.g. `RSM` with 3,000 items and 100,000 persons: calibration 250 s against 146 s for `'cpat-rcl'`, sandwich standard errors 619 s against 157 s; `MFRM` with 200 raters, 50 items and 20,000 persons: calibration 60–160 s against 12–35 s, sandwich standard errors 3–20 minutes against about 1). `estimation='cpat-rcl'` remains available where speed matters most.
- **`MFRM.calibrate()` regularisation arguments** for RCL estimation (`'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'`), each in its sub-model's own units: `ridge=` (default 0.3; `global`, `items`, `thresholds`, `bivector`, and the bivector parts of `matrix`) on the facet element deviations in logits; `interaction_ridge=` (default 3; `matrix`) on the item × threshold interactions beyond the bivector parts; `stretch_ridge=` (default 1; `centrality`, `pseudo_halo`, `bistretch`) on (omega − 1)². One passed to a sub-model it does not apply to is ignored, with a `UserWarning`. Defaults chosen by simulation. The stretch models are fitted from two starts from a `'pair-cpat'` fit (its stretch parameters, and omega = 1), keeping the higher composite likelihood.
- **Thin and empty category widths in RCL-CPAT** (`RSM` and `MFRM`): a width with fewer than 20 threshold patterns, or with all its patterns on one side, is pulled toward the mean of the other widths by a ridge of 0.5 · (1 − T/20) (0.5 if one-sided); widths next to an empty middle category, which their own patterns do not identify, are left out of the joint fit and estimated by the `cpat-cml` rule of `RSM.threshold_set()` given the joint fit's item locations. The existing "no information" and "poorly informed" width warnings still apply.
- **Analytic (sandwich) standard errors for `RSM` under RCL-CPAT**, the default in `std_errors()` as before: the bread is the negative Hessian of the penalised composite log-likelihood at the estimates, the meat the sum over persons of their score outer products (with the Kauermann-Carroll correction where `global_fit()` asks for it). On simulated data they agreed with the bootstrap to within a few percent.
- **Analytic (sandwich) standard errors for `MFRM` under RCL-CPAT, now the default in `std_errors()`** (`se_method=None`; `'sandwich'`/`'bootstrap'` to choose). The covariance of the fitted parameters is a sandwich (bread: negative Hessian of the penalised composite log-likelihood at the estimates; meat: sum over persons of their score outer products), and the standard errors of every reported quantity -- item locations, thresholds, category widths, facet element effects and their marginals, the stretch parameters and their covariances -- follow by the delta method, anchored or not; intervals are estimate ± z · SE. In simulation (300 replicate datasets for each of the 8 sub-models, crossed and double-marking designs), the median ratio of the sandwich SE to the sampling SD of the estimates was 0.98–1.06 for item locations, thresholds and stretch parameters, with median 95% coverage 0.94–0.97 (around the mean estimate). It takes milliseconds rather than the seconds to minutes of 500 recalibrations. The bootstrap is still used for `'pair-cpat'` sub-models, `'mixed'`, `store_bootstrap=True` (DIF, DRF and the divergence test, which use the fitted resamples), and data with an unused score category (with a `UserWarning`). `self.se_method_{model}` records the method used.
- **`self.convergence`** (`RSM`, `MFRM`; `MFRM` also `convergence_{model}`): Newton-Raphson iterations and whether the RCL fit (`'cp-full-rcl'` or `'cpat-rcl'`) converged, with a `UserWarning` if it did not. `None` under `'pair-cpat'`.
- **`MFRM.dif_test()` and `MFRM.drf_test()`**: Differential Item/Category Functioning (DIF/DCF) and Differential Rater Functioning (DRF) tests by an exogenous variable, under any of the 8 facet element parameterisations (`model=`). `dif_test()` follows `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`'s design: item-location DIF (per-item Wald test by default, optional per-item likelihood-ratio test and ETS A/B/C categories) plus DCF via category widths, as in `RSM`, with a new `dcf_cap` argument (default `2000`, in persons) for `size_adjust=True`. There are two omnibus tests: `dif_omnibus_table` tests items only, with facet effects held at their pooled values in both groups (`df` = shared items − 1); `df_omnibus_table` tests items and facet effects together. `drf_test()` is new: the facet-element-location analogue of DIF. Its tests depend on `model`: one Wald test per facet element for `global`; a separate test of each facet element's effect on each item or threshold for `items`/`thresholds`/`bivector`/`matrix`; and one joint test of each facet element's shift and stretch parameters (T = Δ'Σ⁻¹Δ, with Σ from paired bootstrap draws) for `centrality`/`pseudo_halo`/`bistretch`. Only facet elements that scored at least `min_persons_per_group` persons in both groups are tested. Both methods purify the scale before testing (leave-one-out, see the purification fix below). With `estimation='pair-cpat'`, `dif_test()` warns under the 6 parameterisations at risk of compression of item locations and thresholds (known issue #17); `global`, `items` and `pseudo_halo` carry no warning. Manual updated (new `dif_test`/`drf_test` sections in the MFRM chapter).
- **Formal null-calibration and power validation for MFRM's DIF/DRF omnibus tests** (known issue #38): the `dif_omnibus_table` and DRF omnibus tests was checked by simulation across all 8 rater models (100-rep null, 20-rep power) and found conservative under the null (upper 95% confidence bound ≈3% against nominal 5%) with decisive power (20/20 rejections at a 1.5-logit shift). The DIF/DRF purification's own Type I calibration (known issue #39) was separately validated by simulation and found accurate once corrected — see **Fixed** below.
- **`recovery_stats()`**, across `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` — a ground-truth parameter-recovery diagnostic for simulation studies. Compares generating (known, simulated) parameter values against recovered (calibrated) estimates on a common scale, reporting bias/RMSE/correlation per parameter family (items, thresholds, persons, and, for `MFRM`, rater facet_effects) plus a summary recovery plot. Cross-model support (`anchor=`/`groups=` for e.g. rater-level breakdowns). Worked example notebooks added for all 4 model classes.
- **`wright_map(threshold_type=...)`**, on `PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` (not applicable to the dichotomous `SLM`). - **`missing_method=` on every simulation class** (`SLM_Sim`, `RSM_Sim`, `PCM_Sim`, `MFRM_Sim` and its wrappers). `'bernoulli'` (default, unchanged) makes each response missing independently with probability `missing`, so the realised proportion varies around it (0.284–0.313 across 200 seeds for `missing=0.3` on 20 × 500 cells). `'exact'` makes exactly `round(missing * cells)` responses missing: per facet element for `MFRM_Sim(shared_missing=False)`, or in the one shared pattern otherwise. It reuses the same random draws, taking the cells with the smallest missing-data uniforms, so observed scores are identical to `'bernoulli'`, the two missing patterns differ only in the cells whose uniforms lie nearest the cut-off, and seeded results are reproducible either way.

- **`RSM.calibrate(threshold_method=...)`**, choosing the estimator for the threshold stage. `'cpat-cml'` (the new default) estimates each distance between adjacent thresholds by conditional maximum likelihood: given that a person's responses to two different items are `(k, k)` or `(k-1, k+1)`, the person location cancels and the count of `(k, k)` patterns is binomial with log-odds `tau_{k+1} - tau_k - (delta_i - delta_j)`, the item difference entering as a known offset. `'cpat-info-weighted'` is the previous estimator (Elliott & Buttery, 2022b, eq. 4.18), the harmonic-mean information-weighted mean of the per-pair log-odds. Because its weights are computed from the same observed proportions as the log-odds they weight, that estimator compresses the scale in thin data (median recovery slope 0.93 on small, sparse designs), while `'cpat-cml'` does not (1.00). In simulation (3,840 well-behaved datasets: `K` 2–8, category widths 0.6–3.0 logits, `N` 100–50,000, 5–60 items, 0–60% missing), `'cpat-cml'` (there with smoothing constant 0.01) halved the 90th percentile of threshold RMSE (0.195 against 0.432 logits), with identical results at large `N`; a later small-`N` sweep (`N` 50–500) put its best constant at 0.03, the shipped default for both estimators. A distance whose comparison patterns are observed fewer than 20 times in total falls back to the information-weighted estimator, which was more robust at that level (out of sample, this cut the worst person-measurement error on very thin data from 2.6 to 1.0 logits without changing the 90th percentile).
- **Handling of empty middle categories and uninformative threshold distances in `RSM`.** When a middle category has no responses, the three distances touching it are not identified, and a flat smoothing constant let their artefacts distort the whole scale (median person-measurement error 0.76 logits, 90th percentile 1.63, on 576 simulated datasets with one empty category). These distances now get a targeted constant, `lambda` times the mean count per item pair, with `lambda` a power function of the estimated width of the other categories (from a quick fit with the empty category merged when no other distance is available), fitted by simulation and validated out of sample: median 0.25, 90th percentile 0.60 on the same datasets. A `UserWarning` explains that only the point where the category response curves of the categories either side intersect is identified, and sets out the options (collapse or rescore if the category was never a genuine option or may be miscoded; for `max_score=2`, where the items are effectively dichotomous, analyse as dichotomous instead). A distance with no comparison patterns at all is set to the mean of the others with a warning (new `no_info='mean'`), or raises (`no_info='raise'`); previously it always raised. Poorly informed distances (fewer than 20 patterns) also warn. Regression tests added.
- **Analytic (sandwich) standard errors for `RSM`, now the default in `std_errors()`.** A sandwich covariance for the stacked estimating equations of both calibration stages -- the Bradley-Terry equations of the PAIR item stage and the `cpat-cml` equations of the threshold stage -- with persons as the independent units, so item-location uncertainty is carried into the thresholds. In simulation (6 designs: `N` 150–2,000, 6 or 15 items, `K` 3 or 5, 0% or 30% missing; 200 replicate datasets each), the mean standard error divided by the true sampling standard deviation was 0.97–1.08 for thresholds and 0.97–1.02 for item locations, matching the bootstrap to within 0.01 in every design; ignoring the person-level dependence (the naive likelihood SE) gave 0.39–0.56, and ignoring the item stage 0.78–0.95. It takes about as long as one calibration (0.18 s for 50,000 persons and 40 items, against about 60 s for a 500-resample bootstrap). In very thin data the sandwich understated threshold standard errors by up to 25% (N = 40, 4 items, 40% missing) while the bootstrap overstated them by up to 39%. With the sandwich, confidence intervals are estimate ± z·SE, `category_stats_df()`'s disorder proportion is the normal-approximation probability of a negative category width, the bootstrap draw attributes are `None`, and `self.se_covariance` holds the item and category-width covariance matrices; `self.se_method` records the method used. Regression test added.
- **Analytic (sandwich) standard errors for `SLM` and `PCM` too**, now the default in `std_errors()`, as for `RSM`: a sandwich covariance for the Bradley-Terry estimating equations of PAIR, with persons as the independent units -- on the item pairwise matrix for `SLM`, and on the item-category pairwise matrix for `PCM`, whose central locations, centred thresholds and category widths are linear functions of the uncentred thresholds, so their covariances follow exactly. `SLM`: mean standard error divided by the true sampling standard deviation 0.96–1.01 across six designs (200 replicate datasets each; `N` 40–2,000, 6–40 items, 0–50% missing, including thin data), against 0.94–1.02 for the bootstrap. `PCM`: 0.99–1.00 when all categories are used; with a category nobody used it underestimates (0.90 for thresholds, 0.79 for item locations), so `se_method=None` (default) falls back to the bootstrap whenever any item has an unused category. `SLM` defaults to `se_method='sandwich'`. - **`dif_test()` uses the analytic standard errors too** (`SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`), whenever each group's model uses them. - **`global_fit()`**, on `SLM`/`RSM`/`PCM`: a global test of parameter invariance across two groups of persons, with `method=` (`'wald'`, the default; `'andersen-lr'`, reserved for the planned CML estimator, raises `NotImplementedError` with PAIR/CPAT estimates, which do not maximise the likelihood it evaluates; `'martin-lof'` planned), `split=` (`'score'`, the default median split of mean observed score; `'person_location'`; `'random'`; or `'exogenous'` with `covariate=`), `welch=True`, `alpha=0.05` and `split_prop=0.5` (the proportion of persons in each outer group for the `'score'` and `'person_location'` splits: 0.5 is a median split, 1/3 compares the outer thirds as in RUMM, 0.4 lies in between; the middle group is left out). The Wald test compares the two groups' separately calibrated estimates (re-centred) using their sandwich covariances, W = d'(V_1 + V_2)^+ d, referred with `welch=True` to an F distribution with denominator degrees of freedom from the smaller group. Parameters not estimable in both groups -- thresholds next to a category unused in one group, and (`RSM`) category widths needing a fallback estimator -- are excluded and reported (`global_fit_excluded`, with a warning). This restores a valid score-split test for PAIR/CPAT: `andersen_lr_test()`'s score and person-location splits were disabled in 2026-07 because the likelihood ratio, evaluated at estimates that do not maximise that likelihood, had no null calibration or power. Each group's sandwich covariance uses the Kauermann-Carroll small-sample correction (`se_correction='kauermann-carroll'`, the default; `None` for the uncorrected sandwich): each person's score contribution is scaled by (I − H_n)^(−1/2), H_n being that person's leverage, which removes the downward bias of the sandwich to first order. In simulation (300 null datasets per design; N = 600 and 2,000; score, random and exogenous splits) the mean of W/df was 0.98–1.05 in every design, and the rejection rate at α = 0.05 was 4–7% for `SLM` and `RSM`, and 5–7% for `PCM` except with about 300 persons per group on the exogenous split (10%; W/df's mean was right there, but its spread somewhat wider than the F reference's). Without the correction, `PCM`'s score split with about 300 persons per group rejected 8.7% (mean W/df 1.07); with it, 6.3% (1.03). Of the alternatives tried, the n/(n − p) correction overcorrected random splits (1.8%) and Mancl-DeRouen the score split (2.8%). With two of 12 items shifted by 0.5 logits between groups of 300, power was 59% (`SLM`), 91% (`RSM`) and 88% (`PCM`). On the same data, the existing likelihood ratio test (exogenous split) was conservative for `RSM` (mean statistic 17–37% of its degrees of freedom; rejection 0–0.3%) and `PCM` (38–86%; 0.5–3.8%). Results: `global_fit_summary`, `global_fit_table` (each compared parameter's difference, SE and z), `global_fit_excluded`, `global_fit_groups`. `andersen_lr_test()` is deprecated in favour of `global_fit()` (`FutureWarning`). An oracle check of an empirical Bartlett correction for the likelihood ratio test (exogenous split, 300 null and 300 DIF datasets per design; scaling by the true null mean, estimated on separate datasets) ruled it out: for `RSM` the statistic was exactly 0 in 42–46% of null datasets, so the rescaled test rejected 26%; for `PCM` its variance was 25% above a scaled chi-square's (rejection 6.7–7.3%). Regression test added.

Its item-location RMSE, as a ratio to the best constant for each design, is 1.139 at the 95th percentile and 1.469 at worst, against 1.306 and 3.945 for `0.1`. ### Known issues (fix planned for 1.4.1)
- **`RSM` sandwich standard errors for thresholds are somewhat too small with few persons and many categories** (e.g. N = 150, K = 6: SE/SD 0.70–0.92, 95% coverage 0.90; fine by N = 300). The same under `'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'`. `std_errors(se_method='bootstrap')` is an alternative in such designs.
- **Results computed earlier are not refreshed after recalibrating with different settings.** For example, after `calibrate()`, `item_stats_df()`, `calibrate(constant=2.0)`, `item_stats_df()`, the second table shows the new estimates alongside the earlier standard errors and fit statistics, and person estimates keep their earlier values. - **Standard errors computed implicitly ignore the calibration settings actually used.** When a stats method runs the bootstrap itself, it uses its own default estimator settings rather than those passed to `calibrate()`, e.g. after `calibrate(constant=2.0)` the SEs are those of the default constant. Pass the same settings to the stats method, or call `std_errors()` with them explicitly.
- **`MFRM` anchored and unanchored standard errors can differ by resampling noise alone.** With the defaults (`store_bootstrap=False`, `seed=None`), `anchor_std_errors()` draws fresh resamples, so SEs that should be identical under a pure shift (`global`, `items`, `thresholds`, `matrix`, `bivector`) differed by up to 16% in testing. ### Fixed
- **`MFRM` sandwich standard errors with an unused lowest or highest score category.** Only empty middle categories made `std_errors()` fall back to the bootstrap, so a threshold next to an unused extreme category, which the data do not inform, got a sandwich standard error set by the width penalty. Any unused category now triggers the fallback, with a `UserWarning`, as in `RSM`. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM` standard errors of extreme facet elements are now NaN** (both methods, anchored or not): a facet element that used only the lowest or only the highest category has a location the data do not identify, so it had a normal-looking but meaningless standard error. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.dif_test()`'s known issue #17 warning is now raised only with `estimation='pair-cpat'`.** The compression it warns about comes from `'pair-cpat'`'s separate estimation stages; under `'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'` items, thresholds and facet element parameters are estimated jointly. Regression test added.
- **`calibrate_anchor(calibrate=True)` could not set the estimation method.** In `RSM` and `MFRM` it did not accept `estimation=`, so the calibration it ran always used the object's last method (or `'cp-full-rcl'`). It now accepts `estimation=` (and, in `RSM`, `threshold_method=`; in `MFRM`, `matrix_marginals=`, `ridge=`, `interaction_ridge=` and `stretch_ridge=`) and passes them to `calibrate()`. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.global_fit()` raised an obscure `AttributeError`**: the method is inherited but not implemented for MFRM. It now raises `NotImplementedError`, pointing to `dif_test()`. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.rater_stats_df(divergence_test=...)` raised `AttributeError` for the stretch sub-models under RCL estimation.** The test compares each bootstrap resample's stretch fit with its parent fit (`thresholds` for `centrality`, `items` for `pseudo_halo`, `bivector` for `bistretch`), which `pair-cpat` produces as a by-product but RCL does not, since it fits the stretch model directly. The parent is now calibrated on each resample (anchored, if requested, with the full-data anchor adjustment), keeping the comparison paired, and on a copy of the object, so its latest-calibration attributes are unchanged. Regression test added.
- **Anchored `MFRM.per_rater_model_selection()` evaluated the anchored facet effects against the unanchored item locations and thresholds.** Anchoring moves the anchors' mean effects into the item locations and thresholds, so every facet element's log-likelihoods were computed at locations shifted by the anchors' mean pattern, and the stretch sub-models were derived against the wrong reference values. This pushed facet elements towards the stretch sub-models, increasingly so for larger facet effects: in the design of the rater model selection example (20 raters, 4 flat anchors; 30 replicates), accuracy at N = 2,000 rose from 0.76 to 0.96, and at N = 500 from 0.62 to 0.87. The facet effects of the resulting `'mixed'` model (including in bootstrap resamples) are corrected in the same way. Present since the method was introduced. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()`'s dynamic person subsample is now T = min(20R(I − 1)(K − 1), 1500)** (K = `max_score`; K − 1 taken as 1 for dichotomous data). It was min(20(I − 1)(R − 1), 1500), an incorrect port of `per_rater_model_selection()`'s per-facet-element rule min(20(I − 1)(K − 1), 1500); the new rule is that rule over all R facet elements, mirroring the RSM vs PCM rule with each (facet element, item) pair treated as an item. The old rule ignored K and was too small for the high-df comparisons: in a classification study (4,480 datasets from all eight sub-models; I = 4, 10; K = 3, 5; R = 4, 10; N = 100–6,400), the mean worst-case correct-choice rate for AIC + sig. test was 0.83 against 0.82 at default effect sizes and 0.64 against 0.45 at 0.4 × default (worst design 0.40 against 0.00), mainly from bivector data being assigned to items. The 1,500 cap still protects the low-df comparisons: without subsampling, centrality data at N = 6,400 were assigned to a more complex sub-model 25–30% of the time.
- **`MFRM`'s `'pair-cpat'` threshold stage took the log of sums rather than the sum of logs.** Eq. 4.54 of Elliott & Buttery (2022b) averages, for each item pair, the per-facet-element log-ratios of the CPAT patterns, so that the facet element terms cancel; `_threshold_distance()` summed the counts over facet elements first and took one log-ratio, which attenuates the distance whenever facet elements differ (logit non-collapsibility) and compressed the threshold scale. It now follows eq. 4.54: per-facet-element log-ratios (smoothing constant per facet element cell) averaged over the facet elements with patterns for the pair, item pairs weighted by their information from the summed counts. In simulation (60 datasets per sub-model, crossed and double-marking, default threshold constant 0.3), median threshold RMSE fell from 0.29 to 0.11 under `items`, 0.25 to 0.10 under `bivector`, 0.057 to 0.049 under `global` and 0.080 to 0.070 under `thresholds` (median slopes 0.96, 0.95, 0.99, 0.98). `'pair-cpat'` MFRM threshold estimates (and everything downstream of them) change accordingly. Present since v1.0.0.
- **CPAT threshold estimation counted same-item cells** in `RSM` and `MFRM`. The comparison matrices are built by multiplying indicator matrices, which also fills the diagonal (a person scoring `k` on item `i` "paired" with the same response). CPAT (Elliott & Buttery, 2022b, eq. 4.18) sums only over pairs of different items; the diagonal entered the numerator but not the denominator, inflating the `(k, k)` side. The diagonal is now excluded. Present since v1.0.0. Removing it exposed the information-weighted estimator's compression in thin data, addressed by `threshold_method` (see Added). `RSM` and `MFRM` regression fixtures regenerated.
- **Warm person estimates are now solved directly, fixing extreme scores**, across `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` (`person`, `person_estimates`, `score_lookup`, `score_lookup_table`, and everything built on them). With `warm_corr=True`, the estimate was the ML estimate plus the one-step correction `J / (2 I^2)` evaluated at it. That is a first-order approximation, and it fails where the information `I` is close to zero, which for extreme scores is exactly where the adjusted ML estimate lies: the smaller `ext_score_adjustment`, the further out the ML estimate and the larger the overshoot. On a 10-item, `K = 3` `RSM`, a score of 0 gave −4.10 logits at the default adjustment of 0.5, −1.76 at 0.1, +8.69 at 0.03 and +40.92 at 0.01, against −5.16 for the root of Warm's equation. The same mechanism produced isolated non-extreme blow-ups when thresholds were badly disordered (one person moved −1,166.6 logits from a sensible ML estimate of −0.71). Estimates are now the root of the weighted likelihood equation `r − E(θ) + J(θ) / (2 I(θ)) = 0`, bracketed and refined by Newton steps with a bisection fallback. That root is finite for every score, including 0 and the maximum, so `ext_score_adjustment` now only affects plain ML (`warm_corr=False`) and the starting value. `person()` and `score_lookup()` agree for extreme scores in all four classes. Non-extreme persons move little (median shift 0.001–0.01 logits, at most 0.10, on the 6-item, 200-person `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM` regression datasets); extreme persons move outwards (on the `SLM` regression data, from −1.95/+2.17 to −3.06/+3.34 logits at the default adjustment), which also changes person-based statistics such as reliability, fit and the Andersen test. The public `warm()` methods still return the one-step correction term. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.check_anchor_homogeneity(model='bistretch')` silently skipped the per-component breakdown.** The model passed validation and the overall Cochran's Q / robust test ran correctly, but `bistretch` had no entry in the component list, so `anchor_homogeneity_per_component` was `None` even with `per_component=True`. It now gets one row per (item, threshold) cell, like `bivector`, whose full-vector layout it shares. Regression test added.
- **Docstrings of the stats-table methods said `Returns: None`**; they now describe the returned table.
- **`RSM` raised a pandas deprecation warning on construction** (`isnull().all(1)`: positional arguments to `all` become keyword-only in pandas 4.0); now `all(axis=1)`.
- **`SLM.calibrate()`'s additive smoothing only ever filled structural zero cells, unlike `PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM`, which also add `constant` to both directions of every pair with at least one observed comparison ("full smoothing").** Paired simulation across item count, sample size, item spread and missingness found full smoothing reduces item-recovery RMSE at small-to-moderate N, converging to a wash at large N, consistent across `SLM`/`RSM`/`MFRM`; `SLM` now matches the other three. `PCM`'s existing full smoothing was checked the same way and found inconsistent (favourable, neutral or unfavourable depending on `max_score`/`item_range`/`person_sd`, no clean predictive rule) — left as the default, to revisit later. `SLM` regression fixtures regenerated; bare `calibrate()` output shifts slightly versus prior releases.
- **DIF/DRF purification's leave-one-out translation constant under-covered its own sampling variance (known issue #39), across all 4 model classes.** The translation constant (`tc`) used to place a focal group's item locations (or, for MFRM's DRF, rater severities) onto the reference group's scale was computed once, from the *whole* anchor set, and then reused unchanged when testing every individual item/rater against that same anchor set — a self-referential circularity when the anchor set is small (each anchored unit contributed to its own reference point), and the reported standard error did not account for `tc` itself being an estimated quantity with its own sampling variance. `_purification_loo_tc()` (shared across `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM`) now computes a leave-one-out `tc` per unit (excluding that unit's own contribution), with its variance estimated directly from each group's own stored bootstrap resamples (falling back to an independence-assumption closed form when bootstrap samples aren't available). In simulation, the previous formula under-covered Type I error by roughly 2x nominal in DRF's typically-small rater panels; a first-pass fix (leave-one-out `tc` alone, without the bootstrap-based variance) only partially closed the gap (10.3% → 9.5%, still ~2x nominal), confirming the two components (leave-one-out, and `tc`'s own variance) both mattered. `dif_table`/`threshold_dif_table` output values (`Focal (purified)`, `Difference`, `SE`, `z`, `p` and downstream flags) change versus prior releases on `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`; regression fixtures regenerated. `MFRM`'s `dif_table`/`drf_table` already reflect the corrected formula, since neither had shipped before this fix.
- **`MFRM.rater_stats_df(model='mixed')` silently omitted per-rater `Estimate`/`SE` columns** (known issue #36) instead of raising or warning. Added a `mixed` branch to the group-building dispatch, mirroring `matrix`'s full item×threshold cell path, plus a new "Rater model" column reporting each rater's assigned representation (`self.rater_models`). `marginal=True` (the argument's own default, inherited from the other rater models) now raises a `UserWarning` and falls back to the full table for `model='mixed'`, since no single restricted per-rater marginal exists when raters sit under different representations. `CHANGELOG.md`'s v1.1.0 entry (below) also previously overstated `model='mixed'` support at that release — corrected there.
- **`dif_test(size_adjust=True)`'s threshold/category-width (DCF) component no longer shares `reference_n` with item-location DIF.** Direct simulation (I=15, `max_score` in {3, 5, 8}, a real 0.8-logit category-width shift, N in {500,...,6000}/group) showed the previous design — a single fixed `reference_n` pinning both components' rescaled SE to "as if N=reference_n" — made DCF power collapse to ~0% by N≈1500/group, while item-location DIF showed no comparable blind spot in the same range: threshold information doesn't scale with raw person count the way item-location information does (a category-width estimate's precision depends on how many people land in specific, thin adjacent-category cells, not on the group's total N), so no single fixed `reference_n` could serve both components at once. `dif_table` (item-location) is unchanged — it keeps `reference_n` (default `100`) exactly as before. `threshold_dif_table` (DCF, both `PCM` and `RSM`) now uses a new **`dcf_cap`** argument instead: a per-cell (per item-threshold for `PCM`; per shared threshold for `RSM`) dynamic cap on that cell's own harmonic-mean information weight — `PCM._pairwise_harmonic_weights()` (row-summed harmonic mean of the raw pairwise comparison matrix and its transpose, a Bradley–Terry Fisher-information analogue) and `RSM._threshold_total_weights()` (CPAT's own `total_weight`, the same harmonic-mean weighting `_threshold_distance` already uses; Elliott & Buttery, 2022b) respectively. Below the cap, a cell's own bootstrap SE is used as-is (no adjustment at all, unlike `reference_n`'s unconditional rescale); above it, SE is inflated by `sqrt(weight/dcf_cap)`, preserving the original protective intent (guarding against trivially small effects becoming "significant" at very large N) without the across-the-board conservatism that caused the power collapse. Defaults (`dcf_cap=15000` for `PCM`, `dcf_cap=80000` for `RSM`, reflecting `RSM`'s larger pooled-across-items weight scale) were calibrated by simulation and found stable across every `max_score` tested (3, 5, 8). `size_adjust=False` (the default) is completely unaffected. Supersedes the `reference_n` docstring's earlier "prefer `size_adjust=False` for category-width DIF" caution, added in a previous release pending this fix.

### Fixed
- **`PCM._build_pairwise_matrix()` counted same-item threshold cells.** The joint score-category frequency matrix is built over one-hot category blocks concatenated across all items, and cells linking two thresholds of the *same* item were left populated by that construction — a person has one score per item, so such a cell is a category-frequency count relabelled as a pairwise comparison, not a genuine one (and for non-adjacent thresholds of the same item it is a structural zero regardless of sample size). This biased both item-location and threshold recovery slightly outward (recovery slope > 1, concentrated at the extreme thresholds; roughly 13–16% at `constant=0` in simulation, largely independent of sample size). Same-item cells are now forced to zero after the matrix multiplication. Present since v1.0.0. Changes bare `PCM.calibrate()` output and everything downstream; `PCM` regression fixtures and `Examples/Model classes/PCM` outputs regenerated. `constant`-tuned defaults (above) were chosen against the corrected matrix.
- **`MFRM_Sim_Bivector` (and `MFRM_Sim(model='bivector')`) now generates the identified representation.** The `threshold_effects` table was centred only per threshold (across facet elements), leaving a non-zero mean within each facet element's own row — a per-facet-element level the bivector estimator structurally assigns to the *item*-effect axis instead (the `full[r,i,k] = item_effect[r,i] + threshold_effect[r,k]` split has a per-facet-element gauge freedom, which the estimator pins by forcing `threshold_effect` to sum to zero within each facet element). A direct table-for-table comparison of recovered vs. generating `threshold_effects` therefore showed a spurious ~20% compression (recovery slope ≈ 0.8) that vanished once the vectors were re-centred per facet element. The simulator now applies that second centring itself — subtracting each facet element's mean from its `threshold_effects` row and folding it into the corresponding `item_effects` row — so `full` (and hence every sampled response) is unchanged but recovered and generating parameters are now directly comparable. Recovery of the threshold marginal measures ≈ 0.98 as expected. `MFRM_Sim_Bistretch` is unaffected (its implied `threshold_effects` were already zero-sum per facet element). Present since v1.0.0. Bivector regression fixtures and the bivector example notebook's recovery cells updated.

### Changed
- **Default priority-vector method changed from `'cos'` to `'log-lik'`**, and its `matrix_power` default from `3` to `None` (resolving to `0` — no powering — for `'log-lik'`, and to `5` for every other method — raised from the previous fixed `3` after a power sweep found `SLM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` plateau by `2`–`3` while `PCM`'s wider item-threshold graph keeps improving to `5`–`6`; a no-op for `'log-lik'` and for the three plateaued models), on every `calibrate*` / `std_errors` / `model_selection` across all four model classes and on `base.priority_vector()`. `'log-lik'` is the Bradley–Terry maximum-likelihood fixed point — a lightweight iteration on the pairwise matrix, not a data-scale procedure like JML/MML — and handles an incomplete comparison graph natively, so it needs no matrix powering (whose transitive path counts in fact measurably worsen its recovery). A simulation study (SLM; full and 55%-sparse; small and large person/item samples) gave it the **lowest item-recovery RMSE of the four methods in every cell** — trivial on dense data, up to ~10% under sparsity — at ~1–2× the wall time of `'cos'` (a few ms). **This changes numeric output wherever `method=`/`matrix_power=` are not passed explicitly**, plus everything downstream (`item_stats`, SEs, DIF, `model_selection`, bootstraps) and the regenerated `Examples/Model classes/*` outputs. Pass `method='cos'` (with `matrix_power=3` if wanted) for the previous behaviour; `'cos'`/`'ls'`/`'evm'` output is bit-identical to before when selected explicitly. `'log-lik'` needs a strongly connected comparison graph (`check_data_connectivity` warns otherwise). Manual updated throughout.
- **Performance: `base.priority_vector()`.** `'log-lik'` warm-starts its MM iteration from the (inverted) cosine solution, roughly halving the iteration count — tens of passes over the item×item matrix on the default unpowered path (fewer as the item set grows and the comparison graph densifies), ~1 on a powered matrix — and gains a `max_iter` guard with a non-convergence warning. `'evm'` caps the PCA at its one used component (`n_components=1`); `'cos'` sheds redundant allocations; the PCM threshold-label list is cached across calls. `'cos'`/`'ls'` bit-identical to before, `'evm'` unchanged bar floating-point. `'cos'` is now documented (code and manual) as the Kou & Lin (2014) cosine maximisation method.

Item locations are unchanged (still `0.1`); the Rasch–Andrich thresholds shift — typically <1% RMSE on well-conditioned data, more in tight rating scales — and with them `threshold_se`, `cat_width_se`, `person`/`test` statistics, threshold DIF and bootstraps. Bare `PCM.calibrate()` item locations and thresholds shift — the recovery slope, previously biased outward to ~1.13–1.16, comes back to ~1.0 — and with them `std_errors`, `fit_statistics`, DIF, `model_selection` and bootstraps. Pass `constant` unchanged; there is no flag to restore the old matrix (the previous behaviour was a bug). `PCM` regression fixtures and `Examples/Model classes/PCM` outputs regenerated.
- **`MFRM.calibrate()` default output changes** (see Added). With arguments left at their defaults the calibration now uses `'cos'` for `items`/`thresholds`/`matrix`/`bivector` and `'log-lik'` for `global` and the stretch models, plus the tuned per-stage / per-model constants, rather than a uniform `'log-lik'` + `0.1`. - **Performance: vectorized PAIR/CPAT pairwise-matrix construction** across RSM, PCM, and MFRM. All routines vectorised as far as possible, replaced (often nested) raw Python for-loops with matrix multiplication, following the approach already in place for SLM. Applies to PAIR estimation for items and raters in `_build_pairwise_matrix()` (RSM/PCM/MFRM item calibration) and `_estimate_raters_global()`/`_pair_matrix()` (MFRM rater estimation), and also to CPAT threshold estimation `_threshold_distance()`. Also eliminated redundant dataframe build calls. No API or behaviour change — verified numerically identical to the prior implementation. Performance improvement on small-scale testing showed speeding up of ~1.2x (global, larger designs) to ~2.6x (bivector); benefits `std_errors()` bootstrap loops most, since this involves repeated calibration calls, so may be second or even minutes rather than milliseconds there.

## [1.2.0] - 2026-08-06

### Added
- **Three new MFRM rater-severity parameterisations**, each a closed-form restriction of an existing fully-free representation — no new estimation machinery, derived directly from an already-calibrated fit:
  - `model="centrality"` (alias `"threshold_stretch"`) — Jin & Wang (2018)-style rater centrality/extremity: a shift `beta_r` plus a stretch `alpha_r` applied to the reference thresholds, restricting the `"thresholds"` parameterisation. Sits strictly between `"global"` (1 free param/rater) and `"thresholds"` (K free params/rater) on the model-selection ladder.
  - `model="pseudo_halo"` (alias `"item_stretch"`) — the item-facet analogue: a shift `beta_r` plus a stretch `gamma_r` applied to reference item locations, restricting `"items"`. Named `pseudo_halo` deliberately — it tests compressed item-difficulty *perception* under an otherwise locally-independent response process, not classic halo effect's usual mechanism (elevated local item interdependence); pair with `item_res_corr_analysis`/`facet_res_corr_analysis` run per-rater to check for that separately.
  - `model="bistretch"` — combines both stretch axes at once: a shift `beta_r`, an item-difficulty-stretch `gamma_r`, and a threshold-stretch `alpha_r`, restricting `"bivector"`. Sits strictly between `"global"` and `"bivector"` on the model-selection ladder — the parsimonious middle ground when a rater shows genuine structure on both axes simultaneously.
  - All three integrate at full parity with the original five representations: `calibrate()`/`calibrate_anchor()`, `fit_statistics()`, `std_errors()` (bootstrap SEs), every `*_stats_df()` table, `model_selection()` (AIC/BIC/LR, with LR degrees of freedom against both the parent model and `"global"`), and all plotting methods (`icc`, `crcs`, `threshold_ccs`, `tcc`, `test_info`, `test_csem`, `std_residuals_plot`, etc.) — the MFRM rater-parameterisation family is now eight representations, not five.
  - Matching simulation classes `MFRM_Sim_Centrality`, `MFRM_Sim_PseudoHalo`, `MFRM_Sim_Bistretch` for genuine parameter recovery testing.
- **`item_stretch`/`threshold_stretch` aliases** for `pseudo_halo`/`centrality` — axis-based names matching the `"items"`/`"thresholds"` and `"bistretch"` naming convention. Work everywhere a model name is accepted: as the `model=`/`models=` value, and as the method-name suffix (e.g. `calibrate_threshold_stretch()`) for every method family with a `_centrality`/`_pseudo_halo` variant. Resulting attributes are still stored under the canonical `_centrality`/`_pseudo_halo` names regardless of which spelling was used.
- **`model_selection(models=...)`** — restrict the eight-way comparison to a chosen subset (e.g. a direct two-model test) without paying the cost of calibrating the rest.
- **`per_rater_model_selection()` extended to all eight representations, plus a new `models=` parameter.** Under `test='LR'`, the existing top-down ladder (`matrix → bivector → {items, thresholds} → global`) now runs one additional targeted stretch test depending on which of those five wins, so any rater can be assigned any of the eight representations rather than just the original five. `test='AIC'`/`'BIC'` (AIC is the default) instead pick the minimum-criterion model directly across all active representations, with no ladder involved. `models=` restricts which representations are eligible — for `test='LR'`, `{'global', 'items', 'thresholds', 'bivector'}` must stay included as the mandatory backbone the ladder's fork needs, while `'matrix'` and each stretch model are independently toggleable; for `test='AIC'`/`'BIC'` it is a direct filter over any non-empty subset.

### Fixed
- **MFRM row-order canonicalisation** (the fix v1.1.1's own changelog described) had been silently reverted by the refactor commit that produced that release, and shipped broken in v1.1.1 — any `MFRM` where facet_elements (raters) rate non-overlapping person sets could crash (`IndexError`) or silently corrupt facet/rater severity estimates via a mis-shaped positional reshape. Restored and reverified: `check_data_connectivity()` and `calibrate()` both now behave correctly on ragged rater×person designs.
- `MFRM_Sim_Centrality`/`MFRM_Sim_PseudoHalo`/`MFRM_Sim_Bistretch` raised `TypeError: got multiple values for keyword argument 'manual_items'` if a caller passed `manual_items=`/`manual_thresholds=`/`manual_persons=`/etc. directly to the constructor — fixed.

## [1.1.1] - 2026-07-23

### Added
- **Differential Item Functioning (DIF) testing**: `dif_test()` on `SLM`, `PCM`, and `RSM` — tests every item for DIF against a person-level covariate (`self.exogenous`), with anchor purification (robust-z or Wald-based outlier trimming) before testing, per-item Wald and/or likelihood-ratio tests, an omnibus joint test, BH/Bonferroni multiple-comparison correction, and optional ETS-style A/B/C severity categories (Zwick, Thayer & Mazzeo, 1997). `PCM`/`RSM` additionally report differential category functioning (DCF) via `threshold_dif_table` — tested via category *widths* rather than raw threshold locations, since a single genuine width change otherwise cascades into every downstream threshold.
- `calibrate_anchor()` added to `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM` for item-bank anchoring (equating a new form's items onto a bank's known difficulties), sharing the same robust anchor-selection machinery as DIF purification.
- `andersen_lr_test(split_by='exogenous', covariate=...)` on `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM` — Andersen's (1973) likelihood-ratio invariance test generalised to split by any exogenous person covariate, not just a median split on ability/score. (Not available on `MFRM`, where `andersen_lr_test` remains disabled for an unrelated reason — see Fixed/known issues.)
- `welch=True` option on `dif_test()` (all three models) for Welch–Satterthwaite t-tests instead of z-tests when group sizes are small or unequal; `size_adjust=True` for Tristán (2006) sample-size-adjusted standard errors.
- New worked example: `Examples/DIF testing/DIF testing example.ipynb`.

### Fixed
- Andersen/omnibus DIF tests were over-rejecting under the null (~1.3–1.7x nominal Type I error) because the alternative-hypothesis log-likelihoods used each subgroup's own separately-estimated person locations instead of the pooled model's — fixed across `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`.
- `PCM`'s omnibus DIF test additionally under-charged its degrees of freedom for per-item threshold/step-structure freedom, causing much larger false-positive inflation (~60% rejection under the null); fixed via a properly-scoped `omnibus_scope='item'|'full'` option.
- `MFRM.__init__` now canonicalises person row order on construction — a positional-reshape bug could otherwise silently collapse rater severity estimates toward zero when an `MFRM` was built from a non-canonically-ordered person subset.

### Changed
- `category_counts_df` (SLM, PCM, RSM, MFRM) rewritten to take `persons=`, `items=` (and, for MFRM, `facet_elements=`) filters plus a `counts_name=` option to store multiple named results side by side, instead of always computing across the full dataset. `category_counts_item` is folded into this method and removed as a separate call.
- Internal naming conventions standardised across `base.py`, `slm.py`, `pcm.py`, `rsm.py`, `mfrm.py`, `loaders.py` — `ability`/`abilities`/`difficulty`/`difficulties` renamed to `person_location(s)`/`item_location(s)` (and related parameter names) for consistency, fixing several naming-related bugs along the way.
- Package `__init__.py` consolidated under `raschpy/` (previously partly under a stray top-level `RaschPy/`), with an explicit documented `__all__` public API surface.

### Documentation
- PDF manual fully audited and updated: every model chapter (SLM/PCM/RSM/MFRM) and every simulation class checked method-by-method against the code, with documentation gaps fixed and duplicate LaTeX labels resolved.

### Removed
- Legacy `raschpy/old stable files with old names/` duplicate source files.
