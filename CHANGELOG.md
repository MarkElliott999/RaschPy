# Changelog

All notable changes to RaschPy are documented in this file.

## [1.4.0] - 2026-10-15

### Added
- **RCL-CPAT (regularised composite likelihood CPAT) estimation for `RSM` and `MFRM`** (`calibrate(estimation='cpat-rcl')`; the default `'cp-full-rcl'` extends it to the full pattern set, below). CPAT conditions on response patterns with equal totals: odd totals give the item comparisons within each facet element (and, for `MFRM`, the facet element comparisons within each item), even totals the threshold patterns ((k, k) against (k-1, k+1) on two items). RCL-CPAT fits all of them jointly, as one composite likelihood, by Newton-Raphson, with additive smoothing constants on the pattern counts and, for `MFRM`, ridge penalties on the facet element parameters. `RSM` is the single-facet-element case of the same estimator. `estimation='pair-cpat'` is the method used before v1.4.0 (Elliott & Buttery, 2022b); it reproduces v1.3.1's estimates exactly for `RSM`, and for `MFRM` apart from the threshold-stage fix below; the choice persists across refits of the same object and is carried into bootstrap, DIF, DRF and global-fit refits. `method=`, `matrix_power=`, `log_lik_tol=`, `threshold_method=` (`RSM`) and `matrix_marginals=`/`robust=` (`MFRM`) apply to `'pair-cpat'` only; other values passed with `'cpat-rcl'` are ignored, with a `UserWarning`. In simulation (several thousand datasets across crossed and incomplete rating designs), RCL-CPAT's median RMSE for item locations, thresholds and facet element parameters was equal to or lower than `'pair-cpat'`'s in every sub-model and design studied, and it removed the compression of item locations and thresholds that `'pair-cpat'` showed under the `items` sub-model (median item slope 0.88); person estimates were mostly unchanged. It is slower (milliseconds to a second for typical designs; several seconds for the `matrix` sub-model with many facet elements, items and categories).
- **Full-pattern RCL (`estimation='cp-full-rcl'`), now the default for `RSM` and `MFRM`.** For two items (scored by the same facet element), or two facet elements (scoring the same item), every pair of response patterns (k, l) and (k−1, l+1) with the same total is a binomial comparison in which the person cancels. CPAT's patterns are the subset with a difference of one between the two responses; the other patterns are most informative for item pairs far apart in difficulty, where CPAT's patterns are lopsided. All are fitted jointly by the same penalised composite likelihood as RCL-CPAT. Smoothing constants go on the item, facet element and CPAT threshold pattern counts only; the other pattern counts are left unsmoothed (smoothing them pulled thresholds together, most at high K). Patterns involving an unused category are left out. `estimation='cpat-rcl'` keeps the CPAT-only version (its estimates are unchanged). Sandwich standard errors cover both. In simulation: for `RSM` across 194 random designs (I 5–40, N 60–3,000, K 2–8, up to 60% missing), median threshold RMSE 9.5% below RCL-CPAT's (median paired ratio 0.905), at parity with CML (psychotools; 1.016) and with the lightest tail of the three (99th percentile 0.57 logits, against 0.88 for RCL-CPAT and 1.25 for CML); for `MFRM` `global` across 155 random designs, thresholds 12% below RCL-CPAT (0.880) and below TAM's MML (0.873), facet element effects 20% below RCL-CPAT (0.800) and at parity with TAM (1.005); the extended sub-models gained 3–10% on the full location grid, apart from `matrix` (no change). Sandwich SE/SD 0.94–1.13 and 95% coverage 0.93–0.97 across the 8 sub-models. `model_selection()` classification was unchanged or slightly better (worst-case correct-choice rate over generating sub-models 0.825 against 0.804). Fitting takes longer: about 10% in `model_selection()` at typical sizes, but 2–5 times longer at very large ones, mostly because facet element pairs now contribute all their patterns (e.g. `RSM` with 3,000 items and 100,000 persons: calibration 250 s against 146 s for `'cpat-rcl'`, sandwich standard errors 619 s against 157 s; `MFRM` with 200 raters, 50 items and 20,000 persons: calibration 60–160 s against 12–35 s, sandwich standard errors 3–20 minutes against about 1). `estimation='cpat-rcl'` remains available where speed matters most.
- **`MFRM.calibrate()` regularisation arguments** for RCL estimation (`'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'`), each in its sub-model's own units: `ridge=` (default 0.3; `global`, `items`, `thresholds`, `bivector`, and the bivector parts of `matrix`) on the facet element deviations in logits; `interaction_ridge=` (default 3; `matrix`) on the item × threshold interactions beyond the bivector parts; `stretch_ridge=` (default 1; `centrality`, `pseudo_halo`, `bistretch`) on (omega − 1)². One passed to a sub-model it does not apply to is ignored, with a `UserWarning`. Defaults chosen by simulation. The stretch models are fitted from two starts from a `'pair-cpat'` fit (its stretch parameters, and omega = 1), keeping the higher composite likelihood.
- **Thin and empty category widths in RCL-CPAT** (`RSM` and `MFRM`): a width with fewer than 20 threshold patterns, or with all its patterns on one side, is pulled toward the mean of the other widths by a ridge of 0.5 · (1 − T/20) (0.5 if one-sided); widths next to an empty middle category, which their own patterns do not identify, are left out of the joint fit and estimated by the `cpat-cml` rule of `RSM.threshold_set()` given the joint fit's item locations. The existing "no information" and "poorly informed" width warnings still apply.
- **Analytic (sandwich) standard errors for `RSM` under RCL-CPAT**, the default in `std_errors()` as before: the bread is the negative Hessian of the penalised composite log-likelihood at the estimates, the meat the sum over persons of their score outer products (with the Kauermann-Carroll correction where `global_fit()` asks for it). On simulated data they agreed with the bootstrap to within a few percent.
- **Analytic (sandwich) standard errors for `MFRM` under RCL-CPAT, now the default in `std_errors()`** (`se_method=None`; `'sandwich'`/`'bootstrap'` to choose). The covariance of the fitted parameters is a sandwich (bread: negative Hessian of the penalised composite log-likelihood at the estimates; meat: sum over persons of their score outer products), and the standard errors of every reported quantity -- item locations, thresholds, category widths, facet element effects and their marginals, the stretch parameters and their covariances -- follow by the delta method, anchored or not; intervals are estimate ± z · SE. In simulation (300 replicate datasets for each of the 8 sub-models, crossed and double-marking designs), the median ratio of the sandwich SE to the sampling SD of the estimates was 0.98–1.06 for item locations, thresholds and stretch parameters, with median 95% coverage 0.94–0.97 (around the mean estimate). It takes milliseconds rather than the seconds to minutes of 500 recalibrations. The bootstrap is still used for `'pair-cpat'` sub-models, `'mixed'`, `store_bootstrap=True` (DIF, DRF and the divergence test, which use the fitted resamples), and data with an unused score category (with a `UserWarning`). `self.se_method_{model}` records the method used.
- **`self.convergence`** (`RSM`, `MFRM`; `MFRM` also `convergence_{model}`): Newton-Raphson iterations and whether the RCL fit (`'cp-full-rcl'` or `'cpat-rcl'`) converged, with a `UserWarning` if it did not. `None` under `'pair-cpat'`.
- **`MFRM.dif_test()` and `MFRM.drf_test()`**: Differential Item/Category Functioning (DIF/DCF) and Differential Rater Functioning (DRF) tests by an exogenous variable, under any of the 8 facet element parameterisations (`model=`). `dif_test()` follows `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`'s design: item-location DIF (per-item Wald test by default, optional per-item likelihood-ratio test and ETS A/B/C categories) plus DCF via category widths, as in `RSM`, with a new `dcf_cap` argument (default `2000`, in persons) for `size_adjust=True`. There are two omnibus tests: `dif_omnibus_table` tests items only, with facet effects held at their pooled values in both groups (`df` = common items − 1); `df_omnibus_table` tests items and facet effects together. `drf_test()` is new: the facet-element-location analogue of DIF. Its tests depend on `model`: one Wald test per facet element for `global`; a separate test of each facet element's effect on each item or threshold for `items`/`thresholds`/`bivector`/`matrix`; and one joint test of each facet element's shift and stretch parameters (T = Δ'Σ⁻¹Δ, with Σ from paired bootstrap draws) for `centrality`/`pseudo_halo`/`bistretch`. Only facet elements that scored at least `min_persons_per_group` persons in both groups are tested. Both methods purify the scale before testing (leave-one-out, see the purification fix below). With `estimation='pair-cpat'`, `dif_test()` warns under the 6 parameterisations at risk of compression of item locations and thresholds (known issue #17); `global`, `items` and `pseudo_halo` carry no warning. Manual updated (new `dif_test`/`drf_test` sections in the MFRM chapter).
- **Formal null-calibration and power validation for MFRM's DIF/DRF omnibus tests** (known issue #38): the `dif_omnibus_table` and DRF omnibus tests was checked by simulation across all 8 rater models (100-rep null, 20-rep power) and found conservative under the null (upper 95% confidence bound ≈3% against nominal 5%) with decisive power (20/20 rejections at a 1.5-logit shift). The DIF/DRF purification's own Type I calibration (known issue #39) was separately validated by simulation and found accurate once corrected — see **Fixed** below.
- **`recovery_stats()`**, across `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` — a ground-truth parameter-recovery diagnostic for simulation studies. Compares generating (known, simulated) parameter values against recovered (calibrated) estimates on a common scale, reporting bias/RMSE/correlation per parameter family (items, thresholds, persons, and, for `MFRM`, rater facet_effects) plus a summary recovery plot. Cross-model support (`anchor=`/`groups=` for e.g. rater-level breakdowns). Worked example notebooks added for all 4 model classes.
- **`wright_map(threshold_type=...)`**, on `PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` (not applicable to the dichotomous `SLM`). Governs which threshold convention is plotted when `item_level='thresholds'`: `'rasch-andrich'` (default, unchanged) plots the raw Rasch-Andrich thresholds, which can be disordered; `'thurstonian'` plots the median (cumulative) threshold — the point where `P(X >= k) = 0.5` — found by root-finding, always ordered regardless of the underlying Rasch-Andrich thresholds; `'half-point'` plots the location where the expected item score `E[X] = k - 0.5`, also always ordered. Also governs `item_strip=True`'s category-boundary segments — `'thurstonian'`/`'half-point'` draw the plain (guaranteed monotonic) intervals between consecutive boundaries, so disorder-related warnings cannot occur for those two. Manual updated (`wright_map` sections, `PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM`).
- **CI** (`.github/workflows/tests.yml`, pytest wrappers over the existing smoke/regression/edge-case runners, Python 3.10–3.13 matrix), a ground-truth parameter-recovery benchmark against `eRm`/`TAM`/`pairwise` (R packages covering CML, MML/JML, and Choppin-pairwise estimation respectively) across a 33-condition grid, and `hypothesis`-based invariance property tests (person-row/item-column permutation invariance, anchor-translation invariance). In that benchmark RaschPy completed all 1,650+ runs without failure, fitting 100–1000 times faster than the R packages, and `TAM`'s MML estimates degraded with missing data, sparse categories (K = 7) and non-normal person distributions, where RaschPy's did not. `ARCHITECTURE.md`/`CONTRIBUTING.md` added for contributors.
- **`missing_method=` on every simulation class** (`SLM_Sim`, `RSM_Sim`, `PCM_Sim`, `MFRM_Sim` and its wrappers). `'bernoulli'` (default, unchanged) makes each response missing independently with probability `missing`, so the realised proportion varies around it (0.284–0.313 across 200 seeds for `missing=0.3` on 20 × 500 cells). `'exact'` makes exactly `round(missing * cells)` responses missing: per facet element for `MFRM_Sim(shared_missing=False)`, or in the one shared pattern otherwise. It reuses the same random draws, taking the cells with the smallest missing-data uniforms, so observed scores are identical to `'bernoulli'`, the two missing patterns differ only in the cells whose uniforms lie nearest the cut-off, and seeded results are reproducible either way.

- **`RSM.calibrate(threshold_method=...)`**, choosing the estimator for the threshold stage. `'cpat-cml'` (the new default) estimates each distance between adjacent thresholds by conditional maximum likelihood: given that a person's responses to two different items are `(k, k)` or `(k-1, k+1)`, the person location cancels and the count of `(k, k)` patterns is binomial with log-odds `tau_{k+1} - tau_k - (delta_i - delta_j)`, the item difference entering as a known offset. `'cpat-info-weighted'` is the previous estimator (Elliott & Buttery, 2022b, eq. 4.18), the harmonic-mean information-weighted mean of the per-pair log-odds. Because its weights are computed from the same observed proportions as the log-odds they weight, that estimator compresses the scale in thin data (median recovery slope 0.93 on small, sparse designs), while `'cpat-cml'` does not (1.00). In simulation (3,840 well-behaved datasets: `K` 2–8, category widths 0.6–3.0 logits, `N` 100–50,000, 5–60 items, 0–60% missing), `'cpat-cml'` (there with smoothing constant 0.01) halved the 90th percentile of threshold RMSE (0.195 against 0.432 logits), with identical results at large `N`; a later small-`N` sweep (`N` 50–500) put its best constant at 0.03, the shipped default for both estimators. A distance whose comparison patterns are observed fewer than 20 times in total falls back to the information-weighted estimator, which was more robust at that level (out of sample, this cut the worst person-measurement error on very thin data from 2.6 to 1.0 logits without changing the 90th percentile).
- **Handling of empty middle categories and uninformative threshold distances in `RSM`.** When a middle category has no responses, the three distances touching it are not identified, and a flat smoothing constant let their artefacts distort the whole scale (median person-measurement error 0.76 logits, 90th percentile 1.63, on 576 simulated datasets with one empty category). These distances now get a targeted constant, `lambda` times the mean count per item pair, with `lambda` a power function of the estimated width of the other categories (from a quick fit with the empty category merged when no other distance is available), fitted by simulation and validated out of sample: median 0.25, 90th percentile 0.60 on the same datasets. A `UserWarning` explains that only the point where the category response curves of the categories either side intersect is identified, and sets out the options (collapse or rescore if the category was never a genuine option or may be miscoded; for `max_score=2`, where the items are effectively dichotomous, analyse as dichotomous instead). A distance with no comparison patterns at all is set to the mean of the others with a warning (new `no_info='mean'`), or raises (`no_info='raise'`); previously it always raised. Poorly informed distances (fewer than 20 patterns) also warn. Regression tests added.
- **Analytic (sandwich) standard errors for `RSM`, now the default in `std_errors()`.** A sandwich covariance for the stacked estimating equations of both calibration stages -- the Bradley-Terry equations of the PAIR item stage and the `cpat-cml` equations of the threshold stage -- with persons as the independent units, so item-location uncertainty is carried into the thresholds. In simulation (6 designs: `N` 150–2,000, 6 or 15 items, `K` 3 or 5, 0% or 30% missing; 200 replicate datasets each), the mean standard error divided by the true sampling standard deviation was 0.97–1.08 for thresholds and 0.97–1.02 for item locations, matching the bootstrap to within 0.01 in every design; ignoring the person-level dependence (the naive likelihood SE) gave 0.39–0.56, and ignoring the item stage 0.78–0.95. It takes about as long as one calibration (0.18 s for 50,000 persons and 40 items, against about 60 s for a 500-resample bootstrap). New `se_method=` argument: `None` (default) uses the sandwich unless the threshold estimates rely on something it does not model -- an unused category, a category width observed fewer than 20 times or not at all, or `threshold_method='cpat-info-weighted'` -- and then uses the bootstrap; `'sandwich'` or `'bootstrap'` force one. In very thin data the sandwich understated threshold standard errors by up to 25% (N = 40, 4 items, 40% missing) while the bootstrap overstated them by up to 39%. With the sandwich, confidence intervals are estimate ± z·SE, `category_stats_df()`'s disorder proportion is the normal-approximation probability of a negative category width, the bootstrap draw attributes are `None`, and `self.se_covariance` holds the item and category-width covariance matrices; `self.se_method` records the method used. Regression test added.
- **Analytic (sandwich) standard errors for `SLM` and `PCM` too**, now the default in `std_errors()`, as for `RSM`: a sandwich covariance for the Bradley-Terry estimating equations of PAIR, with persons as the independent units -- on the item pairwise matrix for `SLM`, and on the item-category pairwise matrix for `PCM`, whose central locations, centred thresholds and category widths are linear functions of the uncentred thresholds, so their covariances follow exactly. `SLM`: mean standard error divided by the true sampling standard deviation 0.96–1.01 across six designs (200 replicate datasets each; `N` 40–2,000, 6–40 items, 0–50% missing, including thin data), against 0.94–1.02 for the bootstrap. `PCM`: 0.99–1.00 when all categories are used; with a category nobody used it underestimates (0.90 for thresholds, 0.79 for item locations), so `se_method=None` (default) falls back to the bootstrap whenever any item has an unused category. `SLM` defaults to `se_method='sandwich'`. With the sandwich, confidence intervals are estimate ± z·SE, `PCM.category_stats_df()`'s disorder proportion is the normal-approximation probability, `SLM.fit_statistics()`'s item separation index is computed as √(var(item locations) / mean squared SE) (the quantity the bootstrap version estimates), and the bootstrap draw attributes are `None`. Sandwich standard errors are deterministic: the same data give the same values, with no resampling noise. `SLM` and `PCM` regression fixtures regenerated. Regression test added.
- **`dif_test()` uses the analytic standard errors too** (`SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`), whenever each group's model uses them. The purification's leave-one-out variances, Var(x_r − a'x_sel) for each tested item against its (weighted) anchor mean, previously estimated from each group's bootstrap draws to capture the correlation between item estimates (known issue #39), now follow exactly from the sandwich covariance matrix, V_rr − 2a'V_sel,r + a'V_sel,sel·a. In simulation (two groups of 400, 14 items, 100 replications per condition), Type I error and power matched the bootstrap version: per-item Type I error 0.049–0.062 against 0.052–0.064 with no DIF, and power at a 0.5-logit shift 0.660 / 0.950 / 0.985 for `SLM` / `RSM` / `PCM` against 0.665 / 0.950 / 0.985.
- **A `UserWarning` when standard errors fall back to the bootstrap**, giving the reason (`RSM`: the unused categories or thinly observed category widths, or `threshold_method='cpat-info-weighted'`; `PCM`: the unused item categories), since the fallback itself signals sparse data.
- **`global_fit()`**, on `SLM`/`RSM`/`PCM`: a global test of parameter invariance across two groups of persons, with `method=` (`'wald'`, the default; `'andersen-lr'`, reserved for the planned CML estimator, raises `NotImplementedError` with PAIR/CPAT estimates, which do not maximise the likelihood it evaluates; `'martin-lof'` planned), `split=` (`'score'`, the default median split of mean observed score; `'person_location'`; `'random'`; or `'exogenous'` with `covariate=`), `welch=True`, `alpha=0.05` and `split_prop=0.5` (the proportion of persons in each outer group for the `'score'` and `'person_location'` splits: 0.5 is a median split, 1/3 compares the outer thirds as in RUMM, 0.4 lies in between; the middle group is left out). The Wald test compares the two groups' separately calibrated estimates (re-centred) using their sandwich covariances, W = d'(V_1 + V_2)^+ d, referred with `welch=True` to an F distribution with denominator degrees of freedom from the smaller group. Parameters not estimable in both groups -- thresholds next to a category unused in one group, and (`RSM`) category widths needing a fallback estimator -- are excluded and reported (`global_fit_excluded`, with a warning). This restores a valid score-split test for PAIR/CPAT: `andersen_lr_test()`'s score and person-location splits were disabled in 2026-07 because the likelihood ratio, evaluated at estimates that do not maximise that likelihood, had no null calibration or power. Each group's sandwich covariance uses the Kauermann-Carroll small-sample correction (`se_correction='kauermann-carroll'`, the default; `None` for the uncorrected sandwich): each person's score contribution is scaled by (I − H_n)^(−1/2), H_n being that person's leverage, which removes the downward bias of the sandwich to first order. In simulation (300 null datasets per design; N = 600 and 2,000; score, random and exogenous splits) the mean of W/df was 0.98–1.05 in every design, and the rejection rate at α = 0.05 was 4–7% for `SLM` and `RSM`, and 5–7% for `PCM` except with about 300 persons per group on the exogenous split (10%; W/df's mean was right there, but its spread somewhat wider than the F reference's). Without the correction, `PCM`'s score split with about 300 persons per group rejected 8.7% (mean W/df 1.07); with it, 6.3% (1.03). Of the alternatives tried, the n/(n − p) correction overcorrected random splits (1.8%) and Mancl-DeRouen the score split (2.8%). With two of 12 items shifted by 0.5 logits between groups of 300, power was 59% (`SLM`), 91% (`RSM`) and 88% (`PCM`). On the same data, the existing likelihood ratio test (exogenous split) was conservative for `RSM` (mean statistic 17–37% of its degrees of freedom; rejection 0–0.3%) and `PCM` (38–86%; 0.5–3.8%). Results: `global_fit_summary`, `global_fit_table` (each compared parameter's difference, SE and z), `global_fit_excluded`, `global_fit_groups`. `andersen_lr_test()` is deprecated in favour of `global_fit()` (`FutureWarning`). An oracle check of an empirical Bartlett correction for the likelihood ratio test (exogenous split, 300 null and 300 DIF datasets per design; scaling by the true null mean, estimated on separate datasets) ruled it out: for `RSM` the statistic was exactly 0 in 42–46% of null datasets, so the rescaled test rejected 26%; for `PCM` its variance was 25% above a scaled chi-square's (rejection 6.7–7.3%). Regression test added.
- **Simulation rename methods now rename the generating parameters too** (`SLM_Sim`, `RSM_Sim`, `PCM_Sim`, `MFRM_Sim` and its wrappers: `rename_item`/`rename_items_all`, `rename_person`/`rename_persons_all`, and `MFRM_Sim`'s `rename_rater`/`rename_raters_all`). They previously changed names only in `responses`, leaving `items`, `persons`, `PCM_Sim`'s `thresholds`/`max_score_vector`, `MFRM_Sim`'s `facet_effects`/`item_effects`/`threshold_effects`/`lambda_`/`omega` and the cached response probabilities under the old names, so fitting a model to a renamed simulation and calling `recovery_stats()` raised `KeyError` (items, persons) or found no generating value (facet elements). All four classes now share one relabelling step, and values are unchanged. A rename that is rejected with a warning (old name not found, same name, non-string, or duplicate) now leaves everything unchanged; previously some of these cases still applied the rename, e.g. creating duplicate names. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM` removes facet elements (e.g. raters) with no responses, and reports those that rated very few persons,** when the object is created, with a `UserWarning` naming them (and, for the second group, how many persons each rated) and the new attributes `empty_facet_elements`, `thin_facet_elements` and `facet_element_person_counts` (aliases follow the facet name, e.g. `empty_raters`, `thin_raters`, `rater_person_counts`). Their parameters cannot be estimated, or are poorly determined, and in the extended sub-models they can distort other raters' estimates through the identification constraints. "Very few" is fewer than `min_persons` (new argument, default 3): in simulation (stretch sub-models, 1,470 datasets), raters that rated 1–2 persons were the main source of badly wrong estimates, and from 3 persons upwards the rate fell to a few percent. Raters with no responses are always removed, since nothing can be estimated for them. New argument `drop_facet_elements=None`: `'thin'` also removes those below `min_persons`, and a list removes the named raters, before anything else is done; removed raters (including those with no responses) are listed in `dropped_facet_elements` (alias e.g. `dropped_raters`). New method `drop_facet_elements(which)` (alias following the facet name, e.g. `drop_raters()`) does the same on an existing object, e.g. after the warning or to exclude a rater with erratic rating behaviour: it rebuilds the object from its current responses with the same constructor settings, clearing any results, keeping renames and `generating`, and adding to `dropped_facet_elements`; the thin-rater warning now points to it. `MFRM_Sim` and its wrappers have the same method (`drop_facet_elements(which, min_persons=3)`, alias e.g. `drop_raters()`), which removes the facet elements' responses and their generating parameters (`facet_effects`, bivector's `item_effects`/`threshold_effects`, the stretch models' `lambda_`/`omega` parameters), leaving all other responses and parameters unchanged, and records them in `dropped_facet_elements`. `calibrate()` also warns when a stretch sub-model imposes no restriction on its parent (`centrality` with `max_score=2`, `pseudo_halo` with 2 items, `bistretch` with both), since it then fits the data identically. Facet elements that used only the lowest or only the highest score category are reported in the same way, in one warning on creation, and listed in `extreme_facet_elements` (alias e.g. `extreme_raters`); `'extreme'` removes them (`drop_facet_elements='extreme'`, `drop_facet_elements('extreme')`, and on `MFRM_Sim`). Like a person with an extreme score, their location is not identified by the data: the reported value comes from the smoothing constants (e.g. 5.3 logits under `cpat-rcl` and 7.0 under `pair-cpat` for a rater who gave every response 0), while the other estimates are unaffected. They are not removed automatically, since they did score real responses. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.calibrate()` warns when a facet element used some score categories twice or less** under the sub-models that estimate each facet element's location separately at each threshold (`thresholds`, `bivector`, `matrix`, `centrality`, `bistretch`). One warning lists each such facet element with the categories and counts, and points to `category_counts_df()` for the full breakdown: their locations at the thresholds bounding those categories are poorly determined. In simulation, datasets with a facet element using a category 0–2 times accounted for most of the remaining large rater errors (tail rate 32–35%, against 0.2% when every category was used more than 10 times). It is not raised for bootstrap resamples, where resampling persons often thins a category that the data itself uses enough. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM` warns when the facet elements fall into groups never compared with each other** (no person scored one point apart on the same item by facet elements from different groups), with both estimation methods: how the groups' locations differ is then not identified by the data. One warning per calibration, listing the groups. Under RCL estimation the stretch sub-models leave that difference at its starting value (from the `pair-cpat` fit) rather than letting it drift.
- **Standard errors are always produced** (`RSM`, `MFRM`, RCL estimation): with `se_method=None`, `std_errors()` falls back to the bootstrap, with a `UserWarning` giving the reason, whenever the sandwich does not apply -- an unused score category, a calibration that did not converge, a design too large to form the sandwich's information matrix (beyond about 2 × 10⁸ Jacobian entries), or estimates the data do not identify (directions in which the bread of the sandwich is singular, for example the shift parameters of unlinked groups of facet elements in a stretch model; their bootstrap standard errors then reflect the penalties and starting values rather than the data). An explicit `se_method='sandwich'` gives NaN for such estimates, with a `UserWarning`, rather than a pseudo-inverse's zero variance.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()` and `per_rater_model_selection()` leave out a sub-model that is only a reparameterisation of another for the design** (centrality = thresholds when `max_score=2`; pseudo_halo = items with 2 items; bistretch = bivector when both hold), keeping the first in ladder order, with a warning; all other comparisons still run. Datasets with unobserved categories, directional isolation or disconnection were excluded from the fit, since their thresholds are not identified; on the remaining datasets, rules fitted on item RMSE, threshold RMSE, concordance correlation, a slope-penalised RMSE and expected KL divergence all agreed closely with this one. Out of sample (768 fresh datasets on designs off the fitting grid, including unseen `K = 4, 6`), its 95th-percentile excess person-measurement error over the best constant for each dataset was 0.019 logits, against 0.151 for `0.1`; it was worse than `0.1` by more than 5% in 5 of 136 clean conditions (at most 0.06 logits) and better in 91. With mixed maximum scores the constant is applied per cell of the threshold-pair matrix: cell `(a, b)` gets `0.1 * N ** 0.15 * (1 - missing) ** 0.75 * (K_a * K_b) ** -0.7`, which is the same rule when all items share one `K` (identical output). ### Known issues (fix planned for 1.4.1)
- **`RSM` sandwich standard errors for thresholds are somewhat too small with few persons and many categories** (e.g. N = 150, K = 6: SE/SD 0.70–0.92, 95% coverage 0.90; fine by N = 300). The same under `'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'`. `std_errors(se_method='bootstrap')` is an alternative in such designs.
- **Results computed earlier are not refreshed after recalibrating with different settings.** For example, after `calibrate()`, `item_stats_df()`, `calibrate(constant=2.0)`, `item_stats_df()`, the second table shows the new estimates alongside the earlier standard errors and fit statistics, and person estimates keep their earlier values. Until this is fixed, construct a new model object when changing calibration settings.
- **Standard errors computed implicitly ignore the calibration settings actually used.** When a stats method runs the bootstrap itself, it uses its own default estimator settings rather than those passed to `calibrate()`, e.g. after `calibrate(constant=2.0)` the SEs are those of the default constant. Pass the same settings to the stats method, or call `std_errors()` with them explicitly.
- **`MFRM` anchored and unanchored standard errors can differ by resampling noise alone.** With the defaults (`store_bootstrap=False`, `seed=None`), `anchor_std_errors()` draws fresh resamples, so SEs that should be identical under a pure shift (`global`, `items`, `thresholds`, `matrix`, `bivector`) differed by up to 16% in testing. Call `std_errors(..., store_bootstrap=True)` first, or give both calls the same `seed`.
- **Several methods fail on an uncalibrated model instead of calibrating it themselves**, with an `AttributeError`: on `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`, `person`, `person_estimates`, `csem`, `score_lookup`, `score_lookup_table`, `icc`, `crcs`, `threshold_ccs`, `iic`, `tcc`, `test_info`, `test_csem`, `wright_map` and `class_intervals*`; `class_intervals*` and `std_residuals_plot` also fail after `calibrate()` until person estimates and fit statistics exist. On `MFRM`, `calibrate_anchor` and `anchor_std_errors` fail on an uncalibrated model. Call `calibrate()` (and, as needed, `person_estimates()`/`fit_statistics()`) first.

### Fixed
- **`MFRM` sandwich standard errors with an unused lowest or highest score category.** Only empty middle categories made `std_errors()` fall back to the bootstrap, so a threshold next to an unused extreme category, which the data do not inform, got a sandwich standard error set by the width penalty. Any unused category now triggers the fallback, with a `UserWarning`, as in `RSM`. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM` standard errors of extreme facet elements are now NaN** (both methods, anchored or not): a facet element that used only the lowest or only the highest category has a location the data do not identify, so it had a normal-looking but meaningless standard error. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.dif_test()`'s known issue #17 warning is now raised only with `estimation='pair-cpat'`.** The compression it warns about comes from `'pair-cpat'`'s separate estimation stages; under `'cp-full-rcl'` and `'cpat-rcl'` items, thresholds and facet element parameters are estimated jointly. Regression test added.
- **`calibrate_anchor(calibrate=True)` could not set the estimation method.** In `RSM` and `MFRM` it did not accept `estimation=`, so the calibration it ran always used the object's last method (or `'cp-full-rcl'`). It now accepts `estimation=` (and, in `RSM`, `threshold_method=`; in `MFRM`, `matrix_marginals=`, `ridge=`, `interaction_ridge=` and `stretch_ridge=`) and passes them to `calibrate()`. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.global_fit()` raised an obscure `AttributeError`**: the method is inherited but not implemented for MFRM. It now raises `NotImplementedError`, pointing to `dif_test()`. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.rater_stats_df(divergence_test=...)` raised `AttributeError` for the stretch sub-models under RCL estimation.** The test compares each bootstrap resample's stretch fit with its parent fit (`thresholds` for `centrality`, `items` for `pseudo_halo`, `bivector` for `bistretch`), which `pair-cpat` produces as a by-product but RCL does not, since it fits the stretch model directly. The parent is now calibrated on each resample (anchored, if requested, with the full-data anchor adjustment), keeping the comparison paired, and on a copy of the object, so its latest-calibration attributes are unchanged. Regression test added.
- **Anchored `MFRM.per_rater_model_selection()` evaluated the anchored facet effects against the unanchored item locations and thresholds.** Anchoring moves the anchors' mean effects into the item locations and thresholds, so every facet element's log-likelihoods were computed at locations shifted by the anchors' mean pattern, and the stretch sub-models were derived against the wrong reference values. This pushed facet elements towards the stretch sub-models, increasingly so for larger facet effects: in the design of the rater model selection example (20 raters, 4 flat anchors; 30 replicates), accuracy at N = 2,000 rose from 0.76 to 0.96, and at N = 500 from 0.62 to 0.87. The facet effects of the resulting `'mixed'` model (including in bootstrap resamples) are corrected in the same way. Present since the method was introduced. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.model_selection()`'s dynamic person subsample is now T = min(20R(I − 1)(K − 1), 1500)** (K = `max_score`; K − 1 taken as 1 for dichotomous data). It was min(20(I − 1)(R − 1), 1500), an incorrect port of `per_rater_model_selection()`'s per-facet-element rule min(20(I − 1)(K − 1), 1500); the new rule is that rule over all R facet elements, mirroring the RSM vs PCM rule with each (facet element, item) pair treated as an item. The old rule ignored K and was too small for the high-df comparisons: in a classification study (4,480 datasets from all eight sub-models; I = 4, 10; K = 3, 5; R = 4, 10; N = 100–6,400), the mean worst-case correct-choice rate for AIC + sig. test was 0.83 against 0.82 at default effect sizes and 0.64 against 0.45 at 0.4 × default (worst design 0.40 against 0.00), mainly from bivector data being assigned to items. The 1,500 cap still protects the low-df comparisons: without subsampling, centrality data at N = 6,400 were assigned to a more complex sub-model 25–30% of the time.
- **`MFRM`'s `'pair-cpat'` threshold stage took the log of sums rather than the sum of logs.** Eq. 4.54 of Elliott & Buttery (2022b) averages, for each item pair, the per-facet-element log-ratios of the CPAT patterns, so that the facet element terms cancel; `_threshold_distance()` summed the counts over facet elements first and took one log-ratio, which attenuates the distance whenever facet elements differ (logit non-collapsibility) and compressed the threshold scale. It now follows eq. 4.54: per-facet-element log-ratios (smoothing constant per facet element cell) averaged over the facet elements with patterns for the pair, item pairs weighted by their information from the summed counts. In simulation (60 datasets per sub-model, crossed and double-marking, default threshold constant 0.3), median threshold RMSE fell from 0.29 to 0.11 under `items`, 0.25 to 0.10 under `bivector`, 0.057 to 0.049 under `global` and 0.080 to 0.070 under `thresholds` (median slopes 0.96, 0.95, 0.99, 0.98). `'pair-cpat'` MFRM threshold estimates (and everything downstream of them) change accordingly. Present since v1.0.0.
- **CPAT threshold estimation counted same-item cells** in `RSM` and `MFRM`. The comparison matrices are built by multiplying indicator matrices, which also fills the diagonal (a person scoring `k` on item `i` "paired" with the same response). CPAT (Elliott & Buttery, 2022b, eq. 4.18) sums only over pairs of different items; the diagonal entered the numerator but not the denominator, inflating the `(k, k)` side. The diagonal is now excluded. Present since v1.0.0. Removing it exposed the information-weighted estimator's compression in thin data, addressed by `threshold_method` (see Added). `RSM` and `MFRM` regression fixtures regenerated.
- **Warm person estimates are now solved directly, fixing extreme scores**, across `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` (`person`, `person_estimates`, `score_lookup`, `score_lookup_table`, and everything built on them). With `warm_corr=True`, the estimate was the ML estimate plus the one-step correction `J / (2 I^2)` evaluated at it. That is a first-order approximation, and it fails where the information `I` is close to zero, which for extreme scores is exactly where the adjusted ML estimate lies: the smaller `ext_score_adjustment`, the further out the ML estimate and the larger the overshoot. On a 10-item, `K = 3` `RSM`, a score of 0 gave −4.10 logits at the default adjustment of 0.5, −1.76 at 0.1, +8.69 at 0.03 and +40.92 at 0.01, against −5.16 for the root of Warm's equation. The same mechanism produced isolated non-extreme blow-ups when thresholds were badly disordered (one person moved −1,166.6 logits from a sensible ML estimate of −0.71). Estimates are now the root of the weighted likelihood equation `r − E(θ) + J(θ) / (2 I(θ)) = 0`, bracketed and refined by Newton steps with a bisection fallback. That root is finite for every score, including 0 and the maximum, so `ext_score_adjustment` now only affects plain ML (`warm_corr=False`) and the starting value. `person()` and `score_lookup()` agree for extreme scores in all four classes. Non-extreme persons move little (median shift 0.001–0.01 logits, at most 0.10, on the 6-item, 200-person `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM` regression datasets); extreme persons move outwards (on the `SLM` regression data, from −1.95/+2.17 to −3.06/+3.34 logits at the default adjustment), which also changes person-based statistics such as reliability, fit and the Andersen test. The public `warm()` methods still return the one-step correction term. Regression test added.
- **`MFRM.check_anchor_homogeneity(model='bistretch')` silently skipped the per-component breakdown.** The model passed validation and the overall Cochran's Q / robust test ran correctly, but `bistretch` had no entry in the component list, so `anchor_homogeneity_per_component` was `None` even with `per_component=True`. It now gets one row per (item, threshold) cell, like `bivector`, whose full-vector layout it shares. Regression test added.
- **Docstrings of the stats-table methods said `Returns: None`**; they now describe the returned table.
- **`RSM` raised a pandas deprecation warning on construction** (`isnull().all(1)`: positional arguments to `all` become keyword-only in pandas 4.0); now `all(axis=1)`.
- **`SLM.calibrate()`'s additive smoothing only ever filled structural zero cells, unlike `PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM`, which also add `constant` to both directions of every pair with at least one observed comparison ("full smoothing").** Paired simulation across item count, sample size, item spread and missingness found full smoothing reduces item-recovery RMSE at small-to-moderate N, converging to a wash at large N, consistent across `SLM`/`RSM`/`MFRM`; `SLM` now matches the other three. `PCM`'s existing full smoothing was checked the same way and found inconsistent (favourable, neutral or unfavourable depending on `max_score`/`item_range`/`person_sd`, no clean predictive rule) — left as the default, to revisit later. `SLM` regression fixtures regenerated; bare `calibrate()` output shifts slightly versus prior releases.
- **DIF/DRF purification's leave-one-out translation constant under-covered its own sampling variance (known issue #39), across all 4 model classes.** The translation constant (`tc`) used to place a focal group's item locations (or, for MFRM's DRF, rater severities) onto the reference group's scale was computed once, from the *whole* anchor set, and then reused unchanged when testing every individual item/rater against that same anchor set — a self-referential circularity when the anchor set is small (each anchored unit contributed to its own reference point), and the reported standard error did not account for `tc` itself being an estimated quantity with its own sampling variance. `_purification_loo_tc()` (shared across `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM`) now computes a leave-one-out `tc` per unit (excluding that unit's own contribution), with its variance estimated directly from each group's own stored bootstrap resamples (falling back to an independence-assumption closed form when bootstrap samples aren't available). In simulation, the previous formula under-covered Type I error by roughly 2x nominal in DRF's typically-small rater panels; a first-pass fix (leave-one-out `tc` alone, without the bootstrap-based variance) only partially closed the gap (10.3% → 9.5%, still ~2x nominal), confirming the two components (leave-one-out, and `tc`'s own variance) both mattered. `dif_table`/`threshold_dif_table` output values (`Focal (purified)`, `Difference`, `SE`, `z`, `p` and downstream flags) change versus prior releases on `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`; regression fixtures regenerated. `MFRM`'s `dif_table`/`drf_table` already reflect the corrected formula, since neither had shipped before this fix.
- **`MFRM.rater_stats_df(model='mixed')` silently omitted per-rater `Estimate`/`SE` columns** (known issue #36) instead of raising or warning. Added a `mixed` branch to the group-building dispatch, mirroring `matrix`'s full item×threshold cell path, plus a new "Rater model" column reporting each rater's assigned representation (`self.rater_models`). `marginal=True` (the argument's own default, inherited from the other rater models) now raises a `UserWarning` and falls back to the full table for `model='mixed'`, since no single restricted per-rater marginal exists when raters sit under different representations. `CHANGELOG.md`'s v1.1.0 entry (below) also previously overstated `model='mixed'` support at that release — corrected there.
- **`dif_test(size_adjust=True)`'s threshold/category-width (DCF) component no longer shares `reference_n` with item-location DIF.** Direct simulation (I=15, `max_score` in {3, 5, 8}, a real 0.8-logit category-width shift, N in {500,...,6000}/group) showed the previous design — a single fixed `reference_n` pinning both components' rescaled SE to "as if N=reference_n" — made DCF power collapse to ~0% by N≈1500/group, while item-location DIF showed no comparable blind spot in the same range: threshold information doesn't scale with raw person count the way item-location information does (a category-width estimate's precision depends on how many people land in specific, thin adjacent-category cells, not on the group's total N), so no single fixed `reference_n` could serve both components at once. `dif_table` (item-location) is unchanged — it keeps `reference_n` (default `100`) exactly as before. `threshold_dif_table` (DCF, both `PCM` and `RSM`) now uses a new **`dcf_cap`** argument instead: a per-cell (per item-threshold for `PCM`; per shared threshold for `RSM`) dynamic cap on that cell's own harmonic-mean information weight — `PCM._pairwise_harmonic_weights()` (row-summed harmonic mean of the raw pairwise comparison matrix and its transpose, a Bradley–Terry Fisher-information analogue) and `RSM._threshold_total_weights()` (CPAT's own `total_weight`, the same harmonic-mean weighting `_threshold_distance` already uses; Elliott & Buttery, 2022b) respectively. Defaults (`dcf_cap=15000` for `PCM`, `dcf_cap=80000` for `RSM`, reflecting `RSM`'s larger pooled-across-items weight scale) were calibrated by simulation and found stable across every `max_score` tested (3, 5, 8). `size_adjust=False` (the default) is completely unaffected. Supersedes the `reference_n` docstring's earlier "prefer `size_adjust=False` for category-width DIF" caution, added in a previous release pending this fix.

The `i`-vs-`i` cell is now excluded from both. Changes `'log-lik'` estimates (typically <0.2 logits); `'cos'`/`'ls'`/`'evm'` unaffected. Present since v1.0.0. - **`wright_map`'s divided category strip left an unexplained gap at the open (unbounded) top/bottom categories** on `RSM`/`PCM`/`MFRM` — those categories only drew out to a width borrowed from the adjacent bounded segment (or a small fallback buffer), leaving blank panel background between the end of the strip and the axis edge. They now always draw flush to the location axis limits, in a flat block of the category's own colour by default. `neutral_extremes=True` still applies its fade-from-neutral-cream treatment, but that argument now controls only the fade — not whether the extreme reaches the edge, which it always does. Docstrings and manual updated (`neutral_extremes` row, per model class).
- **`wright_map(distribution_markers=True)` could clip the outermost μ±2σ tick and its label** on all four model classes. The location-axis range was taken from `floor`/`ceil` of the raw person/item (and, for `MFRM`, facet) data extent only; when a distribution is wide or heavy-tailed relative to its sample size, `mean ± 2·SD` can fall outside that extent, so the `μ+2σ`/`μ−2σ` mark was drawn (`clip_on=False`) just past the spine and cut off at the figure edge. The range now also spans every drawn distribution's `mean ± 2·SD` (per person-group when `group_by` is set) before the `floor`/`ceil`, so the marks always stay on-canvas. Only affects `distribution_markers=True` maps where a mark previously fell outside the data extent; the histogram/KDE binning shifts with the widened range in those cases. `Examples/Model classes/*` wright-map images regenerated.

### Changed
- **Default priority-vector method changed from `'cos'` to `'log-lik'`**, and its `matrix_power` default from `3` to `None` (resolving to `0` — no powering — for `'log-lik'`, and to `5` for every other method — raised from the previous fixed `3` after a power sweep found `SLM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` plateau by `2`–`3` while `PCM`'s wider item-threshold graph keeps improving to `5`–`6`; a no-op for `'log-lik'` and for the three plateaued models), on every `calibrate*` / `std_errors` / `model_selection` across all four model classes and on `base.priority_vector()`. `'log-lik'` is the Bradley–Terry maximum-likelihood fixed point — a lightweight iteration on the pairwise matrix, not a data-scale procedure like JML/MML — and handles an incomplete comparison graph natively, so it needs no matrix powering (whose transitive path counts in fact measurably worsen its recovery). A simulation study (SLM; full and 55%-sparse; small and large person/item samples) gave it the **lowest item-recovery RMSE of the four methods in every cell** — trivial on dense data, up to ~10% under sparsity — at ~1–2× the wall time of `'cos'` (a few ms). **This changes numeric output wherever `method=`/`matrix_power=` are not passed explicitly**, plus everything downstream (`item_stats`, SEs, DIF, `model_selection`, bootstraps) and the regenerated `Examples/Model classes/*` outputs. Pass `method='cos'` (with `matrix_power=3` if wanted) for the previous behaviour; `'cos'`/`'ls'`/`'evm'` output is bit-identical to before when selected explicitly. `'log-lik'` needs a strongly connected comparison graph (`check_data_connectivity` warns otherwise). Manual updated throughout.
- **Performance: `base.priority_vector()`.** `'log-lik'` warm-starts its MM iteration from the (inverted) cosine solution, roughly halving the iteration count — tens of passes over the item×item matrix on the default unpowered path (fewer as the item set grows and the comparison graph densifies), ~1 on a powered matrix — and gains a `max_iter` guard with a non-convergence warning. `'evm'` caps the PCA at its one used component (`n_components=1`); `'cos'` sheds redundant allocations; the PCM threshold-label list is cached across calls. `'cos'`/`'ls'` bit-identical to before, `'evm'` unchanged bar floating-point. `'cos'` is now documented (code and manual) as the Kou & Lin (2014) cosine maximisation method.
- **`RSM.calibrate()` default output changes** because the Stage 2 threshold constant now defaults to `0.03` rather than `0.1` (see Added). Stage 1 item locations are unchanged (still `0.1`); the Rasch–Andrich thresholds shift — typically <1% RMSE on well-conditioned data, more in tight rating scales — and with them `threshold_se`, `cat_width_se`, `person`/`test` statistics, threshold DIF and bootstraps. Pass `constant=0.1` (or `threshold_constant=0.1`) for the pre-1.3.0 behaviour — bit-identical to the old `RSM.calibrate()`. `SLM` output is unchanged. `RSM` regression fixtures regenerated; `Examples/Model classes/RSM*` outputs still to regenerate.
- **`PCM.calibrate()` default output changes** because `_build_pairwise_matrix()` no longer counts same-item threshold cells (see Fixed). Bare `PCM.calibrate()` item locations and thresholds shift — the recovery slope, previously biased outward to ~1.13–1.16, comes back to ~1.0 — and with them `std_errors`, `fit_statistics`, DIF, `model_selection` and bootstraps. Pass `constant` unchanged; there is no flag to restore the old matrix (the previous behaviour was a bug). `PCM` regression fixtures and `Examples/Model classes/PCM` outputs regenerated.
- **`MFRM.calibrate()` default output changes** (see Added). With arguments left at their defaults the calibration now uses `'cos'` for `items`/`thresholds`/`matrix`/`bivector` and `'log-lik'` for `global` and the stretch models, plus the tuned per-stage / per-model constants, rather than a uniform `'log-lik'` + `0.1`. Simulated parameter recovery against the previous `'cos'` + `0.1` behaviour: item locations essentially unchanged; threshold locations ~2–15% lower RMSE (largest for `matrix`/`bivector`/`items`, where the threshold-stage constant rises `0.1`→`0.3`); rater-facet vectors within a few percent on RMSE, with the `matrix` rater cells and `bivector` marginals improving most — `matrix` rater-cell recovery slope `1.02`→`0.99`, `bivector` rater item-marginal `1.04`→`0.99` (both onto `1`), `bivector` rater threshold-marginal RMSE down ~13% at slope ≈`0.98`; person locations within ~1% throughout. All `MFRM` regression fixtures and `Examples/Model classes/*MFRM*` / stretch-model outputs regenerated. Pass `constant=0.1`, `method='cos'`, `matrix_power=3` for the pre-1.3.0 result.
- **`category_base` default changed from `1` to `2` in `PCM_Sim`, `RSM_Sim` and `MFRM_Sim`** (and, through `**kwargs`, every `MFRM_Sim_*` subclass). Category widths are drawn `uniform(max_disorder, 2·category_base − max_disorder)`; at `category_base=1` (i.e. `uniform(0, 2)` with the default `max_disorder=0`) the width density is non-zero right down to `0`, so a `max_score=5` instrument drew a category narrower than `0.2` logits in roughly 40% of simulations and narrower than `0.1` in roughly 20% — a near-collapsed category whose implied threshold structure no set of ordered dichotomous item characteristic curves reproduces (a soft disordering). `category_base=2` roughly doubles the expected minimum category width and halves those near-degenerate rates without changing the shape of the draw. This changes the generated data — and therefore every recovered parameter — for any `PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` simulation that does not pass `category_base=` explicitly. `SLM_Sim` is dichotomous and unaffected. `PCM`/`RSM`/`MFRM` regression fixtures and the `Examples/Model classes/*` / `Examples/Simulations/*` outputs regenerated. Pass `category_base=1` for the pre-1.3.0 generating distribution. Manual updated (`MFRM_Sim`, `PCM_Sim`, `RSM_Sim` and stretch-model simulation sections).

## [1.2.0] - 2026-08-06

### Added
- **Three new MFRM rater-severity parameterisations**, each a closed-form restriction of an existing fully-free representation — no new estimation machinery, derived directly from an already-calibrated fit:
  - `model="centrality"` (alias `"threshold_stretch"`) — Jin & Wang (2018)-style rater centrality/extremity: a shift `beta_r` plus a stretch `alpha_r` applied to the reference thresholds, restricting the `"thresholds"` parameterisation. Sits strictly between `"global"` (1 free param/rater) and `"thresholds"` (K free params/rater) on the model-selection ladder.
  - `model="pseudo_halo"` (alias `"item_stretch"`) — the item-facet analogue: a shift `beta_r` plus a stretch `gamma_r` applied to reference item locations, restricting `"items"`. Named `pseudo_halo` deliberately — it tests compressed item-difficulty *perception* under an otherwise locally-independent response process, not classic halo effect's usual mechanism (elevated local item interdependence); pair with `item_res_corr_analysis`/`facet_res_corr_analysis` run per-rater to check for that separately.
  - `model="bistretch"` — combines both stretch axes at once: a shift `beta_r`, an item-difficulty-stretch `gamma_r`, and a threshold-stretch `alpha_r`, restricting `"bivector"`. Sits strictly between `"global"` and `"bivector"` on the model-selection ladder — the parsimonious middle ground when a rater shows genuine structure on both axes simultaneously.
  - All three integrate at full parity with the original five representations: `calibrate()`/`calibrate_anchor()`, `fit_statistics()`, `std_errors()` (bootstrap SEs), every `*_stats_df()` table, `model_selection()` (AIC/BIC/LR, with LR degrees of freedom against both the parent model and `"global"`), and all plotting methods (`icc`, `crcs`, `threshold_ccs`, `tcc`, `test_info`, `test_csem`, `std_residuals_plot`, etc.) — the MFRM rater-parameterisation family is now eight representations, not five.
  - Matching simulation classes `MFRM_Sim_Centrality`, `MFRM_Sim_PseudoHalo`, `MFRM_Sim_Bistretch` for genuine parameter recovery testing.
- **`item_stretch`/`threshold_stretch` aliases** for `pseudo_halo`/`centrality` — axis-based names matching the `"items"`/`"thresholds"` and `"bistretch"` naming convention. Work everywhere a model name is accepted: as the `model=`/`models=` value, and as the method-name suffix (e.g. `calibrate_threshold_stretch()`) for every method family with a `_centrality`/`_pseudo_halo` variant. Resulting attributes are still stored under the canonical `_centrality`/`_pseudo_halo` names regardless of which spelling was used.
- **`model_selection(models=...)`** — restrict the eight-way comparison to a chosen subset (e.g. a direct two-model test) without paying the cost of calibrating the rest.
- **`per_rater_model_selection()` extended to all eight representations, plus a new `models=` parameter.** Under `test='LR'`, the existing top-down ladder (`matrix → bivector → {items, thresholds} → global`) now runs one additional targeted stretch test depending on which of those five wins, so any rater can be assigned any of the eight representations rather than just the original five. `test='AIC'`/`'BIC'` (AIC is the default) instead pick the minimum-criterion model directly across all active representations, with no ladder involved. `models=` restricts which representations are eligible — for `test='LR'`, `{'global', 'items', 'thresholds', 'bivector'}` must stay included as the mandatory backbone the ladder's fork needs, while `'matrix'` and each stretch model are independently toggleable; for `test='AIC'`/`'BIC'` it is a direct filter over any non-empty subset.

### Fixed
- **MFRM row-order canonicalisation** (the fix v1.1.1's own changelog described) had been silently reverted by the refactor commit that produced that release, and shipped broken in v1.1.1 — any `MFRM` where facet_elements (raters) rate non-overlapping person sets could crash (`IndexError`) or silently corrupt facet/rater severity estimates via a mis-shaped positional reshape. Restored and reverified: `check_data_connectivity()` and `calibrate()` both now behave correctly on ragged rater×person designs.
- `MFRM_Sim_Centrality`/`MFRM_Sim_PseudoHalo`/`MFRM_Sim_Bistretch` raised `TypeError: got multiple values for keyword argument 'manual_items'` if a caller passed `manual_items=`/`manual_thresholds=`/`manual_persons=`/etc. directly to the constructor — fixed.

## [1.1.1] - 2026-07-23

### Added
- **Differential Item Functioning (DIF) testing**: `dif_test()` on `SLM`, `PCM`, and `RSM` — tests every item for DIF against a person-level covariate (`self.exogenous`), with anchor purification (robust-z or Wald-based outlier trimming) before testing, per-item Wald and/or likelihood-ratio tests, an omnibus joint test, BH/Bonferroni multiple-comparison correction, and optional ETS-style A/B/C severity categories (Zwick, Thayer & Mazzeo, 1997). `PCM`/`RSM` additionally report differential category functioning (DCF) via `threshold_dif_table` — tested via category *widths* rather than raw threshold locations, since a single genuine width change otherwise cascades into every downstream threshold.
- `calibrate_anchor()` added to `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM` for item-bank anchoring (equating a new form's items onto a bank's known difficulties), sharing the same robust anchor-selection machinery as DIF purification.
- `andersen_lr_test(split_by='exogenous', covariate=...)` on `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM` — Andersen's (1973) likelihood-ratio invariance test generalised to split by any exogenous person covariate, not just a median split on ability/score. (Not available on `MFRM`, where `andersen_lr_test` remains disabled for an unrelated reason — see Fixed/known issues.)
- `welch=True` option on `dif_test()` (all three models) for Welch–Satterthwaite t-tests instead of z-tests when group sizes are small or unequal; `size_adjust=True` for Tristán (2006) sample-size-adjusted standard errors.
- New worked example: `Examples/DIF testing/DIF testing example.ipynb`.

### Fixed
- Andersen/omnibus DIF tests were over-rejecting under the null (~1.3–1.7x nominal Type I error) because the alternative-hypothesis log-likelihoods used each subgroup's own separately-estimated person locations instead of the pooled model's — fixed across `SLM`/`PCM`/`RSM`.
- `PCM`'s omnibus DIF test additionally under-charged its degrees of freedom for per-item threshold/step-structure freedom, causing much larger false-positive inflation (~60% rejection under the null); fixed via a properly-scoped `omnibus_scope='item'|'full'` option.
- `MFRM.__init__` now canonicalises person row order on construction — a positional-reshape bug could otherwise silently collapse rater severity estimates toward zero when an `MFRM` was built from a non-canonically-ordered person subset.

### Changed
- `category_counts_df` (SLM, PCM, RSM, MFRM) rewritten to take `persons=`, `items=` (and, for MFRM, `facet_elements=`) filters plus a `counts_name=` option to store multiple named results side by side, instead of always computing across the full dataset. `category_counts_item` is folded into this method and removed as a separate call.
- Internal naming conventions standardised across `base.py`, `slm.py`, `pcm.py`, `rsm.py`, `mfrm.py`, `loaders.py` — `ability`/`abilities`/`difficulty`/`difficulties` renamed to `person_location(s)`/`item_location(s)` (and related parameter names) for consistency, fixing several naming-related bugs along the way.
- Package `__init__.py` consolidated under `raschpy/` (previously partly under a stray top-level `RaschPy/`), with an explicit documented `__all__` public API surface.

### Documentation
- PDF manual fully audited and updated: every model chapter (SLM/PCM/RSM/MFRM) and every simulation class checked method-by-method against the code, with documentation gaps fixed and duplicate LaTeX labels resolved.

### Removed
- Legacy `raschpy/old stable files with old names/` duplicate source files.
